# arXiv trending physics — weekly report

## Headline
This week’s strongest converging signal is the use of quantum-enabled measurement and computation to push physics into harder-to-access regimes: axion searches, nanoscale molecular NMR, fault-tolerant decoding, and quantum tests of gravity. In parallel, gravitational-wave catalogues are maturing from discovery lists into precision probes of binary formation and cosmology.

This is an inferred “hot” list, not an official arXiv ranking: arXiv publishes no trending chart, readership count, or universal popularity metric.

### 1. [Solid-state nuclear magnetic resonance search for axion-like dark matter with broadband SQUID magnetometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07908)
- **Abstract:** The CASPEr-electric experiment searches for ultralight axion-like dark matter in two 19.5–20.5 and 21.5–22 neV mass bands using 207Pb NMR in a polarized ferroelectric crystal and broadband SQUID readout, setting 95%-confidence limits |g_d| < 4×10⁻⁴ GeV⁻².
- **Authors:** Andrew J. Winter, János Ádám, Deniz Aybas, Dmitry Budker, Derek F. Jackson Kimball, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2609.07908v1` (published 2026-09-07 19:20 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`, `cond-mat.other`, `physics.ins-det`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** New experimental result; three-way cross-listing; directly reports an exclusion limit and demonstrates a calibrated low-field NMR technique. Independent web search located the arXiv abstract and PDF, but Semantic Scholar citation/influential-citation fields were unavailable because of rate limiting.
- **Claimed result:** The authors exclude the tested axion–nuclear-EDM coupling above the stated limit in the two frequency bands and demonstrate sensitivity to neV-scale axions.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; 207Pb solid-state NMR, polarized ferroelectric sample, broadband SQUID magnetometry.
- **Caveat:** The search covers narrow mass windows and reports upper bounds, not a detection; sensitivity depends on calibration and the assumed axion coupling model.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-07 batch.
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 2. [Bottomonium suppression with a machine-learning-informed Debye mass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09047)
- **Abstract:** A lattice-informed machine-learned Debye mass is inserted into a complex heavy-quark potential and real-time quantum-trajectory evolution to model Υ(1S,2S,3S) suppression in 5.02 TeV Pb–Pb collisions, with improved agreement for excited states.
- **Authors:** Ajaharul Islam, Shibo Chen, Fu-Peng Li, Long-Gang Pang, Guang-You Qin
- **arXiv:** `2609.09047v1` (published 2026-09-08 17:08 UTC; categories: `hep-ph`, `hep-ex`, `nucl-ex`, `nucl-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Four-subfield cross-listing; combines lattice-informed inputs, machine learning, quantum trajectories, and comparisons with ALICE, ATLAS, and CMS measurements; arXiv and PDF were independently surfaced in web search. Citation momentum was unavailable for this new paper.
- **Claimed result:** The ML-informed potential improves the calculated nuclear-modification factors and double ratios, especially for excited bottomonium states.
- **Evidence type:** computational; real-time Schrödinger/QTraj evolution compared with LHC heavy-ion data.
- **Caveat:** The result is model-dependent: the learned Debye mass and complex potential encode assumptions about in-medium QCD, and agreement with data is not a unique validation of the model.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-08 batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 3. [Breaking binary formation mechanism degeneracies with gravitational wave clustering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09083)
- **Abstract:** The paper shows that anisotropic clustering and galaxy–gravitational-wave cross-correlation can distinguish binary-formation scenarios through their time-delay distributions and host-environment properties.
- **Authors:** Nicola Bellomo, Michele Bosi, Sarah Libanore, Michele Liguori, Michela Mapelli, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2609.09083v1` (published 2026-09-08 17:32 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`, `astro-ph.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Addresses a central interpretation bottleneck with the roughly 400-event gravitational-wave population; web search returned the paper’s HTML and an independent science-news discussion of next-generation GW population studies. Citation counts were unavailable.
- **Claimed result:** Clustering and cross-correlation contain information that is distinctively sensitive to formation-channel environments and delay-time distributions.
- **Evidence type:** computational/forecast; statistical modeling of GW and galaxy catalogues.
- **Caveat:** The discrimination is prospective and depends on catalogue completeness, event numbers, bias modeling, and the assumed formation-channel populations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-08 batch.
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Cosmology & gravitation; Computational & data methods

### 4. [Local decoders for fault-tolerant quantum computation and translation-invariant stabilizer codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11457)
- **Abstract:** The authors construct a two-dimensional, geometrically local fault-tolerant architecture with bounded-speed classical processing and a time-translation-invariant cellular-automaton decoder, and prove local decodability for translation-invariant topological Pauli stabilizer codes under phenomenological noise.
- **Authors:** Nathaniel Selub, Aditya Bhardwaj, Ethan Lake
- **arXiv:** `2609.11457v1` (published 2026-09-10 12:29 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`, `cond-mat.stat-mech`, `nlin.CG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** 118-page foundational construction with three cross-listed categories and public visualizations; it targets the hardware/latency bottleneck in fault tolerance. Citation metrics were unavailable for the new submission.
- **Claimed result:** A local decoder supports continuous operations including state injection, lattice surgery, and readout, with logical-information preservation lasting stretched-exponentially in code distance.
- **Evidence type:** theoretical; stabilizer-code construction and proofs under phenomenological noise.
- **Caveat:** The proof is not a demonstration on a physical processor; performance under realistic circuit-level noise, fabrication constraints, and full overhead accounting remain open.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Computational & data methods

### 5. [Revealing the Quantum Signature of Gravity via Gravitational Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09931)

- **Authors:** Partha Nandi
- **arXiv:** `2609.09931v1` (published 2026-09-09 09:22 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`, `hep-th`, `quant-ph`)

- **Claimed result:** The proposed operational signatures separate classical stochastic gravitational backgrounds from quantized propagating gravitational radiation in the model.
- **Evidence type:** theoretical; linearized GR, detector Hamiltonians, and quantum-information analysis.
- **Caveat:** This is a framework/proposal, not an observed quantum-gravity signal; the paper itself points to mesoscopic oscillators as a possible future route.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-09 batch.
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Cosmology & gravitation; Instrumentation & detectors

## Trending physics themes
- **Quantum sensing crossing into real measurements:** the CASPEr axion search and individual-NV DNA NMR pair quantum spin control with concrete detector outputs, while the gravity paper frames mesoscopic quantum systems as probes of quantized radiation.
- **Gravitational-wave data becoming population science:** papers 3 and 7 use clustering, host modeling, and catalogue cross-correlation to infer formation channels and cosmology rather than merely detect events.
- **Physics-informed machine learning and simulation:** paper 2 uses ML inside a lattice-informed in-medium QCD model; paper 6 uses detailed detector simulation to quantify a future collider’s reach.
- **Quantum computing’s systems bottleneck:** paper 4 shifts attention from abstract code thresholds toward local, bounded-speed decoding and continuous operation.

## Research opportunities

### Potential research areas
- Combine quantum-sensor searches with wider, adaptive frequency scans and cross-platform confirmation, building on papers 1 and 8.
- Develop joint GW–galaxy hierarchical models that combine clustering, host weighting, and formation-channel inference, linking papers 3 and 7.
- Transfer locality-aware decoding ideas into realistic circuit-level noise and hardware scheduling, extending paper 4.

### Unsolved problems
- Axion searches still cover narrow mass bands and have not established a signal (paper 1).
- Few-molecule NMR remains sensitive to NV–target geometry and surface disorder (paper 8).
- GW host incompleteness and weighting-model uncertainty broaden cosmological inference (paper 7), while formation-channel degeneracies remain model-dependent (paper 3).
- The quantum-gravity signatures in paper 5 remain proposed observables, not experimentally demonstrated effects.

### Potential research directions
- Run blind, multi-band CASPEr-style searches with independent magnetometers and shared calibration standards (paper 1).
- Use engineered DNA test structures and controlled NV depths to separate structural signal from surface heterogeneity (paper 8).
- Forecast joint gains from GW clustering plus host-weighted dark-siren inference on next-generation catalogues (papers 3 and 7).
- Benchmark local decoders under circuit-level noise and realistic communication latency, then compare resource overheads with nonlocal decoders (paper 4).

## Takeaway
The week’s notable pattern is not one confirmed discovery but a shift toward measurement architectures and inference pipelines that make difficult signals usable: quantum sensors, local quantum control, and richer GW catalog statistics. Most entries are new preprints, so the ordering reflects cross-listing, experimental or methodological concreteness, and independent attention signals—not established scientific consensus.
