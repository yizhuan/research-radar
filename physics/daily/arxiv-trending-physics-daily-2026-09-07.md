## Headline
The strongest signal in the latest batch is a push toward precision and deployability: CMS reports a first meaningful constraint on the rare H→Zγ decay, XRISM supplies a kinematic viscosity bound for cluster plasma, and several papers turn next-generation cosmology, quantum-network, and gravitational-wave analyses into practical pipelines. arXiv provides no official trending chart; this is an inferred ranking based on scientific reach, experimental or observational data, cross-subfield relevance, and limited corroboration from metadata and external artifacts—not readership or downloads.

### 1. [Search for the rare Higgs boson decay H → Zγ in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 and 13.6 TeV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04402)
- **Abstract:** CMS searches for the loop-induced H→Zγ decay using 200 fb⁻¹ of 13 and 13.6 TeV pp data and measures a signal strength μ = 1.10^{+0.52}_{−0.61}, with 1.9σ observed significance.
- **Authors:** CMS Collaboration
- **arXiv:** `2609.04402v1` (published 2026-09-03; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Major LHC collaboration result; 200 fb⁻¹ dataset; submitted to Physics Letters B; CERN/CMS public result HIG-25-010; Semantic Scholar lookup was rate-limited for this very recent paper, so citation momentum is unavailable.
- **Claimed result:** The measured rate is compatible with the Standard Model prediction, but the 1.9σ significance is not evidence for a discovery (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** experimental; CMS LHC collision data and a simultaneous fit to dilepton–photon invariant-mass spectra.
- **Caveat:** The significance is below discovery standards and the uncertainty remains large; this is a search/measurement, not an observation of a new decay signal.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, latest batch shown for 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [A Kinematic Measurement of the Effective Viscosity of the Intracluster Medium: The Cold Front of A3667 Seen by XRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04316)
- **Abstract:** XRISM velocity data across the A3667 cold front imply dynamic-viscosity upper limits of 5.2×10³ and 3.3×10³ g cm⁻¹ s⁻¹ on the cool and hot sides, respectively.
- **Authors:** Yutaka Fujita, Yuki Omiya
- **arXiv:** `2609.04316v1` (published 2026-09-03; categories: `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Direct use of new XRISM/Resolve kinematic data; an independent complement to imaging constraints; unusually concrete connection between an X-ray velocity map and plasma microphysics. Citation counts are unavailable for this new paper.
- **Claimed result:** The inferred limits are consistent with prior imaging-based evidence for viscosity below the isotropic Spitzer value, while adding a kinematic constraint (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** observational; XRISM/Resolve velocity map of the A3667 cluster cold front.
- **Caveat:** The interface width is limited by XRISM angular resolution, and viscosity scales as the square of that width, so the reported values are upper limits.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, latest batch shown for 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Plasma & fluid physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Proof-of-principle long-distance Sagnac twin-field quantum key distribution network](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04447)
- **Abstract:** An experiment demonstrates a three-user-pair Sagnac twin-field QKD network over 127 km, with 93±1% interference visibility and a secure key rate of 1.398×10⁻⁵ bits per pulse at 45 dB loss.
- **Authors:** Reem Mandil, Yen-An Shih, Abhay Verma, Li Qian, Hoi-Kwong Lo
- **arXiv:** `2609.04447v1` (published 2026-09-03; categories: `quant-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Experimental network—not merely a two-user link—over 127 km; avoids active phase stabilization and postcompensation; reports stability over one hour. Citation counts are unavailable for this new paper.
- **Claimed result:** The authors claim the first long-fiber TFQKD network demonstration without active phase stabilization or postcompensation (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** experimental; three-user-pair Sagnac network using single-photon avalanche detectors and optical fiber.
- **Caveat:** It is explicitly a proof of principle, with an asymmetric channel and a low key rate; practical scaling, security assumptions, and broader network operation remain to be established.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, latest batch shown for 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors

### 4. [ALPACA I: Controlling source and PSF systematics in JWST time-delay cosmography with differentiable lens modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04312)
- **Abstract:** ALPACA is a GPU-accelerated differentiable strong-lens pipeline that jointly models source, lens mass, and PSF; on realistic JWST mocks it reduces a possible 14% time-delay-distance bias to the percent level.
- **Authors:** Hrvoje Krizic, Martin Millon, Aymeric Galan, Sydney Erickson, Phil Marshall
- **arXiv:** `2609.04312v1` (published 2026-09-03; categories: `astro-ph.IM`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Targets a central JWST systematic in H₀ inference; reduces per-system runtime from weeks to under 15 hours; connects differentiable computation, strong lensing, and hierarchical cosmology. The package is planned for public release on journal publication; citation counts are unavailable.
- **Claimed result:** Ten-lens mock hierarchical inference recovers the input cosmology without significant bias when PSF uncertainty is jointly modeled (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** computational; realistic JWST/NIRCam mock observations and hierarchical inference.
- **Caveat:** Validation is on mocks, and the software release is future-tense; real JWST complexity and model misspecification may not match the test set.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, latest batch shown for 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Computational & data methods; Instrumentation & detectors

## Trending physics themes

- Precision Standard Model tests remain prominent: the CMS H→Zγ search and the DIS one-jettiness calculation both reduce uncertainty in rare processes and event-shape interpretation, though they address different experimental regimes ([2609.04402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04402), [2609.04306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04306)).
- Instrument-aware inference is a repeated motif: XRISM’s finite angular resolution directly controls the viscosity bound, while ALPACA treats JWST PSF uncertainty as part of the cosmological inference ([2609.04316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04316), [2609.04312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04312)).
- Practical computational infrastructure is moving into the result itself: ALPACA, GWMemoryModel, and the DIS resummation framework emphasize reusable pipelines, differentiability, or efficient predictions ([2609.04312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04312), [2609.04340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04340), [2609.04306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04306)).
- Quantum information is represented by an unusually tangible network milestone in this batch: the 127-km Sagnac TFQKD demonstration is an experiment, not only a protocol proposal ([2609.04447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04447)).

## Research opportunities

### Potential research areas
- Joint calibration of instrument systematics and physical inference: extend the XRISM cold-front method to clusters with better angular resolution and combine it with imaging constraints; extend ALPACA-style PSF marginalization to real multi-epoch JWST lens samples ([2609.04316], [2609.04312]).
- Survey-scale microlensing inference for extended dark structures, using Rubin supernova cadence and population modeling rather than single-event searches ([2609.04308]).

### Unsolved problems
- Whether the H→Zγ rate will become a precision Standard Model test depends on reducing statistical and systematic uncertainty; the current 1.9σ result is not a discovery ([2609.04402]).
- The XRISM viscosity estimate remains resolution-limited and is therefore an upper bound, not a sharply measured transport coefficient ([2609.04316]).
- The QKD demonstration leaves practical scaling, security under real network conditions, and operation beyond the proof-of-principle topology open ([2609.04447]).

### Potential research directions
- Combine CMS H→Zγ categories and future luminosity with global Higgs fits to test loop-level new physics, without treating the present fluctuation as evidence for it ([2609.04402]).
- Benchmark ALPACA and GWMemoryModel on real observations and on spinning or more unequal-mass binaries, respectively; both papers identify computational efficiency as an enabler rather than a completed validation ([2609.04312], [2609.04340]).
- Use Rubin supernova populations and complementary lensing observables to turn the proposed minihalo sensitivity into a forecast with survey-selection and astrophysical-contamination uncertainties ([2609.04308]).

## Takeaway
The latest batch is hottest where new instruments meet inference: CMS and XRISM provide data-driven tests, while JWST lens modeling, QKD networking, and GW-memory waveforms provide tools intended to make future measurements reliable and scalable. These are mostly early preprints; the ranking is an evidence-based editorial inference, and several of the most interesting entries are forecasts or proof-of-principle demonstrations rather than settled discoveries.
