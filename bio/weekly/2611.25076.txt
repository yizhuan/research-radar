Abstract
Three-dimensional (3D) molecular similarity scoring and alignment are widely em-
ployed in ligand-based virtual screening and molecular design, and are increasingly
used for training and evaluating machine learning models. Existing open-source meth-
ods utilize various molecular representations spanning shape, electrostatics, pharma-
cophores, and hybrid representations, but their implementations as independent soft-
ware packages with distinct application programming interfaces (APIs), optimizers,
and limited GPU support make them difficult to compare, combine, or extend. Here,
we develop ShEPhERD-score into a unified, modular platform for differentiable 3D
molecular similarity scoring and alignment by combining diverse molecular represen-
tations with high-performance Numba CPU and Triton GPU kernels to support both
large-scale library screening and pairwise similarity workflows. In matched compar-
isons with open-source 3D similarity tools on the DUDE-Z virtual screening bench-
mark, ShEPhERD-score performed comparably or better in eight of nine cases. Across
the scoring modes in ShEPhERD-score, throughput ranges from 17,800 to 1.03 mil-
lion alignments/second for ten-million-conformer libraries using a single NVIDIA L40S
GPU. Moreover, we found that the most effective scoring mode varied by protein target,
indicating that target-specific calibration against validation data may be valuable be-
fore large-scale screening and underscoring the need for a unified platform that provides
diverse molecular representations. Together, these capabilities establish ShEPhERD-
score as a single, extensible toolkit for rapid virtual screening and machine learning
workflows.
Introduction
The three-dimensional (3D) structures of molecules define the spatial arrangement of steric
and electronic features, which governs the intermolecular interactions and chemical reactivity
that underlie their function. For example, proteins recognize ligands through their shape and
2
the placement of polar and hydrophobic groups via non-covalent interactions. 1 Comparing
these features through 3D molecular alignment and similarity scoring is therefore a fun-
damental operation in cheminformatics. This process superimposes two 3D conformations,
quantifying similarity by calculating differences in molecular shape, electrostatic potential
(ESP), pharmacophores, or combinations thereof. 2–7 The alignment itself is an optimization
problem in which the relative orientation of the two conformations is varied to maximize
their similarity. These similarity measures are routinely used to prioritize compounds dur-
ing virtual screening, 8–13 identify bioisosteric replacements in medicinal chemistry, 14–16 and
analyze structure-activity relationships to provide 3D insight to chemists. 17–19 Increasingly,
these alignment scores have been used as objectives for machine-learned screening meth-
ods 20,21 or evaluation metrics for molecular generative models. 22–24
Existing 3D molecular alignment software supports molecular representations based
on molecular shape, 25–30 ESP,31,32 pharmacophores, 33–37 or hybrid scoring functions. 38–44
Among these, OpenEye ROCS and FastROCS are commercial tools widely used for rapid
3D molecular similarity screening with volumetric representations of shape and directionless
pharmacophores. 27 Open-source tools, including ROSHAMBO2, 30,45 ShaEP,39 PheSA, 35
Pharao/Align-it, 34 RDKit (rdShapeAlign), 46 and ESP-Sim, 31 each support one or two of
these representations, and only ROSHAMBO2 runs on GPUs. However, these tools have
largely been developed independently; each similarity metric is implemented with its own
alignment algorithm in its own software package, often with distinct APIs, optimization
strategies, and hardware support. As a result, evaluating multiple definitions of molecular
similarity—or developing new ones—remains difficult, particularly when methods cannot
be compared under consistent alignment and computational settings. This limitation
matters because no single 3D similarity tool performs best for every target class or evalu-
ation metric, 6 motivating the need for flexible frameworks that support diverse molecular
representations.
Rather than treating each similarity measure as a distinct algorithm, we formulate the
3
alignment of molecular conformations as the optimization of arbitrary differentiable simi-
larity objectives over rigid body translations and rotations. This abstraction separates the
optimization procedure from the scoring function itself, allowing new objectives to be imple-
mented by defining only a differentiable scoring function while reusing the same optimization
framework. The resulting implementation naturally supports automatic differentiation, ef-
ficient batched execution on CPUs and GPUs, and straightforward composition of multiple
scoring terms. This design, combined with code-generation agent skills, allows rapid pro-
totyping, validation, and optimization of new similarity objectives with minimal manual
implementation effort. 47–49
Herein we describe ShEPhERD-score as a robust, open-source implementation for differ-
entiable 3D molecular similarity scoring and alignment. This work builds on ShEPhERD
and ShEPhERD–2, our diffusion models for interaction-conditioned 3D molecule genera-
tion. 22,23 While six scoring functions were released alongside these generative models, they
were not optimized for speed or modularity. In this work, we expand those capabilities for
high-throughput virtual screening by introducing twelve new scoring modes and accelerat-
ing their computation. The eighteen modes evaluated here include shape, ESP, pharma-
cophore, and strategic combinations thereof via composite scoring functions, all through a
common Python interface. We demonstrate that this extensible framework preserves com-
petitive virtual screening performance while achieving substantial throughput improvements
through optimized Triton GPU and Numba CPU kernels, reaching 17,800 to 1.03 million
alignments/second for ten-million-conformer libraries on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU. The
same representations can also compare 3D properties beyond those relevant to protein–ligand
recognition, such as condensed Fukui reactivity indices and Crippen molar-refractivity con-
tributions.

Alignment Modes and Workflow
The central abstraction in ShEPhERD-score is the separation of molecular representation,
similarity scoring, and rigid-body optimization. Molecular alignment is formulated as the
maximization of a differentiable similarity objective over SE(3), 22,26 allowing different repre-
sentations and scoring functions to use a common optimization framework. The modes de-
scribed here primarily represent molecules as 3D point clouds associated with isotropic Gaus-
sian functions 25,26 and, where applicable, chemical features or directional information. 22,35
Figure 1A summarizes the representations used by a representative set of eight of the eigh-
teen modes evaluated in this work. Similarity scores are constructed from Gaussian overlaps
with mode-specific weighting and normalization. 35,39 Each mode is therefore defined by its
point representation and its weighting function.

This framework enables new similarity objectives to be implemented with minimal addi-
tional infrastructure.vol colorextends volumetric shape similarity by including direction-
less pharmacophoric features in a ROCS-color-like formulation, 50 whilevol lipoweights
atom-centered Gaussian overlaps by Crippen lipophilicity parameters 51 to favor alignments
between corresponding hydrophobic and polar regions. By introducing these formulations,
ShEPhERD-score extends the combined representation coverage of existing open-source
tools, doubling the number of supported representations (Figure 1C).
These scoring functions share a common SE(3) optimization, batching, and hardware
acceleration infrastructure. Molecule orientations are parameterized by a unit quaternion
and translation vector, and multiple initial orientations are refined in parallel using the Adam
optimizer 52 to identify the highest-scoring alignment. The workflow begins by converting
molecules into reusable molecular profiles that cache the features required for alignment
(Figure 1D). These profiles can then be aligned either in-memory usingMoleculePairBatch
for dense pairwise comparisons or streamed directly from disk usingProfileStorefor large-
scale virtual screening.
To enable users to quickly prototype and optimize new scoring modes, we include struc-
tured instructions that allow coding agents to implement them from a natural language
prompt. New objectives are first expressed as differentiable PyTorch reference implemen-
tations. Optimized Numba and Triton kernels are then generated and automatically vali-
dated against the reference implementation. Twelve new scoring modes includingvol color,
vol fukui, andpharm tverskywere developed using this workflow (Supporting Informa-
tion, Section S1).

Benchmarking Similarity Objectives
We assessed the accuracy of ShEPhERD-score scoring functions on the DUDE-Z benchmark
of 43 protein targets with associated active ligands and property-matched decoys. 53 This
benchmark measures how well a scoring function discriminates known actives from decoys
given a single known active, and its common use for evaluating 3D ligand similarity scoring
functions provides a point of comparison.30,45,54 Although property-matched decoys can favor
methods that represent molecular connectivity, 55,56 this well-established benchmark enables
a fair comparison among methods evaluated on the same query ligand and library conformers.
We compared our scoring functions directly against open-source alignment packages which
use similar molecular representations and scoring functions to test whether the accelerated
implementation retains their retrieval accuracy.
Following an established DUDE-Z evaluation protocol, we used, for each of the 43 protein
targets, the pose of a ligand co-crystallized with that target as the query (Figure 2A). 30,45,54
The query was aligned against up to 50 generated conformers per library ligand to account
for molecular flexibility, with the maximum similarity score retained for ranking. Retrieval
performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve
(ROC-AUC), which measures the probability that a randomly selected active is ranked
above a randomly selected decoy. 57 All scoring functions used the same conformers to iso-
late differences in molecular representation, scoring, and search effort. Alignment settings
for enrichment and throughput measurements are reported in the Supporting Information
(Sections S2.1 and S3.2).

Virtual screening aims to prioritize actives at the top of a ranked library for further
downselection and, ultimately, synthesis and experimental testing. ROC-AUC assesses over-
all ranking accuracy, whereas early enrichment measures how many actives are recovered
among the top-ranked compounds. We therefore also report the 1% enrichment factor
(EF1%) and hit rate, which normalizes EF1% by its maximum attainable value for that
library (Supporting Information, Section S2). 6,57 EF1% and hit rates for every method are in
Table S1, with per-target EF1% in Tables S2 and S3. On EF1%, ShEPhERD-score achieved
comparable or higher enrichment than existing methods in all nine matched comparisons
(Figure S1).
To investigate how retrieval depends on the molecular representation, we compared the
ShEPhERD-score modes on each target and found that the best-performing mode varied
(Figure 2C). Among the ShEPhERD-score modes shown in Figure 2B, weighted volumetric
representations gave the strongest average benchmark performance.vol colorachieved the
highest mean ROC-AUC of 0.721, compared with 0.657 for volumetric shape alone, indicating
that adding molecular attributes can improve retrieval. However, the benefit depended on
both the attributes used and the target. Each of the eight modes ranked first for at least one
target and second for at least one target, demonstrating that no single scoring function was
optimal across all targets. Where target-specific validation data are available, the scoring
mode and any term weights can therefore be selected as hyperparameters and confirmed on
an independent test set before prospective use. Because every mode is called through the
same interface, this selection requires only changing the mode argument.

Throughput and Scaling
Enumerated make-on-demand catalogs of molecules now hold tens of billions of readily syn-
thesizable compounds, 58–60 and the combinatorial spaces that generate them imply hundreds
of trillions or more,61–63 making throughput a practical consideration for 3D similarity screen-
ing. To support large-scale use, we implemented score and gradient evaluation in Numba
CPU 64 and Triton GPU kernels, while grouping conformer pairs by size to improve GPU
utilization. 65
Screening large libraries requires the initial featurization of all candidate ligands and their
conformers. However, because different open-source packages perform this step at different
stages of the alignment workflow, we compare the time required to compute and store molec-
ular objects. Figure 3A reports preparation throughput in conformers per minute on one
CPU core, measured over 100 compounds with 50 conformers each. All packages compared
here share a baseline preparation time of 61.7 ms per conformer for ETKDGv3 embedding
and MMFF94 relaxation. ShEPhERD-score volumetric shape and pharmacophore feature
extraction add minimal overhead to open-source package preparation times at 62.1 and
63.5 ms per conformer, respectively. However, modes requiring GFN2-xTB charges, sur-
faces, or both take an average of 142.4, 186.6, and 267.1 ms per conformer, respectively.
Preparation is therefore a one-time cost comparable to that of existing tools, except for the
charge- and surface-based modes.

ShEPhERD-score provides two alignment workflows. Thescreenworkflow aligns a single
query conformer against a large precomputed library to find compounds similar to a known
13
active. Molecular profiles are stored on disk and streamed into memory during screen-
ing, so a library is featurized once and searched repeatedly. Thepairwiseworkflow holds
two sets of conformers in memory and aligns specified pairs or batches, processing com-
parisons larger than host memory as separate blocks. Thepairwiseworkflow is useful for
smaller library comparisons, cheminformatics analysis, 28 and machine-learning applications
that require large numbers of molecular similarity evaluations.20,21,31 Both workflows contain
accelerated implementations on GPU, to maximize throughput, and on CPU, to maximize
accessibility.
We first measured how screening throughput scales with library size on one GPU and
one CPU core (Figure 3B). GPU screening throughput initially increases with library size
as larger batches improve GPU utilization, then approximately plateaus aboveN= 10 5 and
remains high throughN= 10 7 conformers for all eight modes, whereas single-core CPU
throughput changes less than twofold across library sizes. AtN= 10 7, GPU screening was
296 to 1,972 times faster than single-core CPU screening atN= 10 3, depending on the mode.
Screening throughput can be increased further by distributing the library across additional
CPU workers or GPUs. Forvol, throughput increased 54.7-fold with 64 CPU workers and
3.9-fold with four GPUs relative to the corresponding single-worker runs (Figure S2).
Compared to open-source tools, ShEPhERD-score is typically faster on a single AMD
EPYC 9474F CPU core (Figure 3C, left). Volumetric shape alignment reaches 2,470 align-
ments/s, compared with 726 for RDKit, 46 186 for PheSA, and 22.5 for ShaEP; shape-plus-
color alignment reaches 813 alignments/s, compared with 667 for RDKit; and pharmacophore
alignment reaches 574 alignments/s, compared with 180 for PheSA. ShaEP and ESP-Sim
are faster for combined volume-and-surface electrostatic similarity (22.5 and 187 versus 19
alignments/s), although ESP-Sim scores electrostatic similarity after Crippen O3A align-
ment and therefore uses a different alignment objective. Pharao/Align-it is faster for phar-
macophore alignment (1,533 alignments/s), superposing matched features by their centroids
and optimizing rotation only, but has significantly lower DUDE-Z ROC-AUC thanpharm

(p= 0.018). Single-core CPU screening with ShEPhERD-score is therefore competitive with
existing CPU tools.
Existing open-source GPU implementations support only volumetric shape and shape-
plus-color similarity, so direct comparisons used these two modes (Figure 3C, middle). On
a single NVIDIA L40S GPU screeningN= 10 7 conformers, ShEPhERD-score reaches
1,034,608 alignments/s for volumetric shape, comparable to the 948,736 alignments/s of
ROSHAMBO2, and 348,793 alignments/s for shape-plus-color similarity, twice the 170,289
alignments/s of ROSHAMBO2. For the other six modes in Figure 3 and the ten additional
modes (Table S9), ShEPhERD-score provides GPU screening where no open-source GPU
implementation is available.
The advantage of ShEPhERD-score over existing open-source software is especially ev-
ident for exhaustive pairwise GPU alignment. In this setting, ShEPhERD-score processes
pairs from multiple reference queries simultaneously, while ROSHAMBO2 processes each
query separately. On the same 100,000 ordered conformer pairs, ShEPhERD-score was
16.0×faster forvol(118,293 versus 7,415 alignments/s) and 45.9×faster forvol color
(60,728 versus 1,323 alignments/s) than the corresponding ROSHAMBO2 scoring functions
(Figure 3C, right). At these rates, one GPU computes 3D similarities for one million con-
former pairs in 8.5 to 75 s across the eight modes, which makes pairwise scoring practical for
building machine-learning training sets and evaluating generative models.
Conclusions
This work represents a substantially expanded and improved software package for 3D molec-
ular alignment that is well equipped for modern high-throughput workflows. By formulating
3D molecular alignment as the optimization of modular, differentiable objectives over SE(3),
ShEPhERD-score decouples the definition of 3D chemical similarity from the underlying
optimization, batching, and hardware-acceleration infrastructure. This formulation enabled
15
speedups up to 46-fold relative to best-in-class open-source tools while introducing twelve
new scoring modes and several new representations. Benchmarking on DUDE-Z, we achieve
comparable or better performance than the corresponding open-source tools in eight of nine
comparisons. Because we revealed that no single representation is universally optimal across
protein targets, the ability to seamlessly switch, combine, or prototype scoring functions
within one high-throughput framework offers a practical advantage for ligand-based virtual
screening and is synergistic with 3D generative modeling workflows. Future efforts will ex-
pand the integration of this work with generative models and synthetic planning to enable
molecular optimization. Ultimately, ShEPhERD-score provides an extensible, open-source
toolkit that bridges foundational 3D cheminformatics and modern machine-learning pipelines
for molecular design.
