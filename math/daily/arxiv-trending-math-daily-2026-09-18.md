Semantic Scholar citation data was unavailable because its API returned HTTP 429.
- **Claimed result:** The abstract claims one-dimensionality of bounded stable entire solutions in three dimensions and deduces the full monotone-solution De Giorgi conjecture in four dimensions.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Solutions are bounded, entire, and stable; the result concerns the Allen–Cahn equation and uses the geometry of the zero set, including a weak stability inequality and Gauss–Bonnet arguments.

### 3. [Lyapunov stability of polynomial vector fields is undecidable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22058)
- **Abstract:** The paper proves that, for some fixed dimension and odd degree, no algorithm can decide Lyapunov stability of the origin from rational coefficients of a homogeneous polynomial vector field, settling an algorithmic conjecture attributed to V. I. Arnold.
- **Authors:** Milan Korda
- **arXiv:** `2609.22058v1` (published 2026-09-18 17:50 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.CA`, `math.OC`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The result is a clean undecidability theorem with a named historical conjecture, and independent search results included the arXiv HTML paper and an external explanatory article. Semantic Scholar citation data was unavailable because its API returned HTTP 429.
- **Claimed result:** There exist integers \(N\) and odd \(D\) such that stability is undecidable for homogeneous degree-\(D\) polynomial vector fields in dimension \(N\), as stated in the abstract.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The input consists of rational coefficients; the vector field is homogeneous and polynomial; the fixed dimension and degree are described as large and unoptimized.
- **Caveat:** The theorem does not give practical complexity bounds or optimized \(N,D\); undecidability for one fixed class does not make every stability instance undecidable.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-18 batch
- **Themes:** Optimization & control; Logic & foundations

### 4. [Energy minimization for eight points on the sphere](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22077)
- **Abstract:** For eight points on the unit sphere, the authors identify the unique logarithmic- and Coulomb-energy minimizer up to congruence as a square antiprism and extend the analysis to Riesz and other completely monotone energies.
- **Authors:** Liudmyla Kryvonos; Lukas Liehr; Mitchell A. Taylor
- **arXiv:** `2609.22077v1` (published 2026-09-18 17:58 UTC; categories: `math.MG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It gives an exact finite-configuration classification, uses computer-assisted proof fully verified in Lean for key cases, and answers a universality question negatively for some potentials; independent search results found the HTML paper and external Pith summary. Semantic Scholar citation data was unavailable because its API returned HTTP 429.
- **Claimed result:** The square antiprism, with height determined by a unique stationarity equation, is the unique global minimizer for logarithmic and Coulomb energies; the paper also proves a sufficiently-large-\(s\) Riesz result and a computer-assisted all-\(s\ge0\) result.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Exactly eight points lie on the unit sphere; the energy potential matters, and the square-antiprism conclusion is not universal across all completely monotone potentials.
- **Caveat:** Some conclusions are computer-assisted, and the negative universality examples show that the geometric optimizer depends sensitively on the chosen energy.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-18 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Numerical analysis & computation

## Trending Research Themes

- **Restricted-to-sharp incidence geometry:** Wang–Zakharov combine sticky structure with convex Wolff axioms to reach sharp Furstenberg and Kakeya conclusions in dimensions three and four. This is a specific structural regime, not evidence that the unrestricted conjectures are solved.
- **Elliptic PDE meeting geometry:** Liu et al. reduce stability for Allen–Cahn to geometric information on the zero set, while the Kakeya work also studies geometric structure through incidence estimates. These are separate results sharing geometric-measure techniques rather than a single unified theorem.
- **Qualitative questions becoming algorithmic boundaries:** Korda's undecidability theorem marks a limit on complete automated stability classification, complementing the more constructive quantitative results elsewhere in the batch.
- **Rigorous computation as part of proof:** Kryvonos–Liehr–Taylor use Lean-verified and computer-assisted arguments for a spherical energy problem, illustrating a formal/computational route to finite configuration classification.
- **Global versus local randomness:** Porath's construction shows that strong global discrepancy control can coexist with Poissonian local pair statistics, challenging an apparent tension in metric number theory.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Beyond sticky Kakeya regimes (author/result boundary):** Determine how much of the Wang–Zakharov argument survives without stickiness or under weaker incidence hypotheses; the present theorem does not settle the unrestricted four-dimensional conjecture.
- **Allen–Cahn beyond stability (scope boundary):** Investigate whether related one-dimensionality conclusions can be obtained for broader classes of bounded entire solutions, or identify the precise role of stability in the dimension-four De Giorgi problem (Liu et al.).
- **Optimize the undecidability parameters:** Korda explicitly describes the dimension and degree as large and unoptimized. Reducing them, or locating the threshold between decidable and undecidable fixed classes, is a concrete follow-up.
- **Classify energy-dependent eight-point optimizers:** The square antiprism is not universal for all completely monotone potentials. Characterizing the potential classes that select it—or other configurations—would extend Kryvonos–Liehr–Taylor's result.
- **Make the compatible pseudorandom construction explicit and robust:** Porath's existence result invites questions about simpler formulas, discrepancy constants, and extensions to higher-dimensional or digital-sequence settings.
