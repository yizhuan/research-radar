## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [The critical Ising magnetization field can be reconstructed from its +/- interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02064)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove that the planar critical Ising magnetization scaling-limit field is measurable from the nested CLE_3 spin-interface ensemble, complementing an earlier construction from colored CLE_{16/3}.
- **Authors:** Paul Cahen, Christophe Garban, Avelio Sepúlveda
- **arXiv:** `2610.02064v1` (published timestamp unavailable; submitted 1 Oct 2026; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A precise new measurability result links two central continuum objects; the paper also spans probability and mathematical physics. It is placed first for the result’s structural reach, not measured readership.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove reconstruction of the critical Ising magnetization field from nested CLE_3, and show that the route through CLE percolation to colored CLE_{16/3} recovers the same field (abstract and introduction).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Critical planar Ising scaling limits on a sufficiently regular domain; the introduction describes + boundary conditions and the nested interface limit.
- **Caveat:** The converse—that nested CLE_3 can be reconstructed from the magnetization field—is explicitly conjectured, not proved.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Friday 2 Oct 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics; Geometry & topology

### 2. [On Colorful Kruskal--Katona Theorems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02165)
- **Abstract:** Using entropy, the authors bound the number of rainbow triangles in an r-edge-coloured graph with m edges by `C_r m^(3/2)`, where `C_r = sqrt(2(r-2)/(9r))`, establish sharpness when an affine plane of order r−1 exists, and give a stability statement; they also study properly coloured larger cliques.
- **Authors:** Ting-Wei Chao, Maya Sankar, Hung-Hsun Hans Yu
- **arXiv:** `2610.02165v1` (published timestamp unavailable; submitted 1 Oct 2026; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It states an explicit extremal bound, sharpness conditions, and near-extremal structure, a particularly concrete package of results in the batch.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove the stated rainbow-triangle upper bound and show near-extremizers must reflect affine-plane structure; for odd r, blowups of a properly edge-coloured K_{r+1} maximize the larger-clique count (abstract and introduction).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Simple graph with m edges and r≥3 edge colours; the triangle bound is sharp under the stated affine-plane condition.
- **Caveat:** Sharpness depends on existence of the relevant plane; the abstract does not give a universal exact improvement when it does not exist.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Friday 2 Oct 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 3. [Geometry and Convergence of Quadratically Regularized Optimal Transport II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02077)
- **Abstract:** For small quadratic regularization, the authors describe the support geometry and potential expansions of optimal transport couplings, and prove sharp L^p approximation rates for two estimators of the Brenier map.
- **Authors:** Alberto González-Sanz, Marcel Nutz
- **arXiv:** `2610.02077v1` (published timestamp unavailable; submitted 1 Oct 2026; categories: `math.AP`, `math.OC`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper connects analysis, optimization, and probability and gives explicit asymptotic profiles and sharp error constants rather than only qualitative convergence.
- **Claimed result:** Under the paper’s assumptions, the support contains the Brenier-map graph for sufficiently small regularization; rescaled sections have ellipsoidal interior and half-space boundary limits, with sharp L^p rates and leading biases for two map approximations (abstract and introduction).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Quadratic cost and quadratic regularization; marginals have positive C^{1,α} densities on bounded uniformly convex supports with C^{3,α} boundaries, bounded above and away from zero.
- **Caveat:** The theorems rely on substantial smoothness and uniform convexity; transfer to rough, degenerate, or nonconvex settings is not established by this paper’s stated assumptions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Friday 2 Oct 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Optimization & control; Analysis & PDE; Probability & statistics

### 4. [Linear arboricity conjecture for infinite graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02065)
- **Abstract:** The authors extend the linear-arboricity conjecture to infinite graphs of finite maximum degree, prove equivalence of its finite and infinite versions, and bound a related topological linear arboricity for high-girth regular graphs.
- **Authors:** Leandro Aurichi, Rodrigo Santos Monteiro, Caio Fernando Rodrigues
- **arXiv:** `2610.02065v1` (published timestamp unavailable; submitted 1 Oct 2026; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper connects a classic finite extremal conjecture to infinite graph decompositions and supplies a distinct high-girth bound, giving it a clear structural contribution.
- **Claimed result:** The authors show finite and infinite versions of the conjecture are equivalent; their new topological variant differs from linear arboricity by at most one, and every 2k-regular graph of girth at least 2k has topological linear arboricity at most k+1 (abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Infinite graphs are required to have finite maximum degree; the final bound assumes 2k-regularity and girth at least 2k.
- **Caveat:** The abstract does not claim an unconditional resolution of the underlying finite Linear Arboricity Conjecture; it establishes equivalence and related bounds.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Friday 2 Oct 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 5. [An optimal constant for vector balancing with permutations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02127)
- **Abstract:** The authors study vector balancing when each vector can be signed and its coordinates permuted, proving explicit bounds for the balancing and prefix problems and asymptotic optimality in dimension by a geometric method.
- **Authors:** Jonathan Niles-Weed, Shay Sadovsky, Jacob Shkrob
- **arXiv:** `2610.02127v1` (published timestamp unavailable; submitted 1 Oct 2026; categories: `math.CO`, `cs.DM`, `math.MG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It pairs an asymptotically optimal bound with a geometric proof and is cross-listed in combinatorics, discrete mathematics, and metric geometry.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove explicit bounds for both the signed-permutation vector-balancing problem and its prefix variant, and show the bound is asymptotically optimal in dimension (abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Vectors may each receive a sign and a coordinate permutation; the abstract does not specify the detailed norm or exact bound.
- **Caveat:** The abstract omits the formula and detailed hypotheses, so the constant and the precise range of the optimality statement require reading the full paper.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Friday 2 Oct 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Geometry & topology

### 6. [On the prime geodesic theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01998)

## Trending Research Themes

- **Sharp bounds with structural equality cases:** Chao, Sankar, and Yu combine an explicit rainbow-triangle bound with affine-plane extremizers and stability; Aurichi, Monteiro, and Rodrigues likewise relate graph-decomposition bounds to regularity and girth. These are parallel uses of extremal structure, not evidence of one shared technique.
- **Limit objects and quantitative approximation:** Cahen, Garban, and Sepúlveda recover a continuum random field from interface geometry, while González-Sanz and Nutz quantify the small-regularization geometry and bias of transport couplings. Both extract richer limiting information than convergence alone, in distinct settings.
- **Geometric methods in discrete problems:** Niles-Weed, Sadovsky, and Shkrob use a geometric argument for an asymptotically optimal balancing bound; Chao et al. use entropy in colored extremal combinatorics. The methodological link is broad—geometric/information-theoretic tools for discrete bounds—not a single common method.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem (authors’ conjecture):** Can the nested CLE_3 interface ensemble itself be reconstructed from the critical Ising magnetization field? This is the converse measurability conjecture stated by Cahen, Garban, and Sepúlveda.
- **Open issue (from the theorem’s sharpness condition):** For color counts r where no affine plane of order r−1 exists, determine the best rainbow-triangle constant and classify near-extremizers beyond the available structural statement (Chao, Sankar, and Yu).
- **Research direction (synthesis):** Test whether the regularized-transport support profiles and sharp L^p constants persist under weaker density regularity or less regular domains; these cases lie outside the assumptions stated by González-Sanz and Nutz.
- **Research direction (synthesis):** Read the full prime-geodesic paper to compare the precise old and new error terms and identify which subgroup/arithmetic features drive the improvement (Acosta Reche).
