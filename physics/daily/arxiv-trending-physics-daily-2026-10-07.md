## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [First observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz decay ψ(3686) → μ⁺μ⁻η′](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07054)
- **Abstract:** BESIII reports the first observation of this rare decay, measuring its branching fraction and comparing the muon and electron channels with vector-meson-dominance expectations.[2]
- **Authors:** BESIII Collaboration: M. Ablikim, M. N. Achasov, P. Adlarson, X. C. Ai, C. S. Akondi, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.07054v1` (published 2026-10-05 03:52:22 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A first-observation result based on 2.7124 billion ψ(3686) events, with a reported 6.1σ significance; the paper supplies a measured branching fraction and uncertainty rather than a forecast.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The authors measure B(ψ(3686) → μ⁺μ⁻η′) = (4.1 ± 1.0(stat) ± 0.4(syst)) × 10⁻⁷ and find the muon/electron channel ratio 0.22 ± 0.07, consistent with the model within 1σ.[2]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; BESIII data collected at the BEPCII e⁺e⁻ collider.[2]
- **Caveat:** The authors report no significant lepton-flavor-universality violation; the branching-fraction statistical uncertainty exceeds the systematic term.[2]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [First Measurement of Compton Scattering on ³He at HIγS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07579)
- **Abstract:** The collaboration presents the first elastic Compton-scattering measurements on a ³He target, using polarized photons at two energies to probe neutron electromagnetic polarizabilities.[1]
- **Authors:** J. Zhou, E. Mancil, M. W. Ahmed, B. Crowe, G. Feldman, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.07579v1` (published 2026-10-06 01:13:50 UTC; categories: `nucl-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** First absolute differential cross-section data for this target, measured at multiple angles and two photon energies; the arXiv record says accepted for Physical Review Letters.[1]
- **Claimed result:** The authors find chiral effective field theory agrees with the 97.1 MeV data but overpredicts the data at 61.5 MeV, motivating further theory work.[1]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; circularly polarized quasi-monoenergetic beams from the High Intensity γ-ray Source at TUNL.[1]
- **Caveat:** The low-energy disagreement is unresolved; the paper reports point-to-point and normalization systematics, so the discrepancy should not be treated as a settled failure of the theory.[1]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Calibration-Free Broadband Reconstructive Spectrometer based on Conical Phase Modulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07021)
- **Abstract:** The authors demonstrate an axicon-based reconstructive spectrometer that builds its spectral-encoding matrix computationally, removing the need for optical calibration with known-wavelength sources.[5]
- **Authors:** Junrui Liang, Zhongming Huang, Yanting Guo, Jun Ye, Yanzhao Ke, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.07021v1` (published 2026-10-04 15:18:55 UTC; categories: `physics.optics`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The work reports an experimental calibration-free demonstration across a 650 nm band, with stated spectral resolution, and compares reconstructed peaks against a commercial spectrometer.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The reported reconstruction spans 1050–1700 nm at 2.9 nm resolution, using a deterministic transmission matrix derived from conical phase modulation.[5]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; axicon-based optical setup with broadband reconstruction measurements.[5]
- **Caveat:** The authors say the underlying data are not publicly available at submission and may be requested from them; independent reproduction and evaluation beyond the reported setup remain open.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.
- **Themes:** Instrumentation & detectors; Computational & data methods

### 4. [The pseudospectrum for gravitational perturbations in Kerr spacetimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06954)
- **Abstract:** The authors formulate spin-2 Kerr quasinormal-mode perturbations as a non-self-adjoint eigenvalue problem and analyze how the associated pseudospectrum changes under energy norms.[3]
- **Authors:** Hongsheng Zhang, Zong-Kuan Guo, Rong-Gen Cai
- **arXiv:** `2610.06954v1` (published 2026-10-03 13:57:55 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`, `astro-ph.HE`, `hep-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It connects general relativity, high-energy theory, and astrophysical phenomena, and gives quantitative pseudospectral comparisons for scalar and gravitational perturbations.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The authors find substantial Kerr spectral sensitivity persists through the norms they study and is stronger for spin-2 gravitational perturbations than for the scalar case.[3]
- **Evidence type:** theoretical/computational; hyperboloidal formulation with two-dimensional Chebyshev collocation and a symmetrized H² energy norm.[3]
- **Caveat:** This is an operator-analysis result, not an observation of unstable black-hole ringdown; the proposed H^|s| norm pattern is verified here for s = 0 and s = 2, not established for every spin.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Computational & data methods

### 5. [All-Orders Classical Soft Graviton Theorems in the Newtonian Limit and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07134)
- **Abstract:** The authors derive an infinite series of leading-logarithmic gravitational-wave tail terms in the Newtonian limit and examine how an existing all-orders conjecture behaves for multi-particle scattering.[8]
- **Authors:** Debanjan Karan, Babli Khatun, Raghu Mahajan, Biswajit Sahoo, Ashoke Sen
- **arXiv:** `2610.07134v1` (published 2026-10-05 18:00:00 UTC; categories: `hep-th`, `gr-qc`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Cross-listed between high-energy theory and gravitation; the authors identify a concrete boundary of an existing conjecture at three or more incoming or outgoing particles.[8]
- **Claimed result:** Their Newtonian-limit results agree with the prior conjecture for two-particle scattering but not in general for three or more; a beyond-Newtonian extension is proposed and passes consistency checks.[8]
- **Evidence type:** theoretical; analytic soft-theorem and scattering-amplitude analysis.[8]
- **Caveat:** The beyond-Newtonian all-orders statement is explicitly a conjecture, not a proof.[8]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 6. [An Assessment of the Triangulation Capabilities of a Multi-Site All-Sky Infrared Camera Array](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06922)
- **Abstract:** The Galileo Project team describes commissioning three multi-camera infrared sites and validates an unconstrained-trajectory triangulation pipeline against aircraft with ADS-B records.[6]
- **Authors:** Abraham Loeb, Alex Delacroix, Ezra Kelderman, Eric Masson, Regina Sarmiento
- **arXiv:** `2610.06922v1` (published 2026-10-02 22:01:40 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.IM`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It reports a week-long, multi-site test with 1,650 camera-hours, 210 million detections, and quantified position and speed recovery against known aircraft tracks.[6]
- **Claimed result:** For aircraft triangulated from all three sites, 99% of recovered distances are within 5% of ADS-B references; the corresponding figure is 83% for a single site pair.[6]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; commissioned long-wave infrared arrays, validated using ADS-B aircraft records collected during 24–30 May 2026.[6]
- **Caveat:** The validation concerns aircraft with external ADS-B truth data; the paper does not establish detection of anomalous or unexplained objects, and it is submitted to a journal rather than peer-reviewed in the record.[6]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Instrumentation & detectors; Computational & data methods

- **Evidence type:** computational; numerical Rydberg-array dynamics and estimator tests, not a hardware experiment.[4]
- **Caveat:** The result depends on simulated dynamics and calibration data; the paper does not demonstrate a deployed sensor or an experimental Heisenberg-limited measurement.[4]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026 batch.

- **Gravitational-wave theory at two scales:** Kerr ringdown pseudospectra concern strong-field black-hole perturbations, while soft-graviton tails concern scattering and waveform asymptotics. - **Searches moving into new measurement channels:** The lunar dark-photon proposal uses radio telescopes, while Rydberg-array sensing uses many-body correlations; both are promising methods whose claims remain projection- or simulation-based.[4][7]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-identified:** The ³He Compton paper finds χEFT overpredicts the 61.5 MeV data while matching at 97.1 MeV. Follow-up measurements at additional energies and a refined theory comparison could determine whether the mismatch reflects truncation, nuclear effects, or experimental uncertainties.[2]
- **Author-identified:** The soft-graviton paper’s beyond-Newtonian all-orders result remains conjectural. A proof or counterexample for general multi-particle scattering is the direct next theoretical test.[8]
- **Synthesis:** The Kerr study finds large pseudospectral response under its tested norms, but does not quantify the resulting bias in astrophysical waveform inference. Propagating realistic perturbations through ringdown templates and comparing with detector noise would connect the mathematical result to observables.[3]
- **Synthesis:** The Rydberg sensing advantage is numerical. A hardware experiment should test calibration drift, finite sampling, and the estimator’s claimed noise robustness against an SQL baseline.[4]
- **Synthesis:** The lunar-radio paper offers projected reach rather than data. End-to-end background modeling and an observational pilot would test whether the predicted region is practically accessible.[7]
