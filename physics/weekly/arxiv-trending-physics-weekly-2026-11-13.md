## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Imaging exoplanets below the telescope stability limit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13257)
- **Abstract:** The authors use a differentiable physical model to subtract starlight from JWST/NIRCam coronagraph data, reporting 2–15× better sensitivity than standard methods and a first 4.3 μm image of 51 Eridani b.
- **Authors:** Rodrigo Ferrer-Chavez, Jason J. Wang, Katherine L. Bouman, William T. Freeman, Laurent Pueyo et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.13257v1` (published 2026-11-09 21:26:14 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.EP`, `astro-ph.IM`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Published in Nature; independent coverage from Caltech; the authors demonstrate the method on real JWST observations and report a previously unavailable planet detection.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report 2–15× sensitivity improvements on JWST/NIRCam data and an SNR 6.3 detection of 51 Eridani b in the 4.3 μm filter.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; tested on observational JWST/NIRCam coronagraph data.
- **Caveat:** The authors have not reached the photon-noise limit and say their model is not yet systematically calibrated to infer instrument-instability parameters with uncertainties.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-09 batch.
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [Intracycle Interference in Strong-Field Photoemission from a Tungsten Needle Tip](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14205)
- **Abstract:** Carrier-envelope-phase-resolved spectra from a tungsten needle driven at 1570 nm reveal intracycle interference among rescattered plateau electrons, supported by quantum-orbit and time-dependent Schrödinger-equation simulations.
- **Authors:** Timo Wirth, Jonas Heimerl, Stefan Meier, Peter Hommelhoff
- **arXiv:** `2611.14205v1` (published 2026-11-10 15:27:46 UTC; categories: `physics.optics`, `physics.atom-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Published in Physical Review Letters; the paper reports a first experimental observation of this interference in plateau electrons and has an associated public data/figure package.
- **Claimed result:** The authors identify intracycle interference near the rescattering cutoff in two-cycle, CEP-stable photoemission spectra and use the pattern to probe electron dynamics on attosecond timescales.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; carrier-envelope-phase-resolved photoemission from a nanometric tungsten tip, interpreted with quantum-orbit and TDSE simulations.
- **Caveat:** The authors suggest that electronic decoherence may explain the lack of clear interference at lower energies; that interpretation is not itself a direct measurement of the decoherence mechanism.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Atomic, molecular & optical physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Hierarchical interfacial anchoring for durable perovskite solar cells](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13746)
- **Abstract:** The authors report a halide pretreatment that strengthens buried interfaces in inverted perovskite cells, reaching 26.9% certified efficiency and retaining 94% of initial efficiency after 400 thermal cycles from −40 to 85 °C.
- **Authors:** Jiaxin Wu, Hongzhuo Wu, Zuhong Zhang, Xiaoyu Guan, Jinbo Zhao et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.13746v1` (published 2026-11-10 08:48:23 UTC; categories: `physics.optics`, `cond-mat.mtrl-sci`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper has a Science Advances DOI and reports both certified device efficiency and a concrete thermal-cycling test, connecting materials chemistry to device durability.
- **Claimed result:** The authors attribute improved adhesion and cycling tolerance to hierarchical interfacial anchoring, with device efficiency of 27.3% (26.9% certified) and 94% retention after 400 cycles.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; inverted perovskite solar-cell fabrication, efficiency certification and thermal cycling.
- **Caveat:** The reported cycling protocol is a laboratory stress test; it does not by itself establish field lifetime under the full range of operating and degradation conditions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

### 4. [Nucleon sigma terms in lattice QCD at physical pion mass and in the continuum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14103)
- **Abstract:** Using four physical-pion-mass lattice ensembles at four lattice spacings, the authors extract continuum light, strange and charm nucleon sigma terms while fitting contributions from low-lying multihadron excited states.
- **Authors:** Constantia Alexandrou, Christos Iona, Giannis Koutsou, Yan Li, Srijit Paul, Ferenc Pittler
- **arXiv:** `2611.14103v1` (published 2026-11-10 13:57:35 UTC; categories: `hep-lat`, `hep-ex`, `hep-ph`, `nucl-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Cross-listed across four high-energy and nuclear subfields; the study combines physical-mass ensembles, four lattice spacings and an explicit treatment of excited-state contamination, with direct relevance to nucleon inputs used in dark-matter constraints.
- **Claimed result:** The authors obtain σπN = 53.0(7.4)(1.6) MeV, σs = 58.2(17.0)(2.1) MeV and σc = 112(28)(25) MeV, quoting statistical and systematic uncertainties.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; Nf=2+1+1 twisted-mass lattice-QCD ensembles and global continuum fits.
- **Caveat:** The reported errors remain sizable, particularly for σs and σc; the extraction also depends on modeling multihadron excited-state contributions and continuum behavior.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 5. [Supercurrent-controlled magnon transport via suppression of the superconducting gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11781)
- **Abstract:** In a ferromagnet/superconductor heterostructure, the authors observe declining magnon transport as supercurrent rises and attribute it to gap suppression that increases dissipation into Bogoliubov quasiparticles.
- **Authors:** Tetsuma Mandokoro, Yoichi Shiota, Hisakazu Matsuki, Ryusuke Hisatomi, Shutaro Karube et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.11781v1` (published 2026-11-09 02:39:13 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.supr-con`, `cond-mat.mtrl-sci`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Accepted for publication in Physical Review Applied; the experiment links supercurrent, superconducting gap changes and magnon transport without relying on Joule heating.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report direct experimental evidence that supercurrent suppresses magnon transport through enhanced quasiparticle dissipation in the superconducting layer.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; magnon-transport measurements in a ferromagnet/superconductor heterostructure.
- **Caveat:** The result is demonstrated in a specific heterostructure; transferability and operating range for other magnonic devices remain to be established.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-09 batch.
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

### 6. [UniHam: A Large-Scale SOC-Complete Dataset and Benchmark for Hamiltonian Learning in Materials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12509)
- **Abstract:** UniHam provides more than 100,000 DFT-computed, spin-orbit-coupled Hamiltonians spanning 72 elements and benchmarks learned Hamiltonians for reconstruction, out-of-distribution generalization and downstream properties.
- **Authors:** Yuewen Huang, Pin Chen, Yutong Lu
- **arXiv:** `2611.12509v1` (published 2026-11-09 14:49:03 UTC; categories: `cs.LG`, `cond-mat.mtrl-sci`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A KDD 2026 proceedings paper; the released benchmark addresses a stated data gap and evaluates model behavior under composition and symmetry shifts rather than relying on a single accuracy measure.
- **Claimed result:** The authors find element- and SOC-dependent failure modes, variable compositional OOD robustness, and a need for spectral-level evaluation before Hamiltonian predictions are trusted for downstream properties.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; DFT-generated Hamiltonian dataset and machine-learning benchmark.
- **Caveat:** The benchmark is built from DFT-computed Hamiltonians; its results do not alone validate predictions against measured material behavior.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-09 batch.
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials; Computational & data methods

### 7. [MAGI (Multivariate Autoencoder for particle Generative Inference): a generative Machine Learning framework for accelerated multi-stage particle simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14710)
- **Abstract:** MAGI learns particle crossings with a conditional variational autoencoder and, in benchmark Geant4 simulations, reports detector-level rates consistent with full simulation at about 1σ while accelerating some cases by nearly 240×.
- **Authors:** Francesco Monastra, Marco Pietrosanto, Giuseppe Angora, Stefano Cavuoti, Francesco Fenu et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.14710v1` (published 2026-11-10 20:16:18 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.IM`, `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Cross-listed between astrophysical instrumentation and particle experiment; the authors provide a Zenodo software release and compare resampled simulations against full Geant4 results.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report statistical amplification from 150 to 12,000 and computational speedups from 2.1× to almost 240×, depending on source and mass model.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; conditional-VAE resampling validated against Geant4 detector-level simulations.
- **Caveat:** Validation is on benchmark models within the same Geant4 simulation framework; accuracy and speedup may vary with geometry, source and simulation configuration.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Instrumentation & detectors; Computational & data methods

### 8. [Impact of dynamical, quadratic, and higher-order tidal effects on gravitational-wave observations of binary neutron stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14628)
- **Abstract:** The authors incorporate dynamical and nonlinear tidal terms through 8PN order into neutron-star inspiral waveforms and use Bayesian comparisons to estimate when these terms could be distinguished or bias inferred tidal deformabilities.
- **Authors:** Andrea Valletta, Francesco Crescimbeni, Takuya Katagiri, Francesco Pannarale, Paolo Pani
- **arXiv:** `2611.14628v1` (published 2026-11-10 19:00:05 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.HE`, `gr-qc`, `hep-ph`, `nucl-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Cross-listed across astrophysics, relativity, phenomenology and nuclear theory; it quantifies a waveform-modeling effect relevant to future precision inference of dense nuclear matter.
- **Claimed result:** The authors find that 8PN tidal response can rival lower-order terms for subsolar-mass systems and project that exceptionally loud GW170817-like events could resolve higher-order effects with third-generation detectors.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; post-Newtonian waveform modeling and Bayesian model comparisons, including Einstein Telescope projections.
- **Caveat:** The detector forecasts are conditional on unusually loud signals and assumed waveform/noise models; this is not a detection of an additional tidal effect.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Astrophysics; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 9. [Quantum Computing Enhanced Search for Wavelike Dark Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14629)
- **Abstract:** The authors propose a Grover-search-based quantum sensing strategy for weak axion signals and report projected sensitivity beyond astrophysical limits in a transmon-based benchmark, reaching the QCD axion band only in a scenario with one million entangled sensing qubits.
- **Authors:** Yunjia Bao, Yu Cheng, Liang Jiang, Lian-Tao Wang
- **arXiv:** `2611.14629v1` (published 2026-11-10 19:00:05 UTC; categories: `hep-ph`, `astro-ph.CO`, `hep-ex`, `quant-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Cross-listed across four communities—particle phenomenology, cosmology, experiment and quantum physics—and develops a concrete sensing protocol with a transmon-based resource benchmark.
- **Claimed result:** The authors project access to axion parameter space beyond existing astrophysical limits and to the QCD axion band at the stated million-entangled-qubit scale.
- **Evidence type:** Forecast/proposal; quantum-algorithm analysis and transmon-based sensitivity benchmark, not an experimental dark-matter observation.
- **Caveat:** The headline reach depends on very large entangled-qubit resources and favorable sensing/coherence assumptions; the abstract does not demonstrate that scale in hardware.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 batch.
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Cosmology & gravitation

## Trending Research Themes

- **Physics-informed inference is moving from simulated benchmarks to real observations.** FLIP applies differentiable instrument modeling to JWST coronagraph data, while MAGI validates learned particle resampling against Geant4 and UniHam tests learned Hamiltonians across material and symmetry shifts. These are distinct workflows, not replications of one result.
- **Interfaces and dissipation are practical control knobs.** The perovskite study engineers buried-interface anchoring to improve thermal-cycle retention; the magnon study uses supercurrent to tune dissipation across a ferromagnet/superconductor boundary. Both emphasize interface-specific mechanisms, but address different devices and observables.
- **Precision observables increasingly depend on modeling what used to be treated as nuisance structure.** Strong-field photoemission resolves sub-cycle interference; lattice QCD fits low-lying multihadron contamination in nucleon matrix elements; and the binary-neutron-star study tracks higher-order tides in waveform inference. These are separate advances, not independent confirmations.
- **Quantum sensing and high-energy searches are converging at the proposal stage.** The axion-search paper translates a Grover-based sensing protocol into projected reach; the QCD sigma-term calculation supplies hadronic inputs relevant to dark-matter scattering. The connection is methodological and phenomenological, not evidence for an axion signal.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated — FLIP calibration and sensitivity:** FLIP has not reached photon-noise-limited performance, and its instrument-instability parameters lack systematic uncertainty calibration. Test the pipeline on additional JWST targets and independent instruments, propagate PSF-model uncertainty, and measure injection-recovery completeness. [2611.13257]
- **Author-stated — electron coherence:** The strong-field paper says electronic decoherence may obscure lower-energy interference. Follow-up measurements could vary tip temperature/material and CEP while comparing with an open-quantum-system model. [2611.14205]
- **Author-stated — lattice excited states and precision:** Sigma-term errors remain large, and excited-state treatment is a central analysis issue. Independent ensembles and fit strategies, with broader control of continuum and finite-volume effects, could test the extracted sigma terms. [2611.14103]
- **Synthesis — device durability:** The perovskite result covers 400 thermal cycles, not full outdoor aging; longer light/heat/humidity testing and larger device areas would test whether the interfacial design transfers to practical modules. [2611.13746]
- **Synthesis — model-to-hardware gap:** The axion reach is projected at very high entangled-qubit count, while the gravitational-wave result forecasts observability for exceptionally loud events. Staged hardware/resource demonstrations and injection-recovery studies across realistic noise and waveform uncertainties would test these assumptions. [2611.14629, 2611.14628]

## Takeaway

The strongest signals this week are methods that make hidden physical structure measurable: a JWST image-reconstruction advance, sub-cycle electron interference, and carefully engineered or modeled interfaces. Several other papers improve the inputs and simulation tools needed for future searches, but their wider value depends on uncertainty control and experimental follow-through.
