## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [The Erdős–Sós conjecture in dense graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05417)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim that every sufficiently large graph with more than $(k-2)n/2$ edges contains every $k$-vertex tree whenever $k$ is at least a fixed positive fraction of $n$, implying a solution to a 51-year-old Erdős–Graham problem on multicolor Ramsey numbers of trees.
- **Authors:** Bruce Reed, Maya Stein
- **arXiv:** `2609.05417v1` (published 2026-09-04 17:59 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete claimed advance on a famous long-standing extremal-combinatorics conjecture, with a direct Ramsey-theoretic corollary. Semantic Scholar citation and influential-citation counts were unavailable because its API returned HTTP 429; no popularity claim is made.
- **Claimed result:** For every fixed γ>0, there is n₀ such that for n≥n₀ and k≥γn, every n-vertex graph with more than (k−2)n/2 edges contains every k-vertex tree. The authors state that this yields the Erdős–Graham multicolor tree-Ramsey result.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Dense finite graphs; n sufficiently large; tree size k grows linearly with n, bounded below by γn.
- **Caveat:** This is a new arXiv preprint; the abstract states the claimed theorem but does not establish peer-reviewed verification of the proof or its relationship to the full, unrestricted Erdős–Sós conjecture.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 2. [The weak-type (1,1) bound for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is O(√n log n)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05377)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim a weak-type (1,1) estimate with dimensional dependence O(√n log n) for the centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal function over Euclidean balls, improving the classical O(n) order.
- **Authors:** Daniel Spector, Cody B. Stockdale
- **arXiv:** `2609.05377v1` (published 2026-09-04 17:28 UTC; categories: `math.CA`, `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A clean improvement to a classical high-dimensional harmonic-analysis bound, cross-listed between classical analysis and PDE, with a named mechanism for the improvement. Semantic Scholar counts were unavailable (HTTP 429); no citation momentum is inferred.
- **Claimed result:** The paper claims O(√n log n) weak-type control by first bounding the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator pointwise by the heat maximal operator with a √n loss, then improving the heat maximal weak-type bound from O(√n) to O(log n).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Centered maximal operator; Euclidean balls; weak-type (1,1) constants as dimension n varies.
- **Caveat:** The abstract gives the asymptotic order, not whether the logarithmic factor is optimal; the preprint’s 27-page proof requires expert checking.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 3. [Sophus Lie's problem on two-dimensional metrics with projective symmetries: completing the local classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05280)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim a complete local, up-to-isometry classification of two-dimensional Riemannian and Lorentzian metrics with projective symmetry algebra of dimension at least two, including previously untreated non-regular points.
- **Authors:** Gianni Manno, Vladimir S. Matveev, Filippo Salis
- **arXiv:** `2609.05280v1` (published 2026-09-04 15:33 UTC; categories: `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It claims to close a classification problem originating with Sophus Lie in 1882 and supplies a complete list of non-isometric normal forms in the singular/non-regular case. Semantic Scholar counts were unavailable (HTTP 429); the ranking reflects mathematical scope, not measured attention.
- **Claimed result:** The paper states that it completes the local classification, up to isometry, of two-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian metrics with projective symmetry algebra of dimension at least two by adding the non-regular case to the known regular classification.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Local neighborhoods of points on two-dimensional Riemannian or Lorentzian manifolds; projective symmetry algebra dimension at least two; classification is up to isometry.
- **Caveat:** “Complete” is the authors’ preprint claim and depends on the correctness of both the new singular-case analysis and the previously known regular-case classification.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology

### 4. [Replica symmetry breaking at arbitrary depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05317)
- **Abstract:** In mixed even-p spin Ising spin glasses at positive temperature and zero field, the authors construct finite polynomial mixtures exhibiting exact k-step replica symmetry breaking for every finite k and transitions between successive depths.
- **Authors:** P. M. Aronow, Patrick Lopatto
- **arXiv:** `2609.05317v1` (published 2026-09-04 16:11 UTC; categories: `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It targets arbitrary-depth phase structure in a central probabilistic model of spin glasses and states a general transition mechanism, with an 84-page treatment. Semantic Scholar counts were unavailable (HTTP 429); no external attention signal was verified.
- **Claimed result:** For every integer k≥1, the authors claim to construct polynomial mixtures whose Parisi measures are exactly k-RSB, with each phase persisting on an open coefficient set; they also claim r-RSB to (r+1)-RSB transitions generated by adding λq^p for large even p.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Mixed even p-spin Ising spin glasses; positive temperature; zero external field; finite-step Parisi measures and admissible finite polynomial covariances.
- **Caveat:** The results concern finite RSB depths and specified covariance constructions; they do not by themselves settle the full structure of arbitrary spin-glass models or infinite-step behavior.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics

### 5. [A Log-Free n^(1/5) Bound for Chowla's Cosine Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05338)
- **Abstract:** The paper claims a uniform lower bound K(S)≥c|S|^(1/5) for Chowla's cosine problem, removing the subpolynomial loss from a recent |S|^(1/5−o(1)) estimate.
- **Authors:** Abhishek Shankar
- **arXiv:** `2609.05338v1` (published 2026-09-04 16:43 UTC; categories: `math.CA`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It removes a loss term from a recent quantitative bound and bridges harmonic analysis with additive number theory. The abstract reports an exact averaging identity and an ancillary verification script. Semantic Scholar counts were unavailable (HTTP 429).
- **Claimed result:** For every finite set S of positive integers, with K(S) defined as the negative minimum of its cosine sum on the circle, the authors claim K(S)≥c|S|^(1/5) for an absolute c>0.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite sets of positive integers; trigonometric sums on the one-dimensional torus; absolute-constant quantitative bound.
- **Caveat:** The exponent 1/5 is not claimed to be optimal in the abstract, and the preprint’s short proof remains to be independently checked.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Algebra & number theory

- **Claimed result:** The author claims a generalized Coleman isomorphism for various cohomology theories evaluated on proper regular p-adic formal schemes, derived from a description of transfer limits in a cyclotomic tower as a cyclotomic synthetic spectrum.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Proper regular p-adic formal schemes; syntomic/cohomological settings; cyclotomic towers; THH and motivic filtration; uses cited calculations of THH of cyclotomic integer rings and free-loop transfer.
- **Caveat:** The abstract describes future applications rather than proving a specific new Iwasawa-theory theorem; the technical dependencies and 57-page argument require specialist review.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Geometry & topology

## Trending mathematical themes

- **Sharp quantitative improvements:** Papers 2 and 5 remove or reduce losses in dimension-dependent and additive-combinatorial bounds, replacing older O(n) or n^(1/5−o(1)) behavior with sharper stated forms.
- **Classification and extremal completion:** Papers 1 and 3 both present closure-type results: one for dense graph embeddings of trees and one for local geometric normal forms. These are isolated signals rather than evidence of a field-wide shift.
- **Bridges across mathematical physics and probability:** Paper 4 uses Parisi-measure structure to analyze arbitrary finite RSB depth, while paper 2 uses heat-flow maximal estimates as an analytic bridge.
- **Higher-structure methods:** Paper 6 links p-adic geometry, THH, K-theory, and homotopical transfer constructions, showing continued activity at interfaces rather than within a single subfield.

## Research opportunities

### Potential research areas

- Determine whether the O(√n log n) Hardy–Littlewood estimate is sharp, and identify geometric settings where the heat-maximal comparison can remove the logarithm (paper 2).
- Extend the arbitrary-depth RSB construction beyond finite polynomial covariances or characterize the limiting/infinite-depth regime (paper 4).
- Develop explicit arithmetic and Iwasawa-theoretic consequences of the generalized Coleman isomorphism (paper 6).

### Unsolved problems

- Whether the dense-graph theorem can be strengthened to the full parameter range of the Erdős–Sós conjecture, rather than k≥γn for fixed γ>0 (paper 1; synthesis based on the stated range).
- Whether the exponent 1/5 in Chowla’s cosine problem can be improved (paper 5; the abstract does not claim optimality).
- Whether the logarithmic factor in the maximal-function bound is necessary (paper 2; not resolved in the abstract).

### Potential research directions

- Test the dense-graph tree-embedding threshold near the boundary where k/n tends to zero, and compare the claimed result with extremal constructions (paper 1).
- Build explicit examples from the non-regular projective-symmetry normal forms and study global, rather than local, completeness and topology (paper 3).
- Rework the exact averaging identity in Chowla’s problem for related trigonometric or higher-dimensional additive-intersection inequalities (paper 5).
