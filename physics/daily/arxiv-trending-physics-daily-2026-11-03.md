### 1. [The Neptunian ridge through the lens of TESS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03643)
- **Abstract:** Using TESS occurrence posteriors, the author finds a roughly twofold excess of 5–8-Earth-radius planets at 3–6-day periods, consistent with the ridge previously seen by Kepler.
- **Authors:** Amadeo Castro-González
- **arXiv:** `2611.03643v1` (published 2026-11-02 18:58:36 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.EP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A distinct survey's TESS occurrence data recover a concrete population feature, with a quantified 3.1σ-equivalent posterior contrast and explicit comparison against six occurrence profiles; this is a strong measurement-and-analysis signal, not a popularity metric.
- **Claimed result:** The author reports that the ridge's occurrence density is about twice that of the denser neighboring region (J = 2.05 with −0.44/+0.52 uncertainty; 99.9% posterior support), while evidence for an asymmetric ridge profile remains weak.
- **Evidence type:** Observational; TESS occurrence posteriors for FGK main-sequence stars, based on the survey's first four years (Sectors 1–55) and compared with earlier Kepler occurrence estimates.
- **Caveat:** The inferred ridge shape depends on occurrence-rate posteriors and profile choices; the author says the asymmetry is weakly supported and calls for more precise occurrence measurements.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-03 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics

### 2. [Ultrafast X-ray-induced dephasing of valence electrons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03635)
- **Abstract:** In silicon, energy-chirped X-ray diffraction measurements show that valence-bond coherence is lost in under 20 femtoseconds, before substantial lattice motion and even at low ionization fluence.
- **Authors:** J. Vonka, B. Pedrini, J. Ravnik, A. Steppke, E. Prat, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.03635v1` (published 2026-11-02 18:57:40 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.str-el`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper reports a direct time-resolved experiment separating valence-sensitive forbidden reflections from core-dominated Bragg peaks, with a sharply stated sub-20-fs effect that bears on how ultrafast crystallography is interpreted.
- **Claimed result:** The authors attribute the rapid loss of valence-sensitive scattering to valence-bond dephasing and argue that probing pristine valence-electron structure requires pulses shorter than the roughly 50-fs standard discussed in the paper.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; broadband, energy-chirped X-ray pulses probe elemental silicon at the SwissFEL Bernina instrument, comparing a structurally forbidden reflection with a simultaneously measured Bragg peak.
- **Caveat:** The authors describe their fluence model as phenomenological and say quantitative treatment of the forbidden-reflection suppression requires explicit nonequilibrium excited-state calculations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-03 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Mercury (Hg): a relativistic N-body simulation for Horndeski gravity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03637)
- **Abstract:** The authors introduce a relativistic particle-mesh N-body code that evolves matter, metric fields, and an additional scalar degree of freedom across the general linear Horndeski alpha-basis.
- **Authors:** Ahmad Nouri-Zonoz, Farbod Hassani, Julian Adamek, Emilio Bellini, Martin Kunz
- **arXiv:** `2611.03637v1` (published 2026-11-02 18:58:03 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.CO`, `gr-qc`, `physics.comp-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The work spans cosmology, gravitation, and computational physics through three category listings and provides a reusable simulation framework; the authors report validation against hi_class in the linear regime and recovery of established limits.
- **Claimed result:** The authors show that, in the luminal Horndeski models considered, a running effective Planck mass changes gravitational slip and vector/tensor friction terms, with corresponding changes in the effective dark-energy density-contrast power spectrum.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; relativistic particle-mesh simulations for six representative Horndeski models, checked against hi_class linear predictions and earlier k-essence/KGB limits.
- **Caveat:** This is an intermediate framework: the paper retains linear Horndeski scalar-field dynamics, so nonlinear scalar self-interactions and screening are not yet incorporated.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-03 announcement batch

### 4. [Fermi surface and field-induced Lifshitz transition in the antiferromagnet NbMnP exhibiting an anomalous Hall effect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02360)
- **Abstract:** Shubnikov–de Haas measurements on NbMnP match calculated low-field Fermi-surface orbits and show a changing oscillation spectrum at high fields that the authors interpret as a Lifshitz transition.
- **Authors:** Taichi Terashima, Yuya Hattori, David Graf, Yuki Arai, Hitoshi Sugawara, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.02360v1` (published 2026-11-02 01:07:26 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.mtrl-sci`, `cond-mat.str-el`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** High-field measurements extend to 41.5 T and combine quantum-oscillation data with first-principles calculations grounded in a neutron-diffraction magnetic structure; the paper reports a concrete field-dependent change rather than a proposed effect.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report that the low-field oscillation frequencies agree with the calculated bands, while the spectrum evolves at high field in a manner indicating a field-induced change in Fermi-surface topology; measured effective masses are about twice the band masses.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations in NbMnP single crystals, measured to 17.5 T and 41.5 T and compared with first-principles band calculations.
- **Caveat:** The microscopic reconstruction is not fully resolved; the authors note that NbMnP's noncollinear magnetism prevents a simple majority/minority-spin interpretation of the field evolution.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-03 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

## Trending Research Themes

- **Measurements resolving hidden electronic structure:** The silicon X-ray study isolates rapid valence-bond dephasing; NbMnP's field-dependent oscillations expose a Fermi-surface reconstruction; and scanning SQUID images show spatially varying current paths in TES devices. These are distinct samples and techniques, not independent confirmations of one result.
- **Population inference and model comparison:** The TESS paper tests multiple occurrence-profile families against the Neptunian ridge, while the nuclear active-subspace study compresses parameter sensitivity for selected observables. These are isolated examples of statistical/model-reduction tools serving different physical questions, not evidence of a single coordinated subfield trend.
- **Extending computational and theoretical frameworks:** Mercury moves relativistic Horndeski simulations beyond linear cosmological predictions; the ringdown paper improves a semianalytic waveform treatment; and the tomography paper closes a formal query-complexity regime. Together with graphene's edge-control model, these are method/theory advances rather than new observational detections.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated — Neptunian ridge shape:** The TESS analysis finds the ridge asymmetry weakly supported and calls for more precise occurrence measurements. A useful follow-up would combine matched TESS, Kepler, and future PLATO occurrence samples, test sensitivity to radius/host-star selections, and compare migration scenarios against atmospheric-loss alternatives. ([2611.03643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03643))
- **Author-stated — X-ray response model:** The silicon paper describes its fluence model as phenomenological and says quantitative treatment needs explicit nonequilibrium excited-state calculations. A follow-up could pair such calculations with pulse-duration and fluence scans, measuring forbidden and allowed reflections together to separate valence response from lattice motion. ([2611.03635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03635))
- **Author-stated — Horndeski nonlinear regime:** Mercury's scalar sector still uses linear perturbation equations; nonlinear self-interactions and screening remain future extensions. Implementing those effects and checking the code against controlled analytic or established numerical limits would test where the current power-spectrum predictions remain reliable. ([2611.03637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03637))
- **Synthesis from the reported NbMnP result:** The high-field oscillation spectrum changes substantially, but the noncollinear magnetic structure complicates a simple microscopic account. Angle- and temperature-resolved high-field oscillations, paired with field-dependent magnetization and band calculations, could help distinguish competing reconstruction mechanisms. ([2611.02360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02360))
