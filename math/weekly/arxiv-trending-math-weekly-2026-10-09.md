## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [The Exact Overlaps Conjecture for Self-Similar Measures on the Real Line](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10511)
- **Abstract:** For finitely supported contracting similarities of the real line, the authors claim the self-similar measure has dimension `min(1, h_mu/|chi_mu|)`, establishing the exact-overlaps conjecture and its generalized dimension formula in dimension one.[2][3]
- **Authors:** Samuel Kittle, Constantin Kogler.[2]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10511v1` (published 2026-10-07 17:52:17 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.PR`).[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper gives a crisp conjecture-level theorem and crosses dynamical systems with probability; its introduction also records a concurrent independent announcement of the same main formula, making this a particularly salient claim to check.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The authors' Theorem 1.1 gives `dim nu = min{1, h_mu/|chi_mu|}` for a probability measure on a finite family of contracting similarities of R; they say this yields the exact-overlaps criterion and a self-similar-set dimension formula when there are no exact overlaps.[3]
- **Assumptions and setting:** A finite family of contracting similarities on R with positive weights; `h_mu` is random-walk entropy and `chi_mu` the Lyapunov exponent.[3]
- **Caveat:** The result is specifically one-dimensional. The paper says another public manuscript independently announced the theorem; resolving the mathematics and the relationship between proofs requires expert comparison, not title-level inference.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026.[2]
- **Themes:** Dynamical Systems; Probability.[2]

### 2. [Abundance for threefolds in positive characteristic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10439)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim the abundance conjecture for projective log-canonical threefold pairs over perfect fields of characteristic greater than 42.[6][7]
- **Authors:** Fabio Bernasconi, Jakub Witaszek, Zheng Xu.[6]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10439v1` (published 2026-10-07 17:15:36 UTC; categories: `math.AG`).[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete advance on a central birational-geometry conjecture, with a precise characteristic threshold and substantial technical proof; the authors explicitly connect the timing of this preliminary posting to competing public claims.[6][7]
- **Claimed result:** If `(X,B)` is a projective lc pair of dimension 3 over a perfect field of characteristic `p > 42` and `K_X+B` is nef, then the authors prove it is semi-ample.[7]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Three-dimensional projective lc pairs over perfect fields, with the stated characteristic bound and nef log-canonical divisor.[7]
- **Caveat:** The authors call this a very preliminary version; they identify an irregular-boundary case as still open and say a companion paper is forthcoming. The claim does not cover all positive characteristics.[7]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026.[6]
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory.[6]

### 3. [Sharp dynamical isoperimetric principle for mean curvature flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10461)
- **Abstract:** For weak mean-curvature flows starting from a smooth bounded domain, the authors give sharp extinction-time and space-time isoperimetric bounds, with equality only for the round shrinking sphere.[8]
- **Authors:** Beomjun Choi, Wenkui Du, Seung Chul Park, Junseo Youn.[8]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10461v1` (published 2026-10-07 17:27:57 UTC; categories: `math.DG`, `math.AP`).[8]
- **Evidence for ranking:** It states explicit sharp inequalities and a rigidity case, linking differential geometry and nonlinear PDE; these are checkable mathematical signals rather than usage statistics.[8]
- **Claimed result:** The paper bounds extinction time by `(1/(2n))(P(Ω)/|S^n|)^(2/n)` for both level-set and integral Brakke flows, with equality only for a round ball and its standard multiplicity-one shrinking sphere; it also gives a sharp parabolic-measure inequality.[8]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Euclidean `R^(n+1)`, smooth bounded initial domain, and the stated weak-flow notions; the paper separately assumes mean convexity for its arrival-time estimates.[8]
- **Caveat:** The sharp statements concern specified weak formulations and smooth initial boundaries; they are not a universal comparison theorem for arbitrary initial sets or geometric flows.[8]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026.[8]
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Analysis & PDE.[8]

### 4. [The circle packing and Riemann uniformization embedding of the tree-weighted planar maps converges to Liouville quantum gravity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10505)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim that spanning-tree-weighted planar maps converge under circle-packing and Riemann-uniformization embeddings to the corresponding `sqrt(2)`-Liouville quantum-gravity surfaces, with an associated path converging to `SLE_8`.[4][5]
- **Authors:** Nina Holden, Pu Yu.[4]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10505v1` (published 2026-10-07 17:51:07 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math.CV`).[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A cross-field scaling-limit result linking probability, complex analysis and mathematical physics, with a specific continuum limit and a second path-level convergence claim.[4][5]
- **Claimed result:** In disk, sphere and whole-plane topologies, the embedded maps converge respectively to a `sqrt(2)`-LQG disk, sphere and cone as the number of faces grows; the natural path on map faces converges to `SLE_8`.[5]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Spanning-tree-weighted random planar maps, in the named topologies and discrete conformal embeddings; the proof combines earlier convergence results and a companion comparison of embeddings.[5]
- **Caveat:** This concerns a particular tree-weighted map model and builds on companion/prior work; it does not establish the same limit for planar maps in general.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026.[4]
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Geometry & topology; Mathematical physics.[4]

### 5. [The LeBrun–Salamon conjecture is true in dimension up to 56](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06336)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim the LeBrun–Salamon conjecture for positive quaternionic-Kähler manifolds of quaternionic dimension at most 14 (real dimension at most 56), using symmetry-rank and characteristic-number arguments.[9]
- **Authors:** Jordi Daura Serrano, Aron Gohr, Marie-Amélie Lawn, Travis Schedler.[9]
- **arXiv:** `2610.06336v1` (published 2026-10-05 13:42:00 UTC; categories: `math.DG`, `math.AG`).[9]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A clearly bounded theorem on a named conjecture, cross-listed between differential and algebraic geometry; the paper describes successive new positivity tools as the dimension range increases.[9]
- **Claimed result:** For positive quaternionic-Kähler manifolds with quaternionic dimension `n ≤ 14`, the authors rule out a minimal counterexample by bounding its isometry group and showing a related characteristic-number index is positive.[9]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Positive quaternionic-Kähler manifolds, restricted to quaternionic dimension at most 14.[9]
- **Caveat:** This is a finite-dimensional range, not a proof of the conjecture in all dimensions. The authors mention partial Lean verification, which is not equivalent to independent mathematical review of the full argument.[9]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 5 October 2026.[9]
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Algebra & number theory.[9]

### 6. [Navier-Stokes lost in translation: Why Lean verification of AI autoformalisation does not guarantee correct natural language proofs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144)
- **Abstract:** The authors argue that a mechanically checked Lean proof can fail to validate the natural-language argument it was meant to formalize, and give examples including a claimed Navier–Stokes blow-up proof.[10]
- **Authors:** Alexander Bastounis, Fabian Circelli, Anders C. Hansen.[10]
- **arXiv:** `2610.08144v1` (published 2026-10-06 10:58:01 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `cs.AI`, `math.LO`).[10]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Its three-way cross-listing and direct treatment of semantic fidelity in proof formalization make it timely alongside this week's public AI-mathematics claims; the paper reports concrete translation mismatches.[10]
- **Claimed result:** The authors formulate ambiguity resolution in natural-language mathematics as having unbounded Solvability Complexity Index/arithmetical hierarchy complexity, and argue that the cited Lean verification does not correspond to the natural-language Navier–Stokes argument.[10]
- **Assumptions and setting:** A theoretical analysis of semantic-faithfulness requirements in translating mathematical natural language into formal statements, supplemented by case studies.[10]
- **Caveat:** This is the authors' analysis of formalization fidelity and selected examples, not a resolution of the Navier–Stokes regularity problem or a general audit of every AI-generated proof.[10]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 6 October 2026.[10]
- **Themes:** Logic & foundations; Analysis & PDE; Machine learning theory.[10]

## Trending Research Themes

- **Conjecture-level claims with carefully bounded scopes:** the real-line dimension formula, abundance above characteristic 42, and the LeBrun–Salamon result through quaternionic dimension 14 are all explicit but have different hypotheses and boundaries; they should not be read as three instances of one field-wide movement.[3][7][9]
- **Sharp geometric structure:** the mean-curvature-flow paper pairs optimal bounds with a unique round-sphere equality case, while the LQG paper identifies scaling limits for two conformal embeddings of a specific random-map model.[5][8]
- **Proof-production and verification:** one paper examines whether Lean checks faithfully encode prose arguments; the exact-overlaps paper discusses a concurrent manuscript announcing the same theorem, and the abundance paper explicitly labels its own version preliminary amid competing AI-related claims.[3][7][10]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem (authors' stated limitation):** Bernasconi, Witaszek and Xu say the irregular-boundary case remains open; their theorem assumes characteristic greater than 42. A concrete direction is to test which steps lower the characteristic threshold and whether the argument can handle the remaining boundary case.[7]
- **Open problem (scope):** the LeBrun–Salamon result stops at quaternionic dimension 14. Extending the positivity/characteristic-number method beyond that range, or finding a counterexample there, is a direct next test of the claim.[9]
- **Research direction (synthesis):** compare the two independent proofs announced for the exact-overlaps dimension formula, then investigate which entropy or variance arguments could extend beyond similarities of the real line.[3]
- **Research direction (synthesis):** test whether the stated LQG/SLE limits persist for other weighted planar-map ensembles or embedding choices; the present result is specific to spanning-tree weighting and companion hypotheses.[5]
- **Open problem (methodological):** the Lean paper argues that proof checking alone does not certify faithful translation. A concrete direction is to develop formalization workflows that expose ambiguous natural-language steps and report translation obligations separately from kernel verification.[10]
