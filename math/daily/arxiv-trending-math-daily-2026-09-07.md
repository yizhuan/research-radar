## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [The Erdős–Sós conjecture in dense graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05417)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove the Erdős–Sós conjecture when the tree size is a fixed positive fraction of the host-graph order, and derive a Ramsey-number consequence.
- **Authors:** Bruce Reed, Maya Stein
- **arXiv:** `2609.05417v1` (published 2026-09-04 17:59:54 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Explicitly resolves the conjecture in the dense, linearly sized-tree regime and gives a corollary solving a 51-year-old Erdős–Graham problem; the paper had independent search visibility. Semantic Scholar reported 1 citation and 1 influential citation at the snapshot, unusually notable for a paper only days old.
- **Claimed result:** For every γ>0, sufficiently large n, and k≥γn, every n-vertex graph with more than (k−2)n/2 edges contains every k-vertex tree; the authors state a multicolor Ramsey consequence.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Simple n-vertex graphs, k-vertex trees, and the dense regime k linear in n; this is not the full all-parameter Erdős–Sós conjecture as stated in the abstract.
- **Caveat:** The abstract claims only the dense regime, so the general conjecture remains broader than this result; the preprint claim still requires expert verification.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 2. [Arithmetic Polyhedra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05349)
- **Abstract:** The paper proves a conjecture that arithmetic hyperbolic reflection groups arising from combinatorial polyhedra are commensurable with those from three basic examples, via a seed-polyhedra decomposition theorem.
- **Authors:** Daniel Allcock, Pat Devlin, Anna Felikson, Alex Kontorovich, Ian Whitehead
- **arXiv:** `2609.05349v1` (published 2026-09-04 16:57:28 UTC; categories: `math.MG`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Explicitly proves a 2016 Kontorovich–Nakamura conjecture, bridges metric geometry and number theory, and received independent search visibility including an HTML version and prior seminar context for the problem.
- **Claimed result:** All arithmetic ideal, right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra can be obtained by gluing copies of one of three seed polyhedra; consequently the stated commensurability conjecture is proved.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Three-dimensional hyperbolic reflection groups associated with combinatorial polyhedra, especially ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra.
- **Caveat:** The result concerns the arithmetic subclass and the specified hyperbolic/polyhedral setting, not arbitrary hyperbolic reflection groups; the abstract does not expose the proof’s classification details.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Algebra & number theory

### 3. [Sophus Lie's problem on two-dimensional metrics with projective symmetries: completing the local classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05280)
- **Abstract:** The authors complete the local isometry classification of two-dimensional Riemannian and Lorentzian metrics with projective symmetry algebras of dimension at least two, including the previously untreated non-regular case.
- **Authors:** Gianni Manno, Vladimir S. Matveev, Filippo Salis
- **arXiv:** `2609.05280v1` (published 2026-09-04 15:33:06 UTC; categories: `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract identifies completion of a problem posed by Sophus Lie in 1882 and supplies a complete list of non-isometric normal forms; independent search returned both the arXiv abstract and HTML paper.
- **Claimed result:** A complete list of local normal forms is obtained for singular points where the projective Lie algebra action is non-regular; combined with the known regular case, this completes the classification.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Local two-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian metrics—both Riemannian and Lorentzian—admitting projective symmetry algebras of dimension at least two; classification is up to isometry.
- **Caveat:** “Complete” is local and up to isometry, not a global classification of all such metrics; the preprint’s classification and equivalence arguments need specialist checking.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology

### 4. [The weak-type (1,1) bound for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is O(√n log n)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05377)
- **Abstract:** The authors improve the dimensional weak-type bound for the centered Euclidean Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator from O(n) to O(√n log n).
- **Authors:** Daniel Spector, Cody B. Stockdale
- **arXiv:** `2609.05377v1` (published 2026-09-04 17:28:15 UTC; categories: `math.CA`, `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete order-of-growth improvement on a classical central operator, with a named proof reduction and independent search visibility through the arXiv HTML page.
- **Claimed result:** The weak-type (1,1) estimate for centered maximal functions over Euclidean balls has dimensional dependence O(√n log n); the proof uses a pointwise comparison with the heat maximal operator and improves its bound to O(log n).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, Euclidean balls, weak-type (1,1) estimate, dimension n.
- **Caveat:** The bound is not stated as dimension-free and applies to the centered Euclidean-ball operator described in the abstract; optimality is not established here.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 5. [Replica symmetry breaking at arbitrary depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05317)
- **Abstract:** In mixed even p-spin Ising spin glasses, the authors construct finite polynomial mixtures with exactly k-step replica symmetry breaking for every finite k and establish transitions between consecutive finite depths.
- **Authors:** P. M. Aronow, Patrick Lopatto
- **arXiv:** `2609.05317v1` (published 2026-09-04 16:11:18 UTC; categories: `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Establishes arbitrary finite RSB depth, a broad structural result in mathematical spin-glass theory; the 84-page preprint received independent search visibility, including an HTML version and external mathematical commentary. Citation counts were unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the batch query.
- **Claimed result:** For each k≥1 there are finite polynomial mixtures whose Parisi measures are exactly k-RSB, with phases persisting on open coefficient sets; adding a sufficiently high even-p perturbation produces an r-RSB to (r+1)-RSB transition near a critical parameter.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Mixed even p-spin Ising spin glasses at positive temperature and zero external field; finite-step Parisi measures and admissible finite-polynomial covariances.
- **Caveat:** The result concerns finite RSB depths and the stated model class; it does not by itself settle the full structure of the infinite-step phase or all spin-glass models.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics

### 6. [A Log-Free n^(1/5) Bound for Chowla's Cosine Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05338)
- **Abstract:** The paper removes a subpolynomial/logarithmic loss from a recent lower bound in Chowla’s cosine problem, proving a uniform c|S|^(1/5) bound for every finite set S of positive integers.
- **Authors:** Abhishek Shankar
- **arXiv:** `2609.05338v1` (published 2026-09-04 16:43:05 UTC; categories: `math.CA`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Sharpens a recent bound to a clean polynomial exponent with no loss, cross-lists analysis and number theory, and includes an ancillary verification script. Citation counts were unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the batch query.
- **Claimed result:** If K(S) is the negative minimum over the torus of the cosine sum indexed by S, then K(S)≥c|S|^(1/5) for an absolute c>0.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite sets S of positive integers and the torus cosine sum in Chowla’s problem; the constant is absolute.
- **Caveat:** The exponent 1/5 is established with a sharp loss removal, but the abstract does not claim that the exponent is optimal.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Algebra & number theory

- **Claimed result:** A generalized Coleman isomorphism is derived for cohomology theories evaluated on proper regular p-adic formal schemes, based on a description of a cyclotomic synthetic spectrum and transfer-map constructions.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Proper regular p-adic formal schemes, cohomology theories with the required transfer structure, cyclotomic towers, and the cited THH calculations.
- **Caveat:** The abstract presents future Iwasawa-theory applications rather than proving those applications here; the construction depends on substantial prior THH calculations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-09-07
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Geometry & topology

## Trending mathematical themes
- **Named-problem resolution and classification:** Papers 1–3 each target an explicitly named historical or conjectural problem: a dense Erdős–Sós regime, arithmetic-polyhedra commensurability, and Lie’s 1882 local classification problem.
- **Removing asymptotic slack:** Papers 4 and 6 improve quantitative bounds by replacing weaker dimensional or logarithmic losses with sharper explicit orders.
- **Cross-field structural bridges:** Papers 2 and 7 connect geometry with arithmetic, while paper 5 connects rigorous probability with mathematical physics.
- **Finite-structure control:** Papers 1, 3, and 5 all turn broad qualitative questions into finite-regime classifications or constructions, but in different settings: graphs, metrics, and Parisi measures.

## Research opportunities

### Potential research areas
- Extend the dense Erdős–Sós methods toward regimes where k is sublinear in n, while preserving the Ramsey consequences; linked to paper 1.
- Test whether the three-seed decomposition in arithmetic hyperbolic polyhedra has analogues beyond ideal right-angled polyhedra or in related reflection-group classes; linked to paper 2.
- Transfer the arbitrary-depth RSB construction to other covariance families, nonzero external fields, or limits with infinitely many steps; linked to paper 5.
- Investigate whether the Coleman-isomorphism framework yields concrete Iwasawa-theoretic statements for particular p-adic formal schemes; linked to paper 7.

### Unsolved problems
- The full Erdős–Sós conjecture is broader than the dense linear-k regime proved in paper 1.
- Paper 4 does not settle whether O(√n log n) is optimal, nor whether the logarithmic factor can be removed.
- Paper 6 establishes the exponent 1/5 but does not state optimality; improving or proving a matching obstruction remains a natural question.
- Paper 5 addresses every finite RSB depth in the specified model class, leaving the relationship with infinite-step behavior outside the abstract’s claim.

### Potential research directions
- Combine dense embedding techniques from paper 1 with extremal or stability methods for intermediate tree sizes.
- Analyze the boundary between the seed-polyhedra theorem and non-right-angled or non-ideal cases in paper 2.
- Compare the heat-maximal-function route in paper 4 with geometric covering arguments to isolate the source of the logarithmic factor.
- Build explicit examples and computational checks for the cohomological transfer maps in paper 7, then test their behavior in cyclotomic towers.
