## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [The existence of small defining polynomials for number fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20600)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove that each degree-n number field of absolute discriminant D has a defining integral polynomial with coefficient sizes O_n(√D), resolving a conjecture of Ruppert.[2]
- **Authors:** Manjul Bhargava, Sameera Vemulapalli
- **arXiv:** `2610.20600v1` (published 2026-10-14 17:59:40 UTC; categories: `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The claimed bound applies to every number field of fixed degree and settles the stated 1998 conjecture; the proof combines ideal-lattice geometry with an averaged zeta-function argument.[2]
- **Claimed result:** Theorem 1 gives a degree-n generator α with an integral degree-n polynomial whose height is bounded by a constant depending on n times √D_K.[2]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The field is arbitrary, but the implied constant depends on its degree; the authors say the proof does not assume the Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis.[2]
- **Caveat:** The result gives an O_n bound, not a constant uniform in n.[2]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 15 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory

### 2. [A proof of the rational exponents conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18713)
- **Abstract:** For every rational r in [1,2], the authors claim a graph H with extremal number ex(n,H)=Θ(n^r), the Erdős–Simonovits rational exponents conjecture.[3]
- **Authors:** David Conlon, Oliver Janzer, Tao Jiang, Jaehoon Kim, Joonkyung Lee, Hong Liu, Sean Longbrake, Liana Yepremyan
- **arXiv:** `2610.18713v1` (published 2026-10-13 16:56:19 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The stated result covers the full rational exponent interval, rather than one subrange; the paper identifies a subdivision upper-bound theorem as the technical step that propagates to all exponents.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove the conjecture by combining Bukh–Conlon lower bounds with an upper-bound theorem for subdivisions of connected bipartite graphs.[3]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The conclusion is existential over finite graphs H and rational exponents r∈[1,2], with asymptotics in the number of vertices n.[3]
- **Caveat:** The authors explicitly describe this as a proof of the result “claimed by Epoch AI”; the paper situates that claim in prior combinatorial work, so the statement should be read as an attributed preprint result.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 3. [Superdiffusive Invariance Principle for the two-dimensional Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18847)
- **Abstract:** For two-dimensional ASEP, the paper derives logarithmically enhanced longitudinal diffusivity and proves convergence of the rescaled density-fluctuation field to a conservative stochastic heat equation.[4]
- **Authors:** Quentin Moulard
- **arXiv:** `2610.18847v1` (published 2026-10-13 17:57:59 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors state this is the first space-time scaling-limit result for a nonequilibrium interacting particle system at its critical dimension; it also derives a second-class-particle limit.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The fluctuation field converges under anisotropic scaling—√τ log(τ)^(1/3) along the drift and √τ transversely—to the specified Gaussian fixed point; a superdiffusive central limit theorem follows for the second-class particle.[4]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Initial law is Bernoulli product with density ρ∈(0,1); the jump kernel is finite-range, with nonzero drift and uniaxial asymmetry.[4]
- **Caveat:** The theorem's stated hypotheses restrict the model to that two-dimensional, finite-range, uniaxially asymmetric regime.[4]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics

### 4. [Proof of the Penrose Conjecture for Maximally Parameterized Initial Data Sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20559)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove the spacetime Penrose inequality in every dimension n≥3 for asymptotically flat initial data in a specified maximally parameterized class, under the dominant energy condition.[5]
- **Authors:** Hubert Bray, Tuan Dolmen, Marcus Khuri, David Schmidt
- **arXiv:** `2610.20559v1` (published 2026-10-14 17:46:46 UTC; categories: `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It gives an all-dimensional result for a major geometric inequality and reduces the target case to the Riemannian Penrose inequality using a new Schoen–Yau identity.[5]
- **Claimed result:** For the stated class, the authors construct a nonnegative-scalar-curvature comparison metric and derive the Penrose bound, subject to horizon-completion and asymptotic hypotheses.[5]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The data must be asymptotically flat, satisfy the dominant energy condition, and have the paper's maximally parameterized form; a natural horizon nondegeneracy condition and regularity of the comparison metric are also required.[5]
- **Caveat:** This is not a proof for arbitrary initial data: the authors leave a degenerate PDE existence problem and global surjectivity of the parameterization open, and exclude some degenerate horizon ends.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 15 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Mathematical physics

### 5. [Effective equidistribution of polynomial trajectories in SL₃(R)/SL₃(Z)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20513)
- **Abstract:** For fixed matrix-polynomial trajectories on the SL₃ lattice quotient, the author gives an intrinsic Diophantine criterion for power-rate equidistribution on the homogeneous orbit closure.[6]
- **Authors:** Hao Xing
- **arXiv:** `2610.20513v1` (published 2026-10-14 17:16:12 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The criterion is necessary and sufficient and uses polynomial tests of degree at most two, independent of the trajectory's degree; the paper bridges dynamics and number theory.[6]
- **Claimed result:** The author classifies possible orbit-closure groups and combines quantitative nilmanifold equidistribution, unipotent approximation, and effective flow estimates to prove the criterion.[6]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The result concerns a fixed matrix-polynomial trajectory in SL₃(R)/SL₃(Z) and equidistribution on its homogeneous orbit closure under the stated Diophantine condition.[6]
- **Caveat:** The theorem is specialized to the SL₃ quotient; the abstract does not claim a corresponding criterion for higher-rank SLₙ quotients.[6]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 15 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Probability & statistics

### 6. [Supercritical sharpness for Ising double random currents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20583)
- **Abstract:** The authors establish local uniqueness of the macroscopic cluster in the double-random-current representation of the Ising model throughout the supercritical regime.[7]
- **Authors:** Trishen S. Gunaratnam, Christoforos Panagiotis, Romain Panis, Franco Severo
- **arXiv:** `2610.20583v1` (published 2026-10-14 17:57:12 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The result is uniform over boundary sources for every β>β_c in d≥3 and supplies standard renormalization consequences, including exponential mixing.[7]
- **Claimed result:** The authors derive local uniqueness with high probability and recover exponential decay of truncated Ising correlations and exponential mixing for the FK-Ising measure, using a sprinkling argument.[7]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The model is the double random current on Z^d, d≥3, at β>β_c, uniformly over its source boundary conditions.[7]
- **Caveat:** The abstract says the correlation-decay and FK-mixing consequences were already obtained by earlier authors; the stated advance is a simpler proof route.[7]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 15 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics

### 7. [Sandpile avalanches in two dimensions are finite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13940)
- **Abstract:** The revised paper proves that an avalanche triggered at the origin in the stationary abelian sandpile on Z² has finitely many topplings.[8]
- **Authors:** Ahmed Bou-Rabee
- **arXiv:** `2610.13940v2` (published 2026-10-14 17:47:20 UTC; categories: `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It settles a question attributed to Athreya and Járai for the previously unresolved two-dimensional case; the revision lists a Lean 4 formalization.[8]
- **Claimed result:** Almost surely, adding a grain at the origin to the infinite-volume stationary sandpile in dimension two produces only finitely many topplings.[8]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The process is the abelian sandpile on Z² in the stationary state defined through finite boxes with absorbing boundary.[8]
- **Caveat:** The paper establishes finiteness, not a tail estimate or quantitative bound on avalanche size.[8]
- **Announcement type:** revision, v2 posted 14 Oct 2026; first submission 9 Oct 2026 (the replacement batch is not separately identified on the abstract page)
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 8. [All Serrin bands are unduloidal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20581)
- **Abstract:** The authors show that every planar Serrin domain with two boundary components belongs to the canonical unduloidal family.[9]
- **Authors:** Laurent Hauswirth, Pablo Mira
- **arXiv:** `2610.20581v1` (published 2026-10-14 17:56:58 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It gives a full classification within a geometrically specific overdetermined elliptic setting and connects PDE and differential geometry.[9]
- **Claimed result:** Any Serrin domain in R² with two boundary components is a member of the canonical unduloidal family.[9]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The domain is planar and has exactly two boundary components; the result is for Serrin domains.[9]
- **Caveat:** The abstract does not extend the classification to higher dimensions or domains with other boundary-component counts.[9]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 15 Oct 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Geometry & topology

## Trending Research Themes

- **Conjecture resolution and extremal bounds:** the number-field height bound and rational-exponent result both target long-standing conjectures, while the latter's proof is explicitly tied to prior graph-theoretic techniques.[2][3]
- **Stochastic models at critical or delicate regimes:** the ASEP scaling limit, Ising-current local uniqueness, and two-dimensional sandpile finiteness are distinct results across probability and mathematical physics; this is a topic recurrence, not a shared method.[4][7][8]
- **Geometry through PDE structure:** the Penrose result uses a comparison-metric identity, while the Serrin paper classifies a two-boundary planar family; both are constrained geometric PDE results, not a field-wide shift.[5][9]
- **Dynamics and arithmetic:** the SL₃ equidistribution criterion directly links polynomial trajectories, orbit closures, and Diophantine conditions; it is a single-paper signal in this shortlist.[6]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated open problem — general Penrose data:** the Penrose paper says a natural next step is to regularize its degenerate PDE system to obtain a general existence theory, and it leaves global surjectivity of the maximally parameterized form unresolved.[5]
- **Author-stated scope gap — horizons:** develop the comparison-metric completion for the more degenerate, potentially cylindrical horizon ends excluded by the current theorem.[5]
- **Research direction (synthesis) — stochastic extensions:** test whether the ASEP scaling argument survives broader asymmetry or jump-kernel assumptions, and whether the sandpile finiteness proof yields tail bounds; these are extensions suggested by the papers' stated hypotheses and conclusions, not author-claimed open conjectures.[4][8]
- **Research direction (synthesis) — geometric classification:** investigate analogues of the Serrin-band classification with more boundary components or in higher dimensions, without assuming the answer persists beyond the planar two-boundary case.[9]
