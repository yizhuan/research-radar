### 1. [Arrival-time problem in the quantum information era – observation of a Zeno crossover in monitored arrival-time distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09814)
- **Abstract:** The authors report superconducting-processor measurements of first-arrival distributions in periodically monitored spin chains and circuits, resolving a crossover between quantum-Zeno suppression and a low-monitoring regime for single and interacting particles in one and two dimensions.
- **Authors:** Yousef Mafi, K. G. S. H. Gunawardana, Kim Pöyhönen, Teemu Ojanen
- **arXiv:** `2611.09814v1` (published 2026-11-06 18:01:07 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`, `cond-mat.stat-mech`, `cond-mat.str-el`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete superconducting-hardware result crosses quantum physics and condensed-matter categories, and combines full arrival-time distributions with interacting-particle and 2D tests against quantum-trajectory theory.
- **Claimed result:** The authors say the optimal monitoring period tracks the single-particle bandwidth and that the crossover persists with interactions and in 2D; they describe this as the first experimental measurement of arrival-time distributions in monitored quantum transport.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; superconducting quantum hardware with mid-circuit measurements, compared with quantum-trajectory theory.
- **Caveat:** This uses the repeated-projective-measurement definition on lattice models; as the authors emphasize, arrival-time distributions depend on the detection protocol and there is no universally accepted arrival-time observable.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-11-09
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Condensed matter & materials

### 2. [Quantum-enabled measurement of gravitational forces at the milligram scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08619)
- **Abstract:** The authors use microwave optomechanical readout at millikelvin temperatures to measure the force from a coherently displaced 87 mg source mass on a 1.3 mg test mass, reporting a two-order-of-magnitude advance in small-mass gravity sensing and improved sensitivity at kHz frequencies.
- **Authors:** Matthew Herbst, Ewa Rej, Joe Depellette, Richa Cutting, Jinhoon Jeong, Mika A. Sillanpää
- **arXiv:** `2611.08619v1` (published 2026-11-05 19:13:27 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`, `cond-mat.mes-hall`, `gr-qc`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It reports a direct laboratory measurement at the quantum-mechanics/gravity interface and is cross-listed across quantum physics, mesoscopic physics, and gravitation; the paper also quantifies statistical and systematic uncertainties.
- **Claimed result:** The authors observe a force with the expected distance dependence and infer a gravitational constant consistent with CODATA within their stated 1σ systematic uncertainties; the experiment is a sensing advance, not a demonstration of quantum gravity.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; cryogenic microwave optomechanical membrane, moving gold source mass, and calibrated homodyne readout.
- **Caveat:** The inferred gravitational constant has sizeable statistical and systematic uncertainty; the masses are not prepared in non-classical motional states, which the authors identify as a future goal.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-11-09
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors

- **arXiv:** `2611.09788v1` (published 2026-11-06 17:45:30 UTC; categories: `nucl-th`, `hep-ex`, `hep-ph`, `nucl-ex`)

## Trending Research Themes

- **Quantum experiments meet measurement theory.** The monitored-arrival experiment (2611.09814) turns a foundational arrival-time question into a quantum-hardware measurement; the milligram gravity experiment (2611.08619) uses cryogenic optomechanics to probe a different quantum/gravitation boundary. Both are platform demonstrations, not evidence that quantum gravity has been observed.
- **Nuclear and relativistic matter across scales.** The lattice-QCD crossover result (2611.09150), gallium-transition-density test (2611.09788), BDNK simulations (2611.09582), and TEEC calculation (2611.08579) connect nuclear structure, hot QCD, relativistic fluids, and heavy-ion observables. They use distinct methods and are not independent confirmations of one another.
- **Simulation and inference are becoming part of detector design.** CaloCHILL (2611.08606) compresses high-granularity calorimeter showers into a learned latent space, while the LISA pipeline (2611.08880) separates simulated gravitational-wave transients from glitches. Both report fast analysis, but only the former models detector showers and neither paper establishes real-data performance at full scale.
- **Signals need cross-checks beyond a single model or event.** The CMB feature comparison (2611.08585) tests a limited model set, and the solar burst study (2611.09332) interprets one flare using radio and SDO data; broader model families and multi-event observations would test how general those patterns are.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Arrival-time protocol dependence (author-stated limitation):** arrival distributions depend on how the detector is modeled (2611.09814). Compare repeated-projective monitoring with alternative detector couplings and test scaling with chain length, particle number, and device noise.
- **Small-mass gravity sensitivity (author-discussed systematics):** thermal force noise and several non-gravitational couplings limit the measurement (2611.08619). Further reduce thermal noise, independently vary source-mass geometry and drive frequency, and repeat force calibration before pursuing non-classical source/test states.
- **QCD mass and continuum control:** the chiral-response scan uses heavier-than-physical light quarks, with a discretization check at one temperature (2611.09150). Extend multiple lattice spacings across the crossover and approach physical light-quark masses to test the reported change in slope.
- **Gallium cross-section uncertainty:** the tested shell-model transition densities leave the capture deficit unresolved (2611.09788). Independent nuclear-structure calculations and improved constraints on the relevant transition strengths could test whether the remaining discrepancy is nuclear or experimental.
- **Simulation-to-detector transfer:** CaloCHILL is benchmarked on simulated positive pions (2611.08606), and the LISA classifier uses simulated data (2611.08880). Validate both on independent detector-like data and varied particle/noise conditions before treating their reported speed and accuracy as operational performance.
- **Astrophysical and cosmological generality:** the solar interpretation rests on one event (2611.09332), while the CMB comparison uses simplified, selected signal families (2611.08585). Follow-up work could compare more flare events with radio imaging and expand the CMB likelihood analysis to more realistic feature and energy-injection models.
