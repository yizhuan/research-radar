# ArXiv Trending Physics — Weekly | 3–9 October 2026

Eight notable new submissions span collider and gravitational-wave tests, quantum hardware and clocks, and new measurement reach in astrophysics, optics, and fusion-plasma diagnostics. I’ll start with the clearest particle-physics and black-hole results, then move through quantum platforms and instruments. This is an inferred selection, not an official arXiv trending chart; citation and independent-attention evidence for papers this recent is limited, so the ordering is not a popularity ranking.

## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Observation of single top-quark production in the $s$-channel in proton--proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV with the ATLAS detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11465)
- **Abstract:** ATLAS reports its first single-experiment observation of LHC $s$-channel single-top production, using 140 fb$^{-1}$ of 13 TeV data and a graph-neural-network-assisted analysis.[1]
- **Authors:** ATLAS Collaboration[1]
- **arXiv:** `2610.11465v1` (published 2026-10-08 08:15:59 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)[1]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract reports 5.1σ observed significance, a first single-experiment observation, and a measured cross-section; the paper was submitted to Physical Review Letters.[1]
- **Claimed result:** The fitted significance is 5.1σ observed (5.2σ expected); the measured cross-section is 10.8 ± 1.5 (stat.) ± 1.6 (syst.) pb, consistent with the Standard Model prediction.[1]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; ATLAS proton–proton collision data at 13 TeV, corresponding to 140 fb$^{-1}$, analyzed with a graph neural network and likelihood fit.[1]
- **Caveat:** The result is from one experiment’s Run-2 dataset; the reported systematic uncertainty is 1.6 pb.[1]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 8 October 2026[1]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors; Computational & data methods

### 2. [Ringdown analysis of GW250114 with RingCWB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09503)
- **Abstract:** The authors use the RingCWB pipeline to measure the dominant ringdown mode and first overtone of GW250114 and test whether their frequencies and damping times agree with general relativity.[2]
- **Authors:** Achal Kumar, Poulami Dutta Roy, Sergey Klimenko[2]
- **arXiv:** `2610.09503v1` (published 2026-10-07 06:01:39 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`)[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** It applies an independent ringdown-analysis pipeline to an exceptionally high-signal-to-noise black-hole merger and reports quantitative tests of GR; its result is directly comparable with other analyses of the same event.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The inferred fractional deviations for the dominant mode’s frequency and damping time are consistent with zero: 0.004 (+0.022/−0.023) and 0.010 (+0.096/−0.087), respectively, at the quoted 90% intervals.[2]
- **Evidence type:** observational; LIGO gravitational-wave data for GW250114 reconstructed with coherent WaveBurst and analyzed with the RingCWB ringdown model.[2]
- **Caveat:** The paper finds no evidence for additional subdominant modes beyond the first overtone; the constraints depend on the ringdown model and reconstructed waveform, and the overtone parameters are less precise.[2]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026[2]
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Computational & data methods

### 3. [Error-Corrected Memory and Logic on a Heavy-Hex Superconducting-Qubit Processor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11658)
- **Abstract:** The team demonstrates error-corrected memory and measurement-based logical operations on a 156-qubit superconducting processor, using noise-informed decoding and component selection to handle device nonuniformity.[3]
- **Authors:** Campbell K. McLauchlan, Georgia M. Nixon, Julien M. Drouet, Xanda C. Kolesnikow, Seok-Hyung Lee et al.[3]
- **arXiv:** `2610.11658v1` (published 2026-10-08 10:35:25 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`)[3]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A 156-qubit hardware experiment reports quantitative memory and logical-operation improvements and demonstrates lattice surgery, making this a concrete fault-tolerance benchmark rather than a proposal.[3]
- **Claimed result:** Noise-aware strategies improve logical error probabilities by up to 35% for memory and 56% for measurement-based logic; post-selection raises reported Bell-state fidelity above 97%.[3]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; dynamic compass-code memory, measurement-based logic, and lattice surgery on a monolithic 156-superconducting-qubit heavy-hex processor.[3]
- **Caveat:** The largest quoted Bell fidelities rely on pre-selection and additional logical-gap post-selection; they should not be read as unconditional raw-device fidelities.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 8 October 2026[3]
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors

### 4. [A solid-state nuclear clock based on VUV absorption spectroscopy of $^{229}$Th](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10056)
- **Abstract:** The authors demonstrate sustained operation of a thorium-229 nuclear clock in a calcium-fluoride crystal, locking to a narrow vacuum-ultraviolet absorption component and tracking its output against a hydrogen-maser-referenced frequency comb.[4]
- **Authors:** Pengfei Wang, Xu-Fei Yin, Hanlin Wang, Jinming Liu, Qichen Qin et al.[4]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10056v1` (published 2026-10-07 13:27:01 UTC; categories: `physics.atom-ph`)[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A 30-hour clock-operation record, resolved quadrupole components, and a separately demonstrated feedback run on another component provide specific experimental milestones.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The reported fractional frequency instability follows approximately 1.29×10⁻¹¹(τ/s)⁻¹/² and reaches 1.24×10⁻¹³ at 10⁴ s.[4]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; continuous-wave 148.4 nm absorption spectroscopy of $^{229}$Th:CaF$_2$, with a hydrogen-maser-referenced frequency comb.[4]
- **Caveat:** The headline stability figure is an instability measurement at 10⁴ s, not a claim of 10⁻¹³ absolute clock accuracy; the reported continuous record spans 30 hours.[4]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026[4]
- **Themes:** Atomic, molecular & optical physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 5. [Observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz transition $J/\psi\to e^+e^-\eta_c$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09626)
- **Abstract:** Using about 10.087 billion $J/\psi$ decays recorded with BESIII, the collaboration reports the first observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz decay to $e^+e^-\eta_c$ and extracts its form factors.[5]
- **Authors:** BESIII Collaboration (M. Ablikim, M. N. Achasov, P. Adlarson, X. C. Ai, S. C. Akondi et al.)[5]
- **arXiv:** `2610.09626v1` (published 2026-10-07 08:03:09 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)[5]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper reports a first observation based on a large collider dataset, a measured branching-fraction ratio, and a form-factor analysis; it was submitted to Physical Review D.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The relative branching fraction is (0.65 ± 0.02(stat.) ± 0.07(syst.))%; the measured $q^2$-dependent form factors show no significant deviation from theory.[5]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; BESIII data from $e^+e^-$ collisions at BEPCII, comprising (10.087 ± 0.044)×10⁹ $J/\psi$ events.[5]
- **Caveat:** The form-factor comparison finds no significant discrepancy, and the quoted systematic uncertainty exceeds the statistical one.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026[5]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 6. [Wave-Growth-Limited Particle Confinement in a Near-Parallel Interplanetary Shock Observed by Parker Solar Probe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10067)
- **Abstract:** Parker Solar Probe measurements of a near-parallel coronal-mass-ejection shock are used to constrain how self-generated Alfvén waves limit the energies of protons confined near the shock.[6]
- **Authors:** A. Kouloumvakos, I. C. Jebaraj, M. A. Malkov, G. D. Berland, M. Gedalin et al.[6]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10067v1` (published 2026-10-07 13:33:35 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.SR`, `astro-ph.HE`, `physics.space-ph`)[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper cross-lists solar/stellar, high-energy astrophysics, and space physics; it reports an in-situ constraint from Parker Solar Probe and notes acceptance in The Astrophysical Journal Letters.[6]
- **Claimed result:** For the observed 2023 shock at 0.24 AU, the proton folding energy falls from about 6 to 1.5 MeV as the shock approaches, consistent with a wave-growth-limited confinement boundary.[6]
- **Evidence type:** observational; energetic-particle measurements by Parker Solar Probe during a single CME-driven interplanetary shock.[6]
- **Caveat:** The reported constraint comes from one shock event, and the conversion from the observed energy evolution to wave-growth times relies on a quasi-linear marginal-stability interpretation.[6]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 7 October 2026[6]
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Plasma & fluid physics

### 7. [Undetected Photon Spectroscopy in the Long-wave and Far Infrared](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11582)
- **Abstract:** A nonlinear interferometer lets the authors infer long-wave infrared sample spectra from a detected near-infrared signal, extending undetected-photon spectroscopy to 20 μm.[7]
- **Authors:** Jinghan Dong, Jingrui Zhang, Haichen Zhou, Weijie Nie, John G. Rarity et al.[7]
- **arXiv:** `2610.11582v1` (published 2026-10-08 09:33:18 UTC; categories: `physics.optics`, `physics.app-ph`)[7]
- **Evidence for ranking:** It pushes the demonstrated spectral reach beyond the previously reported roughly 10.5 μm range, validates Kapton absorption against independent infrared measurements, and detects SF₆ near 10.55 μm.[7]
- **Claimed result:** The experiment demonstrates broadband Kapton spectroscopy from 7.8 to 20 μm and sulfur-hexafluoride detection near 10.55 μm while detecting only the near-infrared signal.[7]
- **Evidence type:** experimental; a BaGa₂GeSe₆ nonlinear interferometer with silicon detection of the near-infrared signal.[7]
- **Caveat:** The abstract demonstrates selected laboratory samples and gas; broader analyte coverage and field performance are not established there.[7]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 8 October 2026[7]
- **Themes:** Atomic, molecular & optical physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 8. [Imaging ablator-fuel mix of hot spot in inertial confinement fusion via resonant X-ray absorption using an X-ray free electron laser](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11603)
- **Abstract:** The authors propose resonant X-ray absorption imaging with an XFEL to resolve dopant, ionization, opacity, and material-mix structure in inertial-confinement-fusion implosions, supported by a wire-target proof of principle and implosion simulations.[8]
- **Authors:** Lingen Huang, Long Yang, Alejandro Laso Garcia, Oliver S. Humphries, Michal Šmíd et al.[8]
- **arXiv:** `2610.11603v1` (published 2026-10-08 09:48:04 UTC; categories: `physics.plasm-ph`)[8]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The work connects an XFEL diagnostic concept to a proof-of-principle experiment and simulated direct- and indirect-drive implosion cases; it targets measurements useful for benchmarking radiation-hydrodynamics models.[8]
- **Claimed result:** Resonant absorption images of dopants could provide space- and time-resolved constraints on ionization, opacity, and ablator-fuel mix in hot dense plasma.[8]
- **Evidence type:** experimental and computational; resonant XFEL probing of laser-driven coated copper wires, plus atomic and radiation-hydrodynamic simulations with synthetic images for doped ICF shells.[8]
- **Caveat:** The proof-of-principle uses coated wires; imaging of an actual imploding ICF hot spot is proposed as a future application, not reported as an achieved measurement.[8]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 8 October 2026[8]
- **Themes:** Plasma & fluid physics; Instrumentation & detectors

## Trending Research Themes

- **Precision tests built around rare or short-lived signals.** ATLAS isolates a suppressed top-quark production channel; the GW250114 analysis tests ringdown modes; BESIII resolves a rare electromagnetic transition. These are distinct experiments and datasets, not independent confirmations of one result.[1][2][5]
- **Quantum systems moving from components toward sustained operation.** The thorium work reports a multi-hour clock lock, while the heavy-hex study tests encoded memory and logical operations across a 156-qubit chip. The experiments address different platforms and performance measures.[3][4]
- **New diagnostics and spectral access.** Undetected-photon spectroscopy extends long-wave infrared access, while the XFEL proposal targets time-resolved mix measurements in fusion plasmas; both emphasize measurement capability, but only the infrared work reports the target spectroscopy itself.[7][8]
- **Space-based particle measurements as tests of plasma theory.** Parker Solar Probe's observed confinement/escape boundary is used to infer wave growth near one interplanetary shock.[6]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem — black-hole spectroscopy:** The GW250114 analysis is consistent with GR and finds no evidence for modes beyond the first overtone; several subdominant modes are too weak or filtered for a decisive test. **Direction (synthesis):** compare independent pipelines across more high-SNR events and test robustness to ringdown start time and mode assumptions.[2]
- **Open problem — quantum clock operation:** The thorium experiment demonstrates sustained locking and reports an instability curve, but its 30-hour record does not settle longer-term reproducibility or all frequency-shift systematics. **Direction (synthesis):** extend multi-day operation and characterize environmental shifts across crystal sites and components.[4]
- **Open problem — shock confinement:** The Parker Solar Probe interpretation is based on one near-parallel shock. **Direction (synthesis):** test whether the inferred energy-time scaling recurs across shocks with different geometries and precursor conditions.[6]
- **Open problem — fusion-plasma mix imaging:** The ICF diagnostic is demonstrated on a wire target and modeled for implosions, not yet shown on an imploding shell. **Direction (synthesis):** deploy the method on an implosion and compare measured dopant distributions with radiation-hydrodynamic predictions.[8]
- **Open problem — long-wave sensing:** The infrared demonstration covers Kapton and an SF₆ feature; the abstract does not establish performance across a wider set of materials or complex field samples. **Direction (synthesis):** benchmark sensitivity, repeatability, and spectral accuracy across additional analytes and realistic sample conditions.[7]

## Takeaway

The strongest signals this week are measured rather than speculative: a 5.1σ ATLAS observation, a new ringdown analysis of a high-SNR merger, and several instrument or quantum-hardware demonstrations. Much of the remaining excitement is about enabling better measurements; those proposals and single-event inferences need follow-up before they can support broader conclusions.
