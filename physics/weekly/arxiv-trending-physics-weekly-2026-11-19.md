### 2. [Complete quasinormal modes of Type-D black holes. III. Reissner-Nordström spectra from zero to extremal charge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24647)
- **Abstract:** The authors analytically continue gravitational quasinormal-mode solutions across charge for Reissner–Nordström black holes, tracking 42 overtone families at each of angular orders ℓ=2 and 3 from Schwarzschild to extremality.
- **Authors:** Changkai Chen, Xiaohua Zhang, Zhoujian Cao, Jiliang Jing
- **arXiv:** `2611.24647v1` (published 2026-11-18 15:51:49 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Signal score 6.474; Semantic Scholar reports 17 current citations and 1 influential citation. Dividing the current total by 3.443 days since submission gives a rough 4.938 citations/day. This is a short-window proxy, not a measured citation gain.
- **Claimed result:** The authors find 75 of 84 tracked families approach distinct extremal frequencies with finite damping, while nine approach zero frequency with vanishing excitation; they also report extremal modes on an unconventional Riemann sheet.
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical and computational; analytic continuation and numerical tracking of incoming-amplitude zeros, with 14 figures and 6 tables.
- **Caveat:** The computed survey covers ℓ=2,3 and the first 42 overtones per angular order, not the full spectrum at all angular orders; the authors identify continuation to Kerr–Newman as a next step.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-18 batch
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Computational & data methods

### 3. [Generative diffusion models for lattice field theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23399)
- **Abstract:** The paper connects diffusion-model denoising to stochastic quantisation and discusses learning the complexified distribution produced by complex Langevin dynamics in sign-problem systems.
- **Authors:** Gert Aarts, Diaa E. Habibi, Lingxiao Wang, Kai Zhou
- **arXiv:** `2611.23399v1` (published 2026-11-17 19:02:28 UTC; categories: `hep-lat`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Signal score 5.781, based on Semantic Scholar's 17 current citations (0 influential); the crude age-normalized total is 3.944/day over 4.310 days. The arXiv page also lists a RIKEN report identifier.
- **Claimed result:** The authors show that energy-based diffusion models can learn an unnormalised distribution from complex-Langevin data and use it as a weight in standard Markov-chain Monte Carlo; the demonstration is a quartic model with complex action.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; model demonstration using complex Langevin data and score-/energy-based diffusion models.
- **Caveat:** The reported results are for a simple quartic model, not a demonstrated solution across realistic lattice gauge theories with sign problems.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-17 batch
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 4. [A simple solvable $Z_N$ Hamiltonian in any dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20874)
- **Abstract:** The authors couple Baxter–Fendley clock chains on finite transverse graphs and show that open longitudinal boundaries yield a free-parafermion spectrum, with twisted-chain sectors for periodic longitudinal boundaries.
- **Authors:** Yuguan Li, Murray T. Batchelor
- **arXiv:** `2611.20874v1` (published 2026-11-16 10:48:45 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`, `cond-mat.stat-mech`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Signal score 7.015; Semantic Scholar reports 14 current citations (2 influential), corresponding to a rough 2.476 citations/day over 5.653 days. Two cross-lists contribute to the signal score. The paper ranks below the faster citation-momentum entries above it.
- **Claimed result:** An explicit nonlocal unitary transformation decouples chains for open longitudinal boundaries; the authors also construct an example of a three-dimensional chain array perpendicular to a Penrose rhombus tiling.
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical; exact spectral construction for finite-range clock Hamiltonians.
- **Caveat:** The authors leave original-spin correlation functions and graph-dependent ground-state degeneracies, especially for the Ising limit on the Penrose array, for future study.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16 batch
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Condensed matter & materials

## Trending Research Themes

- **Learned and agentic computation moves into physical workflows.** Diffusion models are proposed for learning complex distributions in lattice field theory (Aarts et al.); a coding agent trains optical hardware from measurements (Oguz et al.); RL searches SMEFT operator combinations (Kumar); and automodel packages model discovery across astrophysical and non-physics examples (Virgolin et al.). These are distinct demonstrations, not independent confirmation of one general-purpose method.
- **Exact structure and numerical continuation remain complementary tools.** Li & Batchelor decouple coupled clock chains to obtain spectra, while Chen et al. numerically trace black-hole resonances across charge. Both use mathematical structure to map complicated spectra, but address different systems and do not constitute a shared physical result.
- **The week's cross-field reach is strongest in methods and instruments.** Optical-network training crosses optics and machine learning (Oguz et al.), while SMEFT search crosses particle phenomenology and AI (Kumar); the multi-listing signals corroborate this methodological overlap, not a common experiment.
- **Isolated high-signal results:** The muon chapter (Gorringe) and alloy nanoparticle simulation (Kushnerov) score highly but do not form a recurring subfield trend in this selected set. Their S2 totals must be read alongside the review nature of the former and the alloy paper's 2019 publication note.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated:** Li & Batchelor identify original-spin correlations and graph-dependent ground-state degeneracies, especially in the Ising limit on the Penrose array, as future work. A concrete follow-up is to calculate these observables and compare arrays with matched chain lengths but different transverse connectivity.
- **Author-stated:** Chen et al. propose using the Reissner–Nordström frequencies as initial data for Kerr–Newman continuation. Extending the continuation while tracking branch identity and excitation factors would test how the reported mode families evolve with rotation.
- **Evidence-tied synthesis:** Aarts et al. demonstrate diffusion-model learning only in a simple quartic complex-action model. Testing the learned distribution against controlled lattice systems with known answers, then more realistic sign-problem theories, would establish where the method remains reliable.
- **Evidence-tied synthesis:** Oguz et al. report optical-hardware training on classification and image encoding; replications across devices, optical architectures, and task types could test calibration burden and robustness beyond the demonstrated setup.
- **Evidence-tied synthesis:** Kumar's improved SMEFT fits are hypotheses in a defined observable set, not discoveries. Re-running the search under alternative uncertainty treatments and held-out observables would test stability and reduce benchmark-selection dependence.
