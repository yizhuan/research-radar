Window: 2026-09-30 through 2026-10-30 UTC. This month's notable recent papers cluster around new extremal-combinatorics conjecture claims, alongside computer-certified bounds and formalized proofs. ## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Proof of the Erdős-Szemerédi sunflower conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26686)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove that for every k ≥ 3 and n ≥ k−1, a family of subsets of [n] containing no k-sunflower has size at most 2^((1−1/(16k³))n), establishing an exponential saving below 2^n.
- **Authors:** Matija Bucić, Fankang He, Hyunwoo Lee
- **arXiv:** `2610.26686v1` (published 2026-10-19T16:32:32Z; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** An IBS Discrete Mathematics Seminar devoted a talk to the result on 28 October; the claimed resolution also has broad extremal-combinatorics and polynomial-method relevance. [Seminar notice](https://dimag.ibs.re.kr/event/2026-10-28/)
- **Claimed result:** Theorem 1 in the paper gives the stated explicit bound for every k ≥ 3; its vector-sunflower bound is transferred to set families by a probabilistic block reduction.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Families are arbitrary subsets of the power set of an n-element ground set; the theorem's nonvacuous stated range is n ≥ k−1. It concerns the Erdős–Szemerédi problem, not the distinct uniform sunflower conjecture.
- **Caveat:** This is an arXiv preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication. The authors report a Lean verification, but this report has not independently checked that formalization or the mathematical proof.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-19 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 2. [Erdős-Hajnal for excluding paths](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24887)
- **Abstract:** The author proves that every path has the Erdős–Hajnal property, using a new Gyárfás-path argument with bookkeeping blockades and splitting paths.
- **Authors:** Tung H. Nguyen
- **arXiv:** `2610.24887v2` (published 2026-10-17T21:55:00Z; revised 2026-10-20T15:57:04Z; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The preprint received a substantive October 20 revision, and its own introduction records independent parallel work on the path case by a Google Research/DeepMind team; the author says the teams had not seen each other's manuscripts at submission. This is a concrete cross-check of active attention, not a citation-count signal.
- **Claimed result:** Theorem 1.2 establishes the Erdős–Hajnal property for every finite path P_t; Theorem 1.3 also covers two families of subdivided-claw graphs.
- **Assumptions and setting:** For each fixed t, every finite simple graph with no induced P_t contains a clique or stable set of size at least |G|^c_t for some c_t > 0. The full conjecture for arbitrary forbidden graphs is not claimed.
- **Caveat:** The general Erdős–Hajnal conjecture remains open beyond the graph families treated here; this is one author's preprint and the proof has not been independently assessed here.
- **Announcement type:** revision, 2026-10-20 batch (first submitted 2026-10-17)
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 3. [The Exact Overlaps Conjecture for Self-Similar Measures on the Real Line](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10511)
- **Abstract:** For finitely supported probability measures on contracting similarities of the real line, the authors claim the exact-overlaps dimension formula and a generalized entropy–Lyapunov formula, using a new entropy inequality for sums of independent random variables.
- **Authors:** Samuel Kittle, Constantin Kogler
- **arXiv:** `2610.10511v1` (published 2026-10-07T17:52:17Z; categories: `math.DS`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper spans dynamical systems and probability, and its introduction documents a separately announced result on the same formula in an October mathematics manuscript release, while distinguishing the present paper's stronger explicit entropy inequality. The cross-list and the authors' direct account are checkable signals of a live result, not proof of correctness.
- **Claimed result:** Theorem 1.1 states dim ν = min{1, h_μ/|χ_μ|} for the self-similar measure generated by a finitely supported probability measure on contracting similarities of R; Corollary 1.2 gives the corresponding set-dimension formula when there are no exact overlaps.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The proved theorem is one-dimensional, with finitely many contracting similarities and finite support. The paper explicitly cautions that its entropy inequality relies on abelian addition and does not settle the general higher-dimensional case.
- **Caveat:** The dimension formula is an author-attributed preprint claim; the paper itself identifies higher-dimensional settings beyond its stated extension as unresolved.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-07 batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Geometry & topology

### 4. [Certified Fourier rounding for the real Grothendieck inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24417)
- **Abstract:** The author gives a computer-assisted proof that the real Grothendieck constant is strictly below 1.77987164, certifying a five-variable rounding construction with interval arithmetic, a Fourier coefficient calculation, and an explicit tail bound.
- **Authors:** Bo Peng
- **arXiv:** `2610.24417v1` (published 2026-10-17T03:30:33Z; categories: `math.FA`, `math.NA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It supplies source code and an exact certificate as ancillary files, cross-lists functional analysis with numerical analysis, and tightens the upper endpoint in another October preprint on the same constant (`2610.10477`).
- **Claimed result:** The paper certifies K_G^R < 1.77987164 for all finite real matrices and arbitrary vector dimensions; its finite coefficient computation runs through degree 403 and the remaining coefficient sum is bounded analytically.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The result is for the real Grothendieck constant and the standard finite-matrix/vector formulation; the numerical inequalities are claimed with outward interval bounds and rational final comparisons.
- **Caveat:** This is a certified upper bound, not an exact evaluation of the constant or a matching lower bound; the certificate is reproducible, but has not been independently audited here.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-17 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Numerical analysis & computation

### 5. [Primal/Dual Method for the Grothendieck Constant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10477)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove 1.773 ≤ K_G ≤ 1.7799 for the real Grothendieck constant, using a primal/dual search based on Hermite projection games and Krivine rounding followed by rigorous certification.
- **Authors:** Steven Heilman, Chris Jones, Giulio Malavolta
- **arXiv:** `2610.10477v1` (published 2026-10-07T17:37:15Z; categories: `cs.CC`, `math.FA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete interval bound on a central constant in functional analysis and computational complexity, and a distinct October preprint subsequently reports a slightly sharper certified upper endpoint. The cross-list and paired development support inclusion, not a popularity claim.
- **Claimed result:** The abstract gives the rigorous interval 1.773 ≤ K_G ≤ 1.7799; the heuristic estimate K_G ≈ 1.779 is not itself a theorem.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The bound concerns the real Grothendieck constant; the paper's primal/dual search uses AI tools, with numerical certificates asserted to be rigorous.
- **Caveat:** The estimate is not an exact value, and the authors distinguish their heuristic point estimate from their certified bounds. The large certificate set remains a matter for independent checking.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-07 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Numerical analysis & computation

### 6. [A Proof of the Koumandos--Ruscheweyh Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02285)
- **Abstract:** The author claims the sharp subordination conjecture for every 0 < ρ ≤ 1 and every degree n, with the parameter μ bounded by an explicitly defined optimal threshold ν(ρ); the remaining finite interval inequalities are certified by ball arithmetic.
- **Authors:** Yicen Ma
- **arXiv:** `2610.02285v2` (published 2026-10-01T14:53:31Z; revised 2026-10-05T14:33:05Z; categories: `math.CV`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The October 5 revision adds stated sharp consequences for starlike functions and Gegenbauer sections; the paper also supplies rational interval covers, source code, and alternative-formula verifiers for the computer-assisted part.
- **Claimed result:** For each 0 < ρ ≤ 1, n ≥ 0, 0 < μ ≤ ν(ρ), and z in the unit disk, (1−z)^ρ s_n^μ(z) is subordinate to ((1+z)/(1−z))^ρ; the threshold is claimed optimal.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Here s_n^μ(z) = Σ_{k=0}^n (μ)_k z^k/k!, and ν(ρ) is the unique root in (0,1] of the integral specified in the abstract. The domain is the open unit disk.
- **Caveat:** The finite numerical certification is part of the proof and depends on the supplied code and interval covers; this report has not rerun or independently checked those certificates.
- **Announcement type:** revision, 2026-10-05 batch (first submitted 2026-10-01)
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Numerical analysis & computation

### 7. [Optimal Reduced Representatives of Quadratic APN Permutations in Odd Dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21169)
- **Abstract:** For every odd n ≥ 5, the author claims that each quadratic APN permutation of F_2^n can be linearly transformed so its reduced polynomial representative has a zero-free derivative and two-element nonempty fibres, yielding permutation-polynomial lifts over the corresponding Galois rings.
- **Authors:** Hiroki Fukui
- **arXiv:** `2610.21169v1` (published 2026-10-15T03:26:54Z; categories: `math.NT`, `cs.CR`, `cs.IT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It cross-lists number theory, cryptography, and information theory, includes code, finite certificates and a Lean 4 formalization, and states it has been submitted to IEEE Transactions on Information Theory (not accepted status).
- **Claimed result:** For odd n ≥ 9 the existence argument is formalized in Lean; n=5,7 are handled by classifications and finite certificates. The paper says the property fails for n=3 and that no such quadratic APN permutations exist for even n.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The theorem concerns quadratic almost-perfect-nonlinear permutations over binary fields F_2^n, with the stated odd-dimension restrictions and invertible binary-linear pre- and post-compositions.
- **Caveat:** The construction is dimension-restricted and does not give the asserted property in even dimensions or at n=3; the journal submission is not evidence of acceptance.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-15 batch
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 8. [A short open space curve of unit width](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19585)
- **Abstract:** The authors construct a three-dimensional unit-width curve of length about 3.905124162243593 and prove it is a strict local minimum within their candidate family, using analytic estimates, interval arithmetic and a Lean formalization, while conjecturing it is globally minimizing.
- **Authors:** Tinko Sebastian Bartels, Dennis Kevin Choy, Ruixiang Wu, Tongxin Li
- **arXiv:** `2610.19585v1` (published 2026-10-14T04:57:13Z; categories: `math.MG`, `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper combines metric and differential geometry with explicit numerical certification and machine-checkable proof materials; it corrects a previously conjectured candidate and provides the programs and formalization as supplements.
- **Claimed result:** It identifies a strict local minimum of length 3.905124162243593… inside a constructed family of rectifiable continuous curves of unit width in R^3, and proves the candidate has unit width in every direction.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The minimizing claim is local to the specified candidate family; the unit-width condition is certified over all directions using analytic estimates plus interval arithmetic on a finite cover.
- **Caveat:** Global minimality is explicitly conjectured, not proved; the local result does not exclude a shorter curve outside the candidate family.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-14 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Numerical analysis & computation

## Trending Research Themes

- **Extremal combinatorics and forbidden configurations:** The sunflower result gives an exponential saving for k-sunflower-free set families, while Nguyen upgrades the Erdős–Hajnal property from a near-bound to a polynomial-size homogeneous set for every forbidden path. Both are substantial claims in math.CO, but their objects and techniques differ; the pair does not establish a general field-wide shift. [Sunflower paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26686), [path paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24887).
- **Entropy as a bridge from probability to dimension:** Kittle–Kogler connect entropy loss under addition to dimensions of one-dimensional self-similar measures, cross-listing math.DS and math.PR. The stated reliance on abelian addition marks a concrete boundary for extending this method to higher dimensions. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10511).
- **Sharper bounds through auditable computation:** Two October papers narrow the real Grothendieck constant's interval, while Ma's complex-analysis result and the unit-width curve use interval arithmetic for finite ranges or direction sets. Across these papers, computation is presented as a proof component with certificates/code, not as an unverified numerical experiment. [2610.10477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10477), [2610.24417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24417), [2610.02285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02285), [2610.19585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19585).
- **Formal verification across distinct settings:** Lean artifacts or formalizations are reported for the APN construction and unit-width curve, and the sunflower authors report a Lean check. This is a shared verification practice, not a shared mathematical method; the formal statements and their correspondence to prose claims still matter. [2610.21169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21169), [2610.19585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19585), [2610.26686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26686).

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated: the uniform sunflower conjecture remains distinct.** The sunflower paper concerns arbitrary families of subsets on a fixed ground set; it does not establish the Erdős–Rado bound for families of fixed-size sets. A follow-up is to determine whether the new vector/polynomial method can strengthen bounds in that uniform setting. [2610.26686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26686); [scope distinction](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Erdos-SzemerediSunflowerConjecture.html).
- **Author-stated: the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture for arbitrary forbidden graphs remains open.** The paper proves all paths and specified subdivided-claw families. One direction is to identify other forbidden-graph classes where the bookkeeping-blockade and splitting-path argument can deliver a polynomial exponent. [2610.24887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24887).
- **Author-stated limitation: higher-dimensional exact overlaps.** Kittle–Kogler explain that their entropy argument fundamentally uses abelian addition and does not make significant progress on the unrestricted higher-dimensional problem. A concrete test is to extend the variance-energy inequality to similarity systems whose rotation groups satisfy their stated virtually-solvable condition, then isolate what fails beyond it. [2610.10511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10511).
- **Author-stated: the exact real Grothendieck constant is still not determined.** The two selected papers give a lower/upper interval and a certified upper-bound refinement, not an exact value. A useful next comparison is to seek a matching lower construction or further certify improvements using independently checkable certificates. [2610.10477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10477), [2610.24417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24417).
- **Author-stated: global minimality of the unit-width curve is conjectural.** The result proves a local minimum only within its candidate family. Search for competitors outside that family, or prove a global lower bound matching its length, would directly test the conjecture. [2610.19585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19585).
