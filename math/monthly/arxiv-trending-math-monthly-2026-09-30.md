## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Proof of the Kahn Saks Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30895)
- **Abstract:** For finite posets, the paper claims that sufficiently large width forces the balancing coefficient of a pair of elements to approach one half, resolving the Kahn–Saks conjecture.[2]
- **Authors:** Max Aires.[2]
- **arXiv:** `2609.30895v1` (published 2026-09-25 07:04 UTC; categories: `math.CO`, `cs.DM`)[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Semantic Scholar citation and influential-citation counts were unavailable (API returned HTTP 429); no independent discussion was verified. Placed first on the strength and combinatorics/discrete-math cross-listing of the stated conjecture-resolution claim, not on measured attention.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The author states that for every target closeness to 1/2, sufficiently large poset width guarantees a pair whose ordering probabilities in a uniformly random linear extension are that balanced; a stronger structural dichotomy is also claimed.[2]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite n-element posets; width is the maximum size of an antichain, and probabilities are under the uniform distribution on linear extensions.[2]
- **Caveat:** This is a 25-page arXiv preprint; I checked the abstract and metadata, not the proof. The abstract does not state the quantitative width threshold.[2]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, September 25, 2026.[2]
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Probability & statistics.[2]

### 2. [The Zygmund conjecture and Rey's exponential integrability conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23895)
- **Abstract:** The author claims a weak `L(log L)^(m-2)` bound for a maximal operator over products of cubes in all dimensions `m ≥ 3`, by proving Rey's exponential-integrability conjecture for sparse incomparable dyadic-rectangle families.[4]
- **Authors:** Henri Martikainen.[4]
- **arXiv:** `2609.23895v2` (first submitted 2026-09-20 22:01 UTC; revised 2026-09-27 17:35 UTC; categories: `math.CA`)[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Semantic Scholar counts unavailable (HTTP 429); no independent attention signal verified. Ranked highly for an all-parameter claim joining maximal-function estimates and exponential integrability; the September revision adds a sharpness example and links a Lean formalization, per the arXiv comment.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The preprint claims the Zygmund estimate for every `m ≥ 3` and every positive coordinatewise nondecreasing side-length function; it says the estimate is new for `m > 3`.[4]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Product maximal operators built from `m`-parameter rectangles with side lengths `(s₁,…,sₘ₋₁, φ(s₁,…,sₘ₋₁))`, where `φ` is positive and coordinatewise nondecreasing.[4]
- **Caveat:** The paper is a preprint; I did not assess its proof or inspect the linked formalization. The claimed estimate is specifically weak-type, not a strong `L(log L)^(m-2)` bound.[4]
- **Announcement type:** revision (v2), September 27, 2026; first submitted September 20.[4]
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Probability & statistics.[4]

### 3. [The Tate conjecture for powers of abelian fourfolds and the Hodge conjecture for powers of CM fourfolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27916)
- **Abstract:** The author claims the Tate conjecture in every codimension for every power of an abelian variety of dimension at most four over a finite field, and the Hodge conjecture for every power of a complex CM abelian fourfold.[3]
- **Authors:** Ningyi Li.[3]
- **arXiv:** `2609.27916v1` (published 2026-09-10 12:15 UTC; categories: `math.AG`, `math.NT`)[3]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Semantic Scholar counts unavailable (HTTP 429); no independent expert discussion verified. The ranking reflects the breadth of the two major conjecture claims and the algebraic-geometry/number-theory cross-listing, not observed readership.[3]
- **Claimed result:** In addition to those Tate and Hodge claims, the author says the finite-field results imply standard conjecture D and expected zeta-function pole orders for the stated powers.[3]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Tate: powers of abelian varieties of dimension at most four over finite fields. Hodge: powers of complex CM abelian fourfolds.[3]
- **Caveat:** These are author-stated results in a 37-page preprint; I inspected the abstract and metadata, not the proof. The stated scope does not include abelian varieties of dimension greater than four.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, September 10, 2026.[3]
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Geometry & topology.[3]

### 4. [AI's solution of Conjecture 9.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33644)
- **Abstract:** Park and Talagrand present an AI-generated proof of a strengthened version of a conjecture about spread probability measures and hereditary subsets of the discrete cube.[5]
- **Authors:** Jinyoung Park, Michel Talagrand.[5]
- **arXiv:** `2609.33644v1` (published 2026-09-27 15:10 UTC; categories: `math.CO`, `math.PR`)[5]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Semantic Scholar counts unavailable (HTTP 429); no independent discussion verified. Included for its explicit cross-listing and the unusual, clearly disclosed AI-generated proof claim; placement is not a popularity measure.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The paper states an integral logarithmic inequality relating the product-measure mass of a nonempty hereditary family to average projection measures, assuming the averaging measure is spread at the parameter specified in Theorem 2.1.[5]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Families `A ⊆ {0,1}^N` are nonempty and hereditary; the theorem uses product measure `μ_p`, a parameter `0 < α < 1`, and a `ρ`-spread measure with `ρ = p(1−α)/((1−p)α)`.[5]
- **Caveat:** The authors say the proofs were generated entirely by GPT-6 Astra, disclaim credit, and say the note is not intended for journal publication. The paper explicitly says the broader conjectures discussed in its introduction remain open; this is not a resolution of those broader conjectures. I did not independently verify the proof.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, September 27, 2026.[5]
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Probability & statistics; Machine learning theory.[5]

### 5. [The irrationality measure of π is at most 7.101862832357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11276)
- **Abstract:** Bai claims a new upper bound `μ(π) < 7.101862832357`, improving the cited Zeilberger–Zudilin upper bound of `7.103205334137…` through a two-parameter specialization of their integral method.[6]
- **Authors:** Yufei Bai.[6]
- **arXiv:** `2609.11276v2` (first submitted 2026-09-10 09:08 UTC; revised 2026-09-11 04:09 UTC; categories: `math.NT`)[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Semantic Scholar counts unavailable (HTTP 429); no independent expert discussion verified. Included for the specific numerical improvement and number-theoretic interest, not for demonstrated attention.[6]
- **Claimed result:** The author derives integer linear forms in `1` and `π` yielding the stated upper bound on the irrationality measure.[6]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The result is an upper bound from a particular admissible parameter choice in a Zeilberger–Zudilin integral construction; it does not determine the exact irrationality measure of `π`.[6]
- **Caveat:** The author notes that the chosen parameter point is a strict local minimizer of the auxiliary bound function in its arithmetic chamber, not a global optimizer among all constructions. Proof not independently assessed.[6]
- **Announcement type:** revision (v2), September 11, 2026; first submitted September 10.[6]
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory.[6]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated open problem:** Park and Talagrand explicitly distinguish their result from the broader conjectures in the paper's introduction, which they say remain open; the note describes the remaining gap in one route as of order `log log(100/p)`. A next step would be to determine whether the new inequality can reduce that gap or whether additional hypotheses are unavoidable.[5]
- **Research direction (synthesis):** The Zygmund result's stated domain leaves natural boundary tests—such as relaxing coordinatewise monotonicity of `φ` or identifying sharp constants and endpoint behavior. These are proposed follow-ups, not open problems asserted by the author.[4]
- **Research direction (synthesis):** For the π bound, investigate whether other admissible parameter regions or constructions can improve on the reported local optimum; the paper expressly does not claim global optimality.[6]
- **Research direction (synthesis):** The Kahn–Saks abstract gives an asymptotic width guarantee but no threshold; extracting explicit width-versus-balance bounds would make the result more quantitative. The abstract does not say whether such bounds are available in the full paper.[2]
