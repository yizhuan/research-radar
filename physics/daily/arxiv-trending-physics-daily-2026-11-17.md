## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Independent interferometric observable confirms black hole shadow in M87*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20418)
- **Abstract:** The authors apply a closure-invariant imaging method to Event Horizon Telescope observations of M87* and recover a consistent compact, ring-like source.
- **Authors:** Samuel Lai, Nithyanandan Thyagarajan, O. Ivy Wong, Foivos Diakogiannis
- **arXiv:** `2611.20418v1` (published 2026-11-16T02:26:43Z; categories: `astro-ph.IM`, `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It is cross-listed across three astrophysics categories, carries a Nature journal reference and DOI, and received independent coverage from [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11241-9) and [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-independent-technique-validates-landmark-m87.html).
- **Claimed result:** From public 2017, 2018, and 2021 EHT data, the authors report a ring diameter of 42.2 ± 1.5 microarcseconds and infer a mass of 6.5 ± 0.5 billion solar masses under their nominal scaling calibration.
- **Evidence type:** observational; reanalysis of 1.3-mm Event Horizon Telescope very-long-baseline interferometry data using closure invariants, generative image reconstruction, and Bayesian geometric modelling.
- **Caveat:** Sparse VLBI coverage leaves some lower-quality epochs less clear; the black-hole mass estimate depends on a calibration factor and its theoretical uncertainty, not on ring diameter alone.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 17 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [Resolving and Suppressing Temporally Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21578)
- **Abstract:** Using a 105-qubit Willow processor, the authors probe time-correlated surface-code errors, identify leakage into a higher transmon state, and test circuit and decoder strategies to suppress it.
- **Authors:** Alec Eickbusch, Nick Noll, Michael Newman, Matt McEwen, Leigh S. Martin, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.21578v1` (published 2026-11-16T18:49:49Z; categories: `quant-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The new hardware result has drawn independent technology-media coverage ([Striding Tech](https://stridingtech.com/archives/quantum-leap-new-circuit-design-slashes-qubit-errors-by-two-orders-of-magnitude/)); the study also identifies a physical source of temporal errors rather than reporting only a decoder benchmark.
- **Claimed result:** The authors measure a time-like suppression factor of 1.47 ± 0.25 and report that a “walking” stability circuit reaches a logical-error floor ≤3.20×10⁻⁸ at 60 cycles, about two orders below the standard construction; an offline adaptive stopping analysis reduces spacetime logical error by 38%.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; surface-code stability and memory experiments on a 105-qubit Willow superconducting processor, with an offline decoder analysis.
- **Caveat:** The 60-cycle floor is limited by ionizing-radiation error bursts, and the walking circuit has higher logical error at low cycle counts; the result is tied to this device and implementation.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 17 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Experimental Evidence of Elastic Instability in Viscoelastic Kolmogorov Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21087)
- **Abstract:** Experiments and Oldroyd-B simulations report arrow-shaped velocity structures and chaotic fluctuations consistent with a center-mode elastic instability in confined, quasi-two-dimensional viscoelastic Kolmogorov flow.
- **Authors:** Christophe Brouzet, Mayank Chopra, Dario Vincenzi, Jason R. Picardo, Atul Varshney
- **arXiv:** `2611.21087v1` (published 2026-11-16T13:36:23Z; categories: `physics.flu-dyn`, `cond-mat.soft`, `nlin.CD`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper is published in Physical Review Letters with a DOI and cross-listed across fluid dynamics, soft matter, and nonlinear science; it combines direct measurements with simulations of the proposed mechanism.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report a purely elastic transition below the Newtonian-flow instability threshold, with arrow-shaped structures and chaotic fluctuations, and attribute it to the center-mode mechanism.
- **Evidence type:** experimental and computational; electromagnetic forcing and particle-image-velocimetry measurements compared with two-dimensional Oldroyd-B simulations.
- **Caveat:** The simulated onset differs from experiment (critical Weissenberg numbers are about 10 versus about 3); the model is two-dimensional and uses one polymer relaxation time, unlike the polydisperse experimental solution.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 17 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Plasma & fluid physics; Biophysics & soft matter; Computational & data methods

### 4. [EP241206a: A Sub-kilosecond X-Ray Flare Possibly from an Active Galactic Nucleus with a Flux Increase over 3 Orders of Magnitude](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21369)
- **Abstract:** Einstein Probe detected a roughly 500-second soft X-ray flare positionally coincident with a spectroscopically confirmed AGN at redshift 0.62, but the physical association and origin remain uncertain.
- **Authors:** Chen-Yu Wang, Tinggui Wang, Hua Feng, Wenxiong Li, Hucheng Ding, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.21369v1` (published 2026-11-16T16:35:44Z; categories: `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper reports publication in The Astrophysical Journal and has a DOI; the transient also has an [Einstein Probe GCN alert](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/38457) and subsequent optical follow-up ([GCN 38480](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/38480)).
- **Claimed result:** The authors estimate a peak-to-persistent X-ray luminosity contrast above three orders of magnitude and favor a relativistic-jet explanation, while retaining alternative scenarios.
- **Evidence type:** observational; Einstein Probe Wide-field X-ray Telescope detection, optical spectroscopy, and archival/follow-up observations.
- **Caveat:** Association with the AGN is not firm; the authors report a trial-corrected chance-coincidence probability of 4.9×10⁻³ for the relevant counterpart class, and the proposed emission mechanisms remain interpretations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 17 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 5. [Reversal of dynamical backaction and cooling below the quantum backaction limit in Floquet-driven multimode optomechanical resonators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21110)
- **Abstract:** A Floquet-modulated optical drive couples mechanical modes in a multimode resonator, enabling control of dynamical backaction and cooling of a MHz mode below its single-mode quantum-backaction limit.
- **Authors:** Menno H. Jansen, Roel Burgwal, Ewold Verhagen
- **arXiv:** `2611.21110v1` (published 2026-11-16T13:58:22Z; categories: `quant-ph`, `cond-mat.mes-hall`, `physics.optics`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The work has a linked [Zenodo data-and-code release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23734788), a quantitative experimental result, and cross-listing across quantum physics, mesoscopic physics, and optics.
- **Claimed result:** The authors cool a MHz mode to 47 ± 13 phonons—about half its single-mode quantum-backaction limit—and demonstrate that modulation frequency can switch cooling to amplification at fixed laser detuning.
- **Evidence type:** experimental and theoretical; multimode optomechanical resonator with a Floquet-modulated optical drive, supported by theory and a data/code release.
- **Caveat:** The reported performance is demonstrated in a particular resonator and mode configuration; broader scalability across devices and operating regimes is not established.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 17 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Atomic, molecular & optical physics; Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors

## Trending Research Themes

- **Mechanism-focused tests across instruments and platforms:** M87* imaging uses closure invariants to reduce calibration dependence; the Einstein Probe flare has optical follow-up; the viscoelastic-flow experiment compares measured structures against a proposed instability; and the QEC study identifies leakage and radiation-related error pathways. These are distinct systems and datasets, not independent confirmations of one result ([M87*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20418), [EP241206a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21369), [viscoelastic flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21087), [QEC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21578)).
- **Control of coupled modes and errors:** The QEC and optomechanics papers both manipulate coupled degrees of freedom to change system-level performance, but one concerns logical errors in a superconducting processor and the other mechanical damping in a resonator ([2611.21578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21578), [2611.21110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21110)).
- **Astrophysical transients and image inference are two isolated observational signals:** the X-ray flare's source association remains uncertain, while the M87* result is a reanalysis of EHT observations that has journal publication and external coverage; neither is a new telescope detection in this batch ([2611.21369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21369), [2611.20418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20418)).

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem (authors’ result):** M87* reconstruction remains affected by sparse VLBI coverage, and converting ring diameter to black-hole mass requires a scaling calibration. **Research direction (synthesis):** test closure-invariant reconstructions on new observing epochs and independently calibrated synthetic datasets, separating morphology from mass-calibration uncertainty ([2611.20418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20418)).
- **Open problem (authors’ result):** The QEC study finds that ionizing-radiation bursts set a residual logical-error floor after third-level leakage is mitigated. **Research direction (synthesis):** extend stability runs, improve multi-level leakage removal and burst-aware mitigation, then test on additional processors and larger code distances ([2611.21578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21578)).
- **Open problem (authors’ result):** The viscoelastic-flow simulations reproduce qualitative structures but predict a higher instability onset than the experiment; the model uses a single relaxation time and two-dimensional geometry. **Research direction (synthesis):** measure the polymer relaxation-time distribution and compare matched three-dimensional simulations with controlled confinement and forcing ([2611.21087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21087)).
- **Open problem (authors’ result):** The EP241206a counterpart association and flare mechanism remain uncertain. **Research direction (synthesis):** coordinated X-ray, optical, and radio follow-up of future similar flares could test association and distinguish jet from AGN-disk scenarios ([2611.21369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21369)).
- **Open problem (authors’ result):** The optomechanical cooling and backaction reversal are shown for one resonator configuration. **Research direction (synthesis):** use the released data and code to map sensitivity to auxiliary-mode occupancy and drive parameters, then test whether the approach generalizes across resonators ([2611.21110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21110)).

## Takeaway

The strongest signals in this batch are the Nature-published M87* reconstruction and several experimentally grounded studies that isolate mechanisms rather than merely reporting performance numbers. The remaining uncertainties are substantial: M87* reuses sparse observations, the X-ray transient's counterpart is not secure, and the laboratory findings still need validation across models or devices.
