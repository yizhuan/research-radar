- **Abstract:** A joint fit to public atmospheric-neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore, and KM3NeT/ORCA favors normal over inverted neutrino mass ordering.
- **Authors:** Miaochen Jin, Carlos A. Argüelles, Pablo Fernández-Menéndez, Iván Martínez-Soler
- **arXiv:** `2610.12208v1` (published 2026-10-08 15:58:49 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)

- **Claimed result:** The analysis prefers normal ordering with Δχ²=11.0 and excludes inverted ordering at 99.95% confidence (3.3σ) after pseudoexperiment calibration.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; simultaneous fit to publicly available atmospheric-neutrino samples from three detector programs.
- **Caveat:** The preference is below discovery-level significance and remains conditional on the data selections, detector-response models, and treatment of shared systematics.

## Trending Research Themes

- **Precision instrumentation is advancing the reach of measurements:** the Timepix4 beam test pushes vertex timing to 54 ps (paper 6), while the Princeton Axion Search validates a large-volume high-Q cavity design (paper 8); the qubit study (paper 5) targets resilience to optical perturbations.
- **Better analysis is extracting more from existing or difficult data:** the combined atmospheric-neutrino fit merges three detector programs (paper 2), Parkes reprocessing recovers hidden pulsars (paper 4), and CAPERS quantifies how selection priors affect JWST high-redshift confirmations (paper 3). These are distinct datasets, not independent confirmations of one result.
- **High-significance claims remain calibrated and conditional:** ATLAS reports a 5.1σ observation of a specific Standard Model process (paper 1); the neutrino mass-ordering preference is 3.3σ (paper 2), and the GW echo analysis is a null constraint rather than a detection (paper 7).

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Neutrino ordering:** the combined fit favors normal ordering at 3.3σ, not discovery significance (paper 2). More data and independent combined fits with scrutinized cross-experiment systematics could test the preference.
- **Very early galaxies:** the UV-luminosity-function prior improves candidate confirmation efficiency in the CAPERS sample, but field-to-field and selection effects remain relevant (paper 3). Apply the approach to independent JWST fields and propagate completeness uncertainties into luminosity functions.
- **Quantum hardware under light exposure:** relaxation improves, but dephasing and readout/state-preparation errors rise with optical power (paper 5). Separate these error channels and test whether the protection persists in larger circuits and under realistic optical-link conditions.
- **Detector timing and black-hole tests:** the Timepix4 resolution model retains clock/reference contributions (paper 6), while the GW reflectivity constraint depends on the prompt-wave interpretation and echo model (paper 7). Better timing-reference systems and independent ringdown events with robust waveform comparisons are direct follow-ups.
- **Axion search readiness:** cavity characterization validates design features but is not a particle search result (paper 8). The next evidence step is operation across the target band with end-to-end noise and sensitivity accounting.

## Takeaway

The batch is strongest in concrete experimental reach: a new ATLAS single-top observation, a calibrated but not discovery-level neutrino-ordering preference, improved early-galaxy candidate validation, and advances in detector and quantum-device engineering. The most striking claims still carry clear boundaries—3.3σ is not a neutrino-ordering discovery, a modeled echo limit is not a horizon measurement independent of assumptions, and cavity validation is not an axion detection.
