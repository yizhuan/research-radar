### 1. [Global asymptotic stability of homogeneous polynomial vector fields is undecidable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12434)
- **Abstract:** The authors study whether one can decide global asymptotic stability of the origin for homogeneous polynomial vector fields with rational coefficients and prove undecidability in fixed degree and dimension settings.[2]
- **Authors:** Ivan O. Shevchenko, Jun Liu, Xinzhi Liu.[2]
- **arXiv:** `2610.12434v1` (published 2026-10-08 17:57:15 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.CA`, `math.LO`).[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The preprint states a many-one completeness result and an explicit undecidability corollary for cubic vector fields in a fixed dimension; its dynamical-systems, analysis and logic categories make the result cross-disciplinary. ArXiv News independently highlighted the paper, but that is not evidence of broad readership.[2][3][10]
- **Claimed result:** The authors reduce undecidable integer-equation questions to global stability, and show the problem is many-one complete for every odd degree at least three when dimension is sufficiently large; cubic stability is undecidable in a fixed dimension.[2][3]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Inputs are homogeneous polynomial vector fields over rational coefficients; the theorem concerns the origin. “Sufficiently large” dimension is not a low-dimensional classification.[2][3]
- **Caveat:** The fixed-dimension corollary does not identify the smallest dimension, and the paper is a new preprint rather than peer-reviewed validation.[2][3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 9 October 2026 batch (submitted 8 October 2026).[1][2]
- **Themes:** Dynamical systems; Logic & foundations

### 2. [Characterization and scaling limit of three-dimensional loop-erased random walk and random-walk loop-catchers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12047)
- **Abstract:** The authors show that a Brownian-loop-soup recovery property uniquely characterizes the three-dimensional loop-erased random walk (LERW) scaling limit, resolving the 3D case of a Sapozhnikov–Shiraishi conjecture, and construct 3D Brownian loop-catchers.[11]
- **Authors:** Gefei Cai, Xinyi Li, Daisuke Shiraishi.[11]
- **arXiv:** `2610.12047v1` (published 2026-10-08 14:33:52 UTC; category: `math.PR`).[11]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper claims a conjecture resolution, uniqueness of a continuum law, and an associated scaling-limit construction; these are substantial concrete probability results, not readership metrics.[11][12]
- **Claimed result:** In a bounded connected Lipschitz domain in three dimensions, the intensity-one loop-soup recovery property determines a unique law, which is the scaling-limit law of LERW in the unparametrized Hausdorff trace topology; the authors also construct 3D Brownian loop-catchers.[11][12]
- **Assumptions and setting:** A bounded connected Lipschitz domain in `R^3` containing the origin; the recovery property uses an independent intensity-one Brownian loop soup and Brownian motion stopped at the domain boundary.[12]
- **Caveat:** The characterization and construction are for three dimensions and the stated domain/recovery setup; they do not settle analogous laws in every dimension.[11][12]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 9 October 2026 batch (submitted 8 October 2026).[1][11]
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Geometry & topology

### 3. [The Kakeya problem for codimension-two planes in R^n](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10603)
- **Abstract:** For `n ≥ 4`, the authors prove near-maximal volume bounds for unions of codimension-two plane neighborhoods satisfying convex Wolff-type nonconcentration, with an application to a curvature-restricted four-dimensional two-plane Kakeya problem.[4]
- **Authors:** Chun-Kit Lai, Yixuan Pang, Rahul Sethi, Chenjian Wang.[4]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10603v1` (published 2026-10-07 01:44:06 UTC; category: `math.CA`).[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper gives a quantitative geometric-measure estimate and a named Kakeya-problem consequence, extending the setting from tubes to higher-codimension planes.[4][5]
- **Claimed result:** Under convex Wolff axioms, the union of a sufficiently nonconcentrated, sufficiently dense family of δ-neighborhoods of `(n−2)`-plane segments has almost maximal volume; for `n=4` it resolves Rogers' two-plane problem under specified curvature conditions.[4][5]
- **Assumptions and setting:** `n ≥ 4`, small scale `δ`, convex nonconcentration and a dense shading; the four-dimensional consequence has additional curvature conditions on the direction family.[4][5]
- **Caveat:** This is not an unconditional resolution of all Kakeya conjectures; the theorem's hypotheses include convex Wolff control, and the Rogers consequence is curvature-restricted.[4][5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 9 October 2026 batch (submitted 7 October 2026).[1][4]
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Geometry & topology

### 4. [Sharp Polynomial Upper Bounds for Anticanonical Volumes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10537)
- **Abstract:** For every dimension `n`, the authors prove a polynomial upper bound for anticanonical volumes of ε-lc log Fano varieties with an exponent that is optimal in all dimensions `n ≥ 2`.[13]
- **Authors:** Pinxian Bie, Peien Du, Zhengjie Yu.[13]
- **arXiv:** `2610.10537v1` (published 2026-10-07 17:59:30 UTC; category: `math.AG`).[13]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The result supplies an optimal dimension-dependent exponent and sharpness examples, including toric Fano varieties of Picard number one; its claimed scope covers every positive dimension.[13][14]
- **Claimed result:** The authors show `vol(−K_X) ≤ C_n ε^{−(2^n−n−1)}` for an n-dimensional variety with an ε-lc log Fano boundary; the exponent cannot be decreased for `n ≥ 2`, and the optimal fourfold exponent is 11.[13][14]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Varieties are over `C`; `X` is a normal projective Q-Gorenstein variety admitting an effective boundary making it ε-lc and log Fano. The constant `C_n` depends only on dimension.[14]
- **Caveat:** The exponent is sharp but the leading constant is not optimized; the estimate is parameterized by ε and dimension.[14]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 9 October 2026 batch (submitted 7 October 2026).[1][13]
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Geometry & topology

### 5. [Further bounds in the polynomial Szemerédi theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12032)
- **Abstract:** The authors show that every sufficiently dense subset of `{1,…,N}` contains a nontrivial pattern `(x, x+y, x+2y, x+y^3)`, giving a quantitative bound for a configuration of positive complexity.[6]
- **Authors:** Bartłomiej Bychawski, Bartosz Głowacki, Ivan Spyrydonov.[6]
- **arXiv:** `2610.12032v1` (published 2026-10-08 14:25:51 UTC; categories: `math.NT`, `math.CO`).[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract calls this the first quantitative bound over the integers for a configuration of complexity strictly above zero beyond arithmetic-progression refinements; the result bridges number theory and combinatorics.[6]
- **Claimed result:** For some `c > 0`, any subset of `{1,…,N}` of size much larger than `N / exp((log log log N)^c)` contains the stated pattern.[6]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite subsets of the integers `{1,…,N}` with the displayed density lower bound; the result concerns one specified polynomial configuration.[6]
- **Caveat:** The abstract establishes a bound for this configuration, not a general quantitative solution of the polynomial Szemerédi theorem.[6]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 9 October 2026 batch (submitted 8 October 2026).[1][6]
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Algebra & number theory

- **Claimed result:** The paper adapts induction over scales to obtain strong RSW crossings for rough topological quadrilaterals with unfavorable boundary conditions, then derives standard consequences including quasi-multiplicativity of arm events.[7]
- **Assumptions and setting:** FK-Ising on s-embeddings whose tangential quadrilaterals have uniformly bounded geometry: edge lengths comparable to one and angles bounded below.[7]
- **Caveat:** The estimates are conditional on this geometry assumption; near-critical regular-grid models below the correlation-length scale are an application, not an unrestricted generalization.[7]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 9 October 2026 batch (submitted 8 October 2026).[1][7]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated scope:** The anticanonical-volume exponent is shown to be optimal, but the leading constant is not optimized. Sharpening constants for fixed dimensions while retaining the optimal exponent is a concrete follow-up.[14]
- **Author-stated hypotheses:** The Kakeya estimate assumes convex Wolff nonconcentration, and its Rogers-problem consequence has curvature restrictions. A natural next step is to identify which geometric hypotheses can be weakened while preserving near-maximal volume.[4][5]
- **Author-stated scope:** The LERW characterization is three-dimensional and uses an intensity-one Brownian loop-soup recovery property. Test whether related recovery principles characterize limits in other dimensions or for other loop-erased models; this is a proposed direction, not a result of the paper.[11][12]
- **Synthesis:** For FK-Ising, investigate whether the strong RSW argument survives weaker geometric control of s-embeddings or broader boundary conditions; the current theorem explicitly assumes uniformly bounded geometry.[7]
- **Synthesis:** The stability theorem proves undecidability for cubic fields in a fixed dimension but does not identify the minimum dimension. Determine sharper dimension thresholds or restricted subclasses where decidability might return.[2][3]
