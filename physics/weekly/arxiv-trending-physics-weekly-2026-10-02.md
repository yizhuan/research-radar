# arXiv physics — weekly notable papers (25 Sep–2 Oct 2026)

Six notable new submissions cluster around precision tests of cosmological and particle-physics models, with new neutrino, CMB-polarization, and quantum-Hall measurements. This short agenda: a dark-matter recoil interpretation, a DESI-era gravity fit, a MicroBooNE neutrino benchmark, and three complementary astrophysics/condensed-matter results. The ordering is inferred, not an official arXiv trending chart; these papers are too new for useful citation momentum, and no popularity ranking is claimed.

## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Interpreting the High-Recoil LUX-ZEPLIN Event with Bino-/Singlino-like and Higgsino Dark Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40234)
- **Abstract:** The authors study elastic and inelastic supersymmetric dark-matter models that could produce LZ's reported approximately 248-keV recoil candidate, and show that the models predict different recoil spectra.[1]
- **Authors:** Subhojit Roy, Pedro Schwaller, Carlos E. M. Wagner.[1]
- **arXiv:** `2609.40234v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:31:39 UTC; categories: `hep-ph`, `astro-ph.CO`, `hep-ex`).[1]
- **Evidence for ranking:** Timely interpretation of a specific LZ candidate, cross-listed across phenomenology, cosmology, and experiment; it gives testable alternative recoil spectra. These are scientific-salience signals, not evidence of readership.[1]
- **Claimed result:** The paper presents viable elastic bino/singlino-like and endothermic pseudo-Dirac Higgsino benchmarks and argues their recoil spectra differ away from the candidate.[1]
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical; supersymmetric model calculations and recoil-spectrum comparisons against the LZ event context.[1]
- **Caveat:** LZ's candidate has only 2.6σ global significance after the look-elsewhere effect, so it is not evidence of a dark-matter detection. The paper's interpretation is model-dependent and uses approximations for detector response and the halo/nuclear inputs.[1]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 1 Oct 2026.[1]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Cosmology & gravitation.

### 2. [Is DESI Seeing Dynamical Dark Energy, or a Cosmic Glitch in Gravity?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40176)
- **Abstract:** Combining DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations with CMB datasets, the authors report a 3.3σ preference for a one-parameter reduction in cosmological gravitational coupling relative to Newton's constant, which fits at least as well as a two-parameter evolving-dark-energy model.[2]
- **Authors:** Robin Y. Wen, Lukas Tobias Hergt, Niayesh Afshordi, Douglas Scott.[2]
- **arXiv:** `2609.40176v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:02:51 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.CO`).[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A quantitative fit combines DESI DR2 with Planck, ACT, and SPT CMB data and is checked with added CMB lensing and supernovae; the authors also compare its CMB-only distance prediction with DESI.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The authors infer `G_cosmo/G_N = 0.9920 ± 0.0025` from CMB+DESI and report that the preference remains about 3.3σ with the additional lensing and supernova datasets.[2]
- **Evidence type:** Observational-data analysis; DESI DR2 BAO, Planck-PR4, ACT DR6, SPT-3G, with optional Planck+ACT+SPT lensing and DES supernova data, interpreted using modified-CAMB/PPF calculations.[2]
- **Caveat:** This is a model-dependent statistical preference, not a discovery of modified gravity; the authors use a phenomenological perturbation prescription and note that the fitted effective-fluid representation has limitations.[2]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 1 Oct 2026.[2]
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation.

### 3. [First double-differential cross-section measurements in proton multiplicity and kinematics for mesonless νμ charged-current interactions on argon using the MicroBooNE detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40237)
- **Abstract:** MicroBooNE reports first double-differential measurements of muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with no mesons and at least one proton, using proton multiplicity and kinematics in its full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset.[3]
- **Authors:** MicroBooNE Collaboration: P. Abratenko, D. Andrade Aldana, J. Asaadi, A. Ashkenazi, S. Balasubramanian et al.[3]
- **arXiv:** `2609.40237v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:32:21 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`).[3]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A first-of-its-kind multidifferential measurement with correlated statistical/systematic uncertainties directly tests neutrino–argon interaction models important to liquid-argon experiments.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The collaboration reports that the measurements are sensitive to final-state-interaction modeling and that none of the tested event generators fully describes the data, with notable mismatches in proton multiplicity and subleading-proton distributions.[3]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; the full MicroBooNE Booster Neutrino Beam sample, corresponding to `1.30×10^21` protons on target, analyzed with detector-response corrections and compared with event-generator predictions.[3]
- **Caveat:** The disagreement is a model/data comparison in one experiment and one beam/detector configuration; it does not by itself identify which nuclear-interaction mechanism is missing.[3]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 1 Oct 2026.[3]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors.

### 4. [Improved Constraints on Cosmic Microwave Background Circular Polarization with CLASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40125)
- **Abstract:** Using CLASS measurements at 90, 150, and 220 GHz alongside its earlier 40-GHz data, the team tightens large-scale CMB circular-polarization limits by about a factor of five and reports first limits on the temperature–circular-polarization power spectrum.[4]
- **Authors:** Thomas Essinger-Hileman, Caleigh Ryan, Yunyang Li, Matthew A. Petroff, John W. Appel et al.[4]
- **arXiv:** `2609.40125v1` (published 2026-09-30 16:46:30 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.CO`).[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** New multi-frequency CLASS constraints extend the angular-scale range and introduce a first TV-spectrum limit, with Planck temperature data used in the combined analysis.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The authors find both VV and TV spectra consistent with zero and give 95%-confidence upper limits, including `5.4×10^-3 μK_CMB^2` for large-scale VV and `5.8×10^-1 μK_CMB^2` for TV at `30<ℓ<50`.[4]
- **Evidence type:** Observational; CLASS data at 40, 90, 150, and 220 GHz, with Planck temperature data for the TV constraint.[4]
- **Caveat:** These are upper limits rather than a detection; separating a cosmological circular-polarization signal from atmospheric contributions remains central to interpretation.[4]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 1 Oct 2026.[4]
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Instrumentation & detectors.

### 5. [Quantization through Dissipation and the Optical Quantum Hall Effect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40337)
- **Abstract:** The authors combine terahertz Faraday-rotation measurements and numerical analysis to argue that a faint, low-frequency dissipative contribution from impurity states is needed to recover quantized Hall plateaus from the optical response.[5]
- **Authors:** Zhenisbek Tagay, Ahmed Abouelkomsan, Yugo Onishi, Adbhut Gupta, Loren Pfeiffer et al.[5]
- **arXiv:** `2609.40337v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:58:02 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.mes-hall`).[5]
- **Evidence for ranking:** An experimental-plus-numerical explanation connects finite-frequency optical response to the DC quantum Hall value, with a concrete mechanism rather than a new plateau claim.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The authors report that the cyclotron resonance alone does not yield quantized plateaus under Kramers–Kronig transformation; including the weak low-frequency, topologically enforced dissipative component restores quantization.[5]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental and computational; high-precision terahertz Faraday rotation, supported by numerical analysis.[5]
- **Caveat:** The proposed mechanism relies on resolving a faint low-frequency impurity-state contribution; its quantitative robustness across samples and disorder conditions remains to be established.[5]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 1 Oct 2026.[5]
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials.

### 6. [When Streams Curve Away: a Test of Dark Matter from Extragalactic Stellar Stream Populations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40057)
- **Abstract:** The authors propose a geometry-only statistic of projected stellar-stream tracks to infer halo-shape populations and forecast that future large stream catalogs could distinguish collisionless CDM from self-interacting dark matter.[6]
- **Authors:** Nathaniel Starkman, Jacob Nibauer, Sarah Pearson, Sirui Wu, Lina Necib.[6]
- **arXiv:** `2609.40057v1` (published 2026-09-30 16:20:23 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.CO`).[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The method bridges extragalactic stream morphology and particle dark-matter models, and the authors state a survey-scale forecast reaching up to 5σ discrimination.[6]
- **Claimed result:** A projected stream segment curving away from its host center is proposed as a simple diagnostic; the rate of such convex segments is predicted to depend on halo triaxiality and thus dark-matter model.[6]
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical and computational forecast; geometric diagnostic and forecast for future survey-scale stream catalogs.[6]
- **Caveat:** The stated significance is a forecast, not a present detection or constraint; its value depends on future catalog size, selection effects, and calibration of the relation between stream shapes and halo populations.[6]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 1 Oct 2026.[6]
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Cosmology & gravitation.

## Trending Research Themes

- **Inference from anomalies versus alternative models:** The DESI analysis reframes a mild expansion-history preference as a change in cosmological gravity, while the LZ paper constructs dark-matter explanations for one high-recoil candidate; both are model tests, not discoveries.[1][2]
- **Precision measurements as model discriminators:** MicroBooNE's proton-level neutrino cross sections challenge current interaction generators, CLASS tightens CMB-polarization limits, and the optical Hall study links finite-frequency dissipation to DC quantization. These are independent experiments and systems, not mutual confirmations.[3][4][5]
- **Morphology-to-fundamental-physics bridges:** The stellar-stream proposal turns projected galaxy-scale shapes into a forecasted test of CDM versus SIDM, complementing particle-scale interpretations without claiming an existing observational constraint.[1][6]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Cosmological model dependence (author-stated and synthesis):** DESI's roughly 3.3σ preference depends on the selected data combination and perturbation prescription. Re-test with independent BAO/CMB likelihoods and alternative consistent modified-gravity perturbation treatments before interpreting it as new physics.[2]
- **Neutrino–argon modeling (author-stated):** None of the tested generators fully describes MicroBooNE's multidifferential distributions. Improve final-state-interaction and multinucleon modeling, then test those changes against independent liquid-argon data.[3]
- **The LZ candidate (paper-context caveat):** The global significance is 2.6σ, not a detection. Additional events and a joint recoil-spectrum analysis with detector-response, nuclear-response, and halo uncertainties could distinguish the proposed elastic and inelastic scenarios.[1]
- **CMB circular polarization (synthesis):** CLASS reports limits consistent with zero. Deeper multi-frequency measurements and better control of atmospheric circular polarization are needed to determine whether a cosmological signal is present.[4]
- **Optical Hall mechanism (synthesis):** The explanation hinges on weak low-frequency dissipation from impurity states. Measure the response over lower frequencies and across samples/disorder conditions to test how generally this contribution restores quantization.[5]
- **Stellar-stream forecast (author-stated extension):** The CDM/SIDM discrimination forecast awaits survey-scale catalogs. Validate the convex-segment statistic with realistic mock observations, projection/selection effects, and varied halo populations before translating it into constraints.[6]

## Takeaway

This week's strongest pattern is not a single confirmed breakthrough, but more precise observables being used to discriminate among competing physical models. The most consequential-looking signals remain provisional: DESI's preference is a model-dependent tension, LZ's candidate is below discovery significance, and the stream result is a forecast.
