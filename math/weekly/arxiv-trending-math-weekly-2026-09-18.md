Citation counts and influential citations: unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the query.
- **Claimed result:** The paper says it proves the sharp Euclidean isoperimetric inequality, using a new sharp inequality for constant-mean-curvature hypersurfaces, Jacobi-field estimates, and an isoperimetric-profile argument.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Complete, connected, simply connected Riemannian manifolds with nonpositive sectional curvature; dimensions 3–9. The statement also treats isoperimetric boundaries trapped in geodesic balls, potentially with singularities.
- **Caveat:** This is an arXiv preprint claim; the proof’s geometric inequalities and the passage through singular boundaries require expert verification. The ranking reflects significance if correct, not independent proof validation.

### 2. [Boolean Small-Ball Inequalities for Discrepancy Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20785)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove small-ball inequalities for random-sign matrix series and derive an interlacing-free route to Kadison–Singer together with consequences for Matrix Spencer, Komlós, and other discrepancy problems.
- **Authors:** Emrullah Akbas, Suvrit Sra
- **arXiv:** `2609.20785v1` (published 2026-09-17 17:52 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math.CO`, `math.FA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A cross-subfield result explicitly connecting probability, functional analysis, and discrepancy theory, with several named consequences and an independently indexed arXiv result. Citation counts and influential citations: unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the query.
- **Claimed result:** For Boolean matrix series under stated variance bounds, the paper proves a determinant small-ball lower bound; it says this yields a new interlacing-free proof of the general Kadison–Singer theorem and quick corollaries including Matrix Spencer and Komlós.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Symmetric matrix coefficients with uniformly random signs; the maximum coefficient energy and an additional variance term must be bounded by universal constants. The exact constants and hypotheses are part of the paper’s theorem statements.
- **Caveat:** The abstract compresses several consequences into a headline claim; the equivalence between the small-ball estimate and each discrepancy theorem should be checked in the full 37-page preprint.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-17 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Probability & statistics; Analysis & PDE

### 3. [Universal completeness of exponentials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20805)
- **Abstract:** The authors construct uniformly discrete frequency sets whose exponential systems are complete in every finite-p space over every measurable set below a prescribed measure threshold, and establish sharpness results for related uniqueness conditions.
- **Authors:** Susanna Bertolini, Enric Florit-Simon, Lukas Liehr, Mitchell A. Taylor
- **arXiv:** `2609.20805v1` (published 2026-09-17 17:58 UTC; categories: `math.CA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A sharp-looking universality construction in harmonic analysis, with a publicly linked Lean formalization and prominent arXiv HTML/PDF indexing within hours of submission. Citation counts and influential citations: unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the query.
- **Claimed result:** The paper constructs density-one uniformly discrete sets universal for completeness in `L^p(S)` for every finite `p` and every measurable `S` of measure less than one; it also gives density-`v` integer-frequency constructions, proves a negative result for asymptotically integer sets, and says the Sobolev threshold `alpha > 1/2` is sharp.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Exponential systems on measurable subsets of the real line, with uniformly discrete frequency sets and the stated density and measure constraints; higher-dimensional extensions are claimed. Lean code is linked from the arXiv record.
- **Caveat:** “Verified in Lean” refers to the linked formalization, not to independent mathematical review of the entire preprint; the scope of the formalized results should be checked separately.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-17 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Logic & foundations

### 4. [Regularity of asymptotically axisymmetric solutions to the 3D Navier–Stokes equations with analytic forcing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20803)
- **Abstract:** Under specific anisotropic Type-II bounds and an exactly axisymmetric collapsing core, the authors claim that analytic forcing prevents a putative finite-time singularity in 3D Navier–Stokes.
- **Authors:** Peter Constantin, Mihaela Ignatova, Vlad Vicol
- **arXiv:** `2609.20803v1` (published 2026-09-17 17:57 UTC; categories: `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Addresses a central PDE problem and gives a concrete obstruction to a specified singularity scenario; it was among the most prominent mathematically substantive entries in the current batch. Citation counts and influential citations: unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the query.
- **Claimed result:** The paper says solutions with the stated asymptotic axisymmetry and anisotropic bounds are regular at the proposed singular point when the body force is real analytic; it derives constraints on any force used in a construction of that type.
- **Assumptions and setting:** 3D Navier–Stokes with real-analytic body force, prescribed angular-mean Type-II bounds, and exact axisymmetry in a collapsing core; the proof uses anisotropic blow-up limits and rigidity of ancient solutions.
- **Caveat:** The result rules out a particular mechanism and forcing regularity class; it is not a resolution of global regularity or blow-up for general 3D Navier–Stokes. The abstract references another manuscript whose status should be verified.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-17 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Mathematical physics

### 5. [Generic Uniqueness of Isoperimetric Regions in Arbitrary Dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20790)
- **Abstract:** For a generic smooth Riemannian metric on a closed connected manifold, the authors claim uniqueness of the isoperimetric region at every prescribed non-half volume, with exactly a complementary pair at half volume.
- **Authors:** Gongping Niu
- **arXiv:** `2609.20790v1` (published 2026-09-17 17:53 UTC; categories: `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A broad arbitrary-dimension genericity theorem that explicitly tolerates singular isoperimetric boundaries; it complements the week’s strong geometric-analysis activity. Citation counts and influential citations: unavailable because Semantic Scholar rate-limited the query.
- **Claimed result:** For each volume fraction `s` other than one-half, a generic metric has a unique isoperimetric region; at half volume, a generic metric has exactly two minimizers, a region and its complement. The paper also states fixed-mass genericity consequences.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Closed connected smooth `(n+1)`-manifolds, `n >= 1`, smooth Riemannian metrics, and prescribed volume fractions in `(0,1)`; the variational proof does not require boundary regularity or nondegeneracy of the constrained Jacobi operator.
- **Caveat:** “Generic” is a Baire-category statement, not a claim that every metric has uniqueness. The preprint’s variational argument and treatment of singular boundaries remain to be checked by specialists.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-17 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Analysis & PDE

- **Universality and rigidity in analysis:** Universal exponential completeness uses density/measure thresholds and a sharp Sobolev cutoff; the Navier–Stokes paper uses blow-up limits and rigidity to exclude one singularity mechanism.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Cartan–Hadamard beyond the stated dimensions:** The selected geometry paper leaves open, at minimum, whether its approach can extend beyond dimensions 3–9; this is an informed follow-up direction, not an author-stated claim unless the full paper says so.
- **Exact scope of the discrepancy consequences:** Check which versions of Kadison–Singer, Matrix Spencer, and Komlós follow under which coefficient hypotheses, and whether the small-ball method yields quantitatively new constants. (Akbas–Sra.)
- **Universality in other ambient spaces:** Test whether the exponential-completeness constructions and sharpness phenomena extend cleanly to higher dimensions and to other spectral-gap or irregular-measure settings. (Bertolini et al.)
- **Other Navier–Stokes singularity geometries:** The regularity theorem leaves general, non-analytic forcing and departures from exact axisymmetry outside its stated obstruction; quantifying stability under approximate symmetry is a natural direction. (Constantin, Ignatova, Vicol.)
- **Beyond generic metrics:** Determine effective or geometric conditions on a particular metric that guarantee the uniqueness conclusions without a genericity hypothesis. (Niu.)
