## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [New Constraints on the Ultrahigh Energy Neutrino Flux from the First Array-Wide Search with the Askaryan Radio Array](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14350)
- **Abstract:** The ARA Collaboration reports its first array-wide diffuse ultra-high-energy neutrino search, with no candidates and a flux upper limit from 50 to 1000 EeV.
- **Authors:** ARA Collaboration, N. Alden, S. Ali, P. Allison, D. Z. Besson, A. Bishop, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.14350v1` (published 2026-11-10 17:15:01 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.HE` (primary), `astro-ph.IM`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** An independent neutrino-news roundup highlighted the result; it combines all five ARA stations and 10.6 years of livetime with a new array-wide flux constraint. The companion methodology paper is part of the same analysis, not an independent confirmation.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report zero events on an expected background of 0.13 ± 0.01 (stat.) +0.05/−0.06 (syst.) and an upper limit they describe as the strongest from 50–1000 EeV; they also exclude some trans-GZK proton-source models at 90% confidence.
- **Evidence type:** observational; 10.6 years of data from all five South Pole ARA stations, with detector and neutrino simulations used in the search.
- **Caveat:** This is a null search, not a neutrino detection; exposure depends on ice optical properties, the Askaryan-emission model, and the neutrino–nucleon cross section. The authors estimate the cross-section contribution to exposure uncertainty at about 150% at high energies.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [Tidal potential measured with distant optical clocks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14293)
- **Abstract:** Fibre-linked strontium and ytterbium clocks 692 km apart measure tidal changes in gravitational potential over 353 days, separate lunar- and solar-frequency semidiurnal components, and model precision using the actual sampling.
- **Authors:** Lei Hou, Xiang Zhang, Qiang Zhu, Xiang-Pei Liu, Fa-Xi Chen, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.14293v1` (published 2026-11-10 16:30:42 UTC; categories: `physics.optics` (primary), `physics.geo-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Cross-listed between optics and geophysics and reports an actual long-baseline clock measurement, rather than a proposed sensitivity. The authors quantify the response to multiple tidal components and provide a sampling-aware precision model.
- **Claimed result:** The authors measure a response of 0.95 ± 0.11 to the combined solid-Earth and ocean-loading tide and 1.01 ± 0.13 for the lunar semidiurnal group, while separating lunar and solar-frequency contributions.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; strontium and ytterbium optical clocks connected by a phase-stabilized fibre link, with 545.9 hours of retained measurements across five campaigns.
- **Caveat:** The diurnal and ocean-loading components have signal-to-noise ratios near unity; gaps, sampling aliases, and daily laboratory variations limit their precision.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Atomic, molecular & optical physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Search for heavy top quark partners with charge 5/3 in the single-lepton final state in proton-proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 TeV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14335)
- **Abstract:** CMS searches 13 TeV proton-proton collision data for pair-produced charge-5/3 vector-like quarks in conventional top–W and exotic top–charged-Higgs decay modes, and sets mass-dependent cross-section limits.
- **Authors:** CMS Collaboration
- **arXiv:** `2611.14335v1` (published 2026-11-10 16:59:03 UTC; categories: `hep-ex` (primary))
- **Evidence for ranking:** The analysis has a CMS public-results record (B2G-24-005) and was submitted to Physical Review D; it uses 138 fb⁻¹ of LHC data and gives the first limits for the stated charged-Higgs decay scenario.
- **Claimed result:** CMS reports no significant excess and excludes masses up to 1630 GeV for the top–W mode and up to 1740 GeV for the top–charged-Higgs mode when the charged Higgs mass is 800 GeV.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; CMS proton-proton collision data at 13 TeV collected during 2016–2018, corresponding to 138 fb⁻¹.
- **Caveat:** The exclusions assume the specified decay chains and branching fractions; they do not cover arbitrary mixtures of vector-like-quark decay modes.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 4. [Nonperturbative string dynamics in a (2+1)D lattice gauge theory on a superconducting quantum processor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14484)
- **Abstract:** The authors use a 63-qubit superconducting processor to measure electric-string dynamics in a two-dimensional $Z_2$ lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter across confined and deconfined regimes.
- **Authors:** Sanziana Stelea, Anthony Gandon, Francesco Tacchino, Jad C. Halimeh, Ivano Tavernelli
- **arXiv:** `2611.14484v1` (published 2026-11-10 18:45:36 UTC; categories: `quant-ph` (primary))
- **Evidence for ranking:** The experiment brings a concrete nonperturbative gauge-dynamics problem to a 63-qubit device and uses local Gauss-law constraints as an error-detection resource; the reported observations are supported by spatially resolved measurements.
- **Claimed result:** The authors observe rapid charge and flux spreading in the deconfined regime, enhanced matter occupation near a pair-creation resonance, and suppressed spreading at strong electric fields, with circuits reaching 15 second-order Trotter steps.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; 63 superconducting qubits, including 12 ancillas, with ancilla checks, noise rescaling, and classical reference calculations.
- **Caveat:** Hardware noise and finite circuit depth limit the evolution time; the tensor-network comparison is itself restricted to times where bond-dimension convergence is obtained.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 5. [Tracing the Epoch of Reionization through extreme emission line galaxies around Cosmic Noon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14332)
- **Abstract:** The authors assemble and spectroscopically validate a sample of 229 extreme-emission-line galaxies at redshifts about 1.0–1.7, assessing them as lower-redshift analogues of galaxies associated with reionization.
- **Authors:** M. Delgado Mancheño, J. Gallego, S. Pascual, M. Tang, R. Pello, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.14332v1` (published 2026-11-10 16:56:06 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.GA` (primary))
- **Evidence for ranking:** The arXiv record says accepted for Astronomy & Astrophysics; the sample is checked against spectroscopy from MMT/MMIRS, Keck/MOSFIRE, HST/WFC3, JWST/NIRSpec, and GTC/OSIRIS+.
- **Claimed result:** The authors identify 102 Hβ+[O III] emitters near z=1.7 and 127 Hα emitters near z=1.0; they report compact, low-mass, strongly star-forming systems whose properties resemble z>5 JWST extreme emitters.
- **Evidence type:** observational; CANDELS/EGS photometry and data products, validated with ground- and space-based spectroscopy including JWST/NIRSpec.
- **Caveat:** The proposed reionization connection is an analogy from cosmic-noon galaxies, not a direct measurement of the z>6 reionization population; photometric selection purities are below 100%.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics

### 6. [High-Speed Control of a Fluxonium Qubit via Picosecond Pulses from Josephson Digital Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14358)
- **Abstract:** The authors propose two single-flux-quantum pulse protocols for fast fluxonium control and report simulated single-qubit gate fidelities above 99.99%.
- **Authors:** M. V. Bastrakova, D. S. Pashin, V. A. Vozhakov, N. V. Klenov, I. I. Soloviev
- **arXiv:** `2611.14358v1` (published 2026-11-10 17:17:08 UTC; categories: `quant-ph` (primary))
- **Evidence for ranking:** The work has a verified DOI and appears as an accepted paper in Physical Review Applied; it connects Josephson digital pulse generation with control of a low-frequency fluxonium device.
- **Claimed result:** Multilevel simulations indicate that flux-channel pulse packets provide robust high-fidelity control, while phase-shifted flux and charge sequences can shorten gates without losing the reported high fidelity.
- **Evidence type:** computational; multilevel simulations of fluxonium dynamics under picosecond single-flux-quantum pulses.
- **Caveat:** The quoted fidelities are simulation results, not a hardware demonstration; performance depends on device parameters, flux bias, pulse design, and the modeled control errors.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors

### 7. [Probing for anisotropies at different angular scales in the arrival directions of the cosmic rays detected at the Pierre Auger Observatory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14233)
- **Abstract:** The Pierre Auger Collaboration applies a von Mises–Fisher model to search for ultra-high-energy cosmic-ray arrival-direction excesses across angular scales using the Observatory’s Phase I data.
- **Authors:** The Pierre Auger Collaboration, A. Abdul Halim, P. Abreu, M. Aglietta, M. Ahmed, I. Allekotte, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.14233v1` (published 2026-11-10 15:45:43 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.HE` (primary))
- **Evidence for ranking:** It combines the Observatory’s full 2004–2022 Phase I exposure (123,000 km² sr yr) with a method that treats multiple angular scales consistently; the Centaurus feature is reported only as an indication.
- **Claimed result:** The authors find the most significant excess to be a dipole in the 8–16 EeV bin, consistent with prior Auger findings, and report indications of a Centaurus-region intermediate-scale excess above 32 EeV.
- **Evidence type:** observational; Pierre Auger Observatory events above 4 EeV from 1 January 2004 through 31 December 2022.
- **Caveat:** The higher-energy feature is not a source identification or discovery; the abstract characterizes it as an indication, and more exposure is needed to establish its persistence.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 8. [Leveraging Lensing-driven Early Warnings: Joint Detectability of lensed Gravitational Waves and their LSST Optical Counterparts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14343)
- **Abstract:** The authors simulate lensed binary-neutron-star and neutron-star–black-hole mergers for the O5 observing run and estimate how often LSST could detect their lensed kilonova counterparts.
- **Authors:** Sourabh Magare, Anindya, Anupreeta More, Shasvath J. Kapadia
- **arXiv:** `2611.14343v1` (published 2026-11-10 17:08:59 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.HE` (primary), `astro-ph.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It is cross-listed across high-energy astrophysics and cosmology and quantifies a direct gravitational-wave/optical follow-up pathway using LSST exposure limits; the result is a forecast rather than a detection.
- **Claimed result:** For the simulated O5 population, the authors estimate that roughly 60% of detectable lensed BNS kilonovae and 10% of NSBH kilonovae would be visible in a single LSST exposure.
- **Evidence type:** forecast/proposal; simulated lensed-merger populations and benchmark lensed-kilonova light curves evaluated against LSST bands and exposure limits.
- **Caveat:** Detectability depends on the assumed merger and lens populations, kilonova light-curve models, and survey exposure limits; no lensed counterpart is observed in this study.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 11 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Astrophysics

## Trending Research Themes

- **Large datasets turned into limits or directional structure:** ARA’s decade-scale radio search sets an ultra-high-energy neutrino limit, CMS updates vector-like-quark exclusions with 138 fb⁻¹, and Auger tests cosmic-ray anisotropy over its full Phase I exposure. These are distinct observatories and analyses, not independent confirmations of one result.
- **Quantum and precision-control hardware:** The optical-clock study resolves geophysical tidal components across a 692 km link; Stelea et al. use a superconducting processor for gauge dynamics; Bastrakova et al. model Josephson-pulse control of fluxonium. Together these link precision measurement, digital control, and quantum simulation, but only the clock and gauge-theory papers report measurements on operating hardware.
- **Astronomical samples and follow-up strategies:** Delgado Mancheño et al. use multi-facility spectroscopy, including JWST/NIRSpec, to validate cosmic-noon galaxy analogues, while Magare et al. forecast LSST follow-up of lensed gravitational-wave mergers. One is an observed galaxy sample; the other is a model-based future observing forecast.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **ARA sensitivity and systematics (author-reported):** The null result’s expected background and detector acceptance retain statistical and systematic uncertainty. **Follow-up (synthesis):** extend livetime, improve in-ice calibration and acceptance modeling, and compare the radio-array constraints with optical-Cherenkov measurements and future radio arrays. [2611.14350; companion methods paper 2611.14354]
- **Separating weaker tidal components (author-reported):** The clock paper finds diurnal and ocean-loading responses at signal-to-noise near unity, with gaps and daily laboratory variations affecting precision. **Follow-up (synthesis):** collect longer, less interrupted records across more clock sites and include local environmental monitoring. [2611.14293]
- **Longer nonperturbative quantum dynamics (author-reported):** Hardware noise limits circuit duration, while classical tensor-network convergence is available only for a subset of times. **Follow-up (synthesis):** test improved gauge-protection/error-mitigation schemes and longer circuits against converged calculations in regimes where both methods apply. [2611.14484]
- **Composition and the Centaurus excess (author-reported):** Auger reports an intermediate-scale indication, not an identified source. **Follow-up (synthesis):** seek persistence in additional exposure and test the angular feature alongside composition-sensitive measurements using pre-specified energy and angular selections. [2611.14233]
- **Forecast-to-observation bridge (synthesis):** The LSST counterpart fractions depend on merger/lens populations and kilonova models. Update the detectability calculation with measured O5 alerts and realized survey cadence, then compare the forecast to actual follow-up outcomes. [2611.14343]
- **Analogue validity (author-reported question):** The EELG study supports cosmic-noon systems as analogues, but its reionization inference is indirect and selection purity is below 100%. **Follow-up (synthesis):** enlarge the spectroscopically confirmed sample and compare its distributions directly with higher-redshift JWST emitters using matched selection functions. [2611.14332]

## Takeaway
