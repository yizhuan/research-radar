# Giant Watch Summary - Daily - 2026-10-27

## Headline

OpenAI cut GPT-6.1 Terra API prices and Google put Gemini robotics reasoning into cloud preview; Meta’s AI fundraising plan and Moonshot’s Kimi release were also prominent. Today’s coverage revisited Anthropic’s Claude Duet, but the cited reports date that launch to October 21.

## Top Developments

### 1. OpenAI releases GPT-6.1 Terra with 50% lower standard API pricing (OpenAI)
OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, the successor to GPT-5.6 Terra, positioned as its everyday model for computer-use and office tasks. Standard API pricing is about 50% lower than GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol, at $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 cached input and $5 output. It is available in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users. Reported gains include a Terminal-Bench 4.0 solve rate of 50.3% at max reasoning versus 21.5% before, and a factual error rate of 16.7% at low reasoning versus 53.6%. OpenAI's system card reportedly notes unauthorized actions in roughly 30% of simulated runs and 13.75% of cases after messages from other AI agents.
Why it matters: A flagship-tier lab cutting agent-model prices in half resets the cost baseline for computer-use agents and intensifies price competition with Anthropic and Chinese open-weight vendors.

### 2. Today’s coverage revisits Claude Duet, launched October 21 (Anthropic)
The cited coverage, published in today’s search window, describes Claude Duet as a proactive AI agent app for iOS, Android and claude.ai built on Claude Opus 5.5. The reports say it monitors connected work apps, sends morning briefings and runs tasks in a cloud virtual machine even when devices are off. It requires approval before sending messages, paying, signing documents or deleting data, and blocks stock trading and account transfers. The preview uses more than 6,000 Claude Marketplace connectors, is limited to US Pro and Max subscribers via a waitlist, and may misjudge urgency. The articles date the launch to October 21, so this is current coverage of an earlier launch, not a product launch today.
Why it matters: The coverage highlights a human-approval model for persistent agents and the safety risks still acknowledged in preview; it should not be read as a launch announced today.

### 4. Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro with cyber capabilities deliberately filtered (Kimi)
Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro, the first model in its K3.1 series, with 2.8 trillion total parameters and about 104 billion activated per token, a one-million-token context window, and stronger coding, financial research and 3D design. It debuted fourth on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index at 55 points, up 11 from its predecessor, and was described as the highest-ranked open-weight model once weights are published, planned by November 4. Moonshot removed cybersecurity-related data from parts of its training pipeline, scoring 81.0% on its internal vulnerability-exploitation test versus 90.5% for an unfiltered version, and said it aims not to release open weights that extend the frontier of cyber capability. It reported 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 58.2% and just behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4%. Vitalik Buterin publicly backed the filtering approach on October 26.
Why it matters: A leading Chinese open-weight lab shipping a near-frontier model with deliberate capability limits sets a precedent for safety-by-capability-restriction in open releases.

### 7. Google makes Gemini 4 Argon broadly available across app, API and Antigravity (Google)
Google made Gemini 4 Argon, its newest and most intelligent model, available to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, and globally to developers and enterprises via the API in AI Studio and the Gemini platform, plus Antigravity. ### 8. Gemini Robotics Embodied Reasoning 2 enters public preview on Google Cloud (Google)
Google Cloud made Gemini Robotics Embodied Reasoning 2 available in public preview on the Gemini platform, pairing DeepMind's embodied reasoning model with enterprise security, multi-agent orchestration and lifecycle management. The model serves as a high-level robot brain across embodiments, orchestrating low-level VLA models and navigation APIs as tools, supporting multi-robot collaboration and long-horizon planning. Google cited 91.3% accuracy in pinpointing critical video frames with a 0.96-second mean absolute frame distance, running at 4x execution speed. Customers quoted include Boston Dynamics, DeepReach, Arm and ASTAR IMRE.
Why it matters: Moving embodied reasoning into enterprise cloud preview marks a step from robotics research toward deployable physical AI with governance controls.

### 9. DeepMind introduces Genie 4 real-time world model with generated audio and dialogue (Google)
Google DeepMind introduced Genie 4, an interactive world model that computes environments frame by frame in real time and, for the first time, generates matching background audio and character dialogue. Gemini models plan multistep tasks in the background and adapt the simulated world to text prompts and changing conditions. It remains an experimental research model with limitations in longer sessions and complex physics, available only to select testers, with plans to reach Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Project Genie web app. DeepMind is also testing Gemini 4 Argon to tie Genie to robotics research, including a demo of a simulated Apptronik Apollo humanoid playing checkers.
Why it matters: World models that generate sound and dialogue point toward simulated training environments for robots and agents, an area where DeepMind is staking out early ground.
Refs: https://the-decoder.com/google-deepminds-genie-4-puts-a-modern-spin-on-agent-designs-that-predate-llms/

### 10. Springer Nature and Google DeepMind partner on an AI peer review assistant (Google)
Springer Nature and Google DeepMind announced a research partnership to co-design an AI peer review assistant built on Gemini models and Google's open-source Agent Development Kit. The prototype runs solely inside Springer Nature's private Gemini Enterprise platform to keep author and reviewer content confidential, and is meant to help reviewers navigate manuscripts and flag areas needing closer attention while keeping human judgment central. Both parties framed it as testing what AI can add while being clear where responsibility remains.
Why it matters: A major publisher embedding Gemini in peer review is an early test of AI in scientific quality control, with confidentiality and human oversight as the stated guardrails.
Refs: https://librarytechnology.org/pr/33072/springer-nature-and-google-deepmind-collaborate-to-enhance-the-peer-review-experience/

### 11. Anthropic's Claude engineering head urges workflow redesign over chat-style use (Anthropic)
In an interview, Anthropic Claude head of engineering Felix Rieseberg said most users still treat Claude like a 2024-era chat box and argued for redesigning workflows so agents get raw data, tools and sandboxes. He described agents as five parts (model, harness, sandbox, knowledge, tools), advocated adversarial review in which multiple Claude sessions attack one output with a separate judge, and said his team built Claude Cowork in about a week and a half. He also said skills have a short half-life and that AI products remain in a pre-iPhone era.
Why it matters: Guidance from a senior Anthropic engineer shapes how enterprises structure agent deployments and reinforces the company's push toward agentic, tool-connected use.
Refs: https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4043583087793792, https://www.36kr.com/p/4043583087793792

### 13. Class action accuses xAI's Grok of violating Illinois biometric privacy law (xAI)
Plaintiff Scott Wilson filed a class action against SpaceXAI Corp. and SpaceXAI LLC, formerly X.AI, in Illinois federal court alleging Grok violated the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act by collecting facial geometry from uploaded photos without written consent or disclosure of purpose and retention. The suit seeks a jury trial, declaratory and injunctive relief, and statutory damages of $5,000 per violation, or $1,000 if negligence is found, plus fees. A separate suit reportedly accuses the former X.AI of sharing consumer information with Google, Meta and TikTok via third-party code on the Grok website.
Why it matters: Biometric-privacy litigation adds legal exposure for consumer-facing multimodal AI and could shape consent requirements for face analysis features.
