# Giant Watch Summary - Daily - 2026-11-10

## Headline

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 just before its planned IPO roadshow, pairing benchmark gains with a 20% price cut. The day's broader theme is competition moving from raw model capability to agent harnesses and distribution channels.

## Top Developments

### 1. Anthropic launches Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 ahead of IPO (Anthropic)
Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, two models built on the same underlying model but split by safeguards: Fable 5.2 is open to all users with biology and cybersecurity guardrails, while Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences organizations. Anthropic reported gains in coding, science, chart understanding and robotics benchmarks, cited a prototype operating system built by 1,000+ sub-agents over eight days, and cut standard pricing to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, 20% below the prior generation. Coverage ties the release to Anthropic's planned IPO.
Why it matters: The strongest single event of the day: a flagship frontier release that also resets Anthropic's price point and is explicitly framed as positioning ahead of a public listing.

### 2. DeepSeek Harness reaches 1.54 million daily active users (DeepSeek)
DeepSeek disclosed on X that its open-source agent application DeepSeek Harness reached 1.54 million daily active users by Nov 4, less than three months after its Aug 13 debut, up from about 271,000 at the end of September after a desktop client launched. Reports describe Harness as model-agnostic, able to connect to Anthropic, OpenAI, Kimi, GLM or any compatible model service, and say DeepSeek plans an official plugin marketplace plus a support program for plugin creators.
Why it matters: A concrete adoption milestone that positions DeepSeek in the agent-harness layer rather than only model performance, directly challenging Claude Code and Codex-style products.

### 3. Vercel adds Z.ai's GLM 5.3 Flash to Token Pass (GLM)
Vercel announced that GLM 5.3 Flash, Z.ai's multimodal coding model with a 1M-token context window, is now available in Token Pass, a $30/month Pro-team subscription granting $80 in monthly credit through AI Gateway. The model is listed alongside Kimi K3, GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, supports text and image inputs, function calling, structured output and streaming, and is available as zai/glm-5.3-flash with zero data retention by default.
Why it matters: A concrete distribution win for a Chinese open-weight model family inside a major developer platform, and evidence that coding-agent subscriptions are becoming a packaging layer for model access.
Refs: https://theagenttimes.com/articles/vercel-adds-glm-5-3-flash-to-30-token-pass-899e9c6d

### 4. Menlo Ventures report: Chinese models take 89% of open-source enterprise spend (Kimi, DeepSeek, GLM, Meta)
A Menlo Ventures report, based on a September survey of nearly 400 US enterprise decision-makers, says Chinese models account for 89% of global enterprise spending on open-source models: Moonshot's Kimi at 61%, DeepSeek at 15%, Z.ai's GLM at 11% and Meta's Llama at 7%. The report also says open-source models are only 4% of overall model spend, while Anthropic and OpenAI together hold 86% of API spend.
Why it matters: The clearest quantitative snapshot of where open-weight enterprise money sits, and a contrast with the closed-API dominance that Anthropic's launch is competing within.

### 6. SoftBank, Grab and PETROS sign JDA for $1.85B Malaysia AI infrastructure (Cross-organization)
SoftBank Group, Grab and Petroleum Sarawak Berhad signed joint development agreements for $1.85 billion in initial combined seed capital to build AI infrastructure in Sarawak and Peninsular Malaysia, with a long-term target of five gigawatts or more of AI compute capacity. The initial capital comprises $1 billion from SoftBank, $750 million from Grab and $100 million from PETROS, deployed in stages subject to development and customer-contract milestones; the parties will seek additional investors and hyperscaler customers.
Why it matters: A large regional compute commitment that shows AI infrastructure buildout spreading beyond US and Chinese hubs, though it involves none of the named AI model organizations.
