### 1. [On a Conjecture of Fiedorowicz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38760)
- **Abstract:** The author proves that every finite simply connected CW complex is homotopy equivalent to the classifying space of a finite monoid, using a mapping-cone construction and giving a 1,795-element example for the complex projective plane.
- **Authors:** Oren Ben-Bassat
- **arXiv:** `2610.38760v1` (published 2026-10-28 19:40:57 UTC; categories: `math.AT`, `math.GR`, `math.RA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The author states a resolution of Fiedorowicz’s conjecture, and the introduction says the construction in fact uses aperiodic finite monoids; the algebraic-topology, group-theory, and algebra categories provide a concrete cross-area signal.
- **Claimed result:** The author claims every finite simply connected CW complex has the homotopy type of the classifying space of an aperiodic finite monoid; the paper constructs such monoids by realizing mapping cones. The introduction states the aperiodicity strengthening.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The realized space is a finite, path-connected CW complex with trivial fundamental group; the constructed monoid is finite.
- **Caveat:** The theorem does not cover arbitrary non-simply-connected finite complexes; the paper notes that a finite monoid classifying space has finite fundamental group.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch not yet displayed for this item at the snapshot
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Algebra & number theory

### 2. [Primitive flag-transitive generalized quadrangles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38819)
- **Abstract:** The authors resolve Kantor’s classification conjecture for finite thick generalized quadrangles when a flag-transitive automorphism group acts primitively on both points and lines, treating all almost simple groups of Lie type.
- **Authors:** Timothy C. Burness, Tao Feng, Jianbing Lu
- **arXiv:** `2610.38819v1` (published 2026-10-28 20:15:15 UTC; categories: `math.CO`, `math.GR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is a claimed completion of the conjecture in the stated primitive-action case, with a direct bridge between finite incidence geometry and finite group theory. The 97-page paper’s abstract is explicit about the remaining scope.
- **Claimed result:** The authors claim that Kantor’s conjecture holds for all generalized quadrangles in this setting, by resolving the cases where the acting almost simple group has Lie-type socle of any Lie rank.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The quadrangle is finite and thick; its flag-transitive group acts primitively on both the point set and line set.
- **Caveat:** This theorem covers the primitive point-and-line action case, not every flag-transitive action covered by the broadest formulation of Kantor’s conjecture.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch not yet displayed for this item at the snapshot
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Algebra & number theory

### 3. [The Borisov--Nuer conjecture and root decompositions in even unimodular Lorentzian lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38414)
- **Abstract:** The authors classify when every vector in the even unimodular Lorentzian lattices `II_{8n+1,1}` is a sum of two roots, settling the Borisov–Nuer conjecture and deriving an Ulrich-line-bundle consequence for unnodal Enriques surfaces.
- **Authors:** Haowu Wang, Shaoyun Yi
- **arXiv:** `2610.38414v1` (published 2026-10-28 08:00:56 UTC; categories: `math.NT`, `math.AG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors claim to settle a named lattice conjecture and connect the classification to algebraic geometry; the abstract also describes a theta-series method for root counts.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove that every vector of `II_{8n+1,1}` is a sum of two roots exactly for `n=1` or `n=2`; they derive the stated Ulrich existence result for every very ample polarization on a complex unnodal Enriques surface.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The lattice is even, unimodular, and of signature `(8n+1,1)`, with `n≥1`; the geometric consequence assumes a complex unnodal Enriques surface and a very ample polarization.
- **Caveat:** For `n≥3` universal representability fails; the paper’s finer criteria for negative-norm vectors distinguish sufficiently large norms and specific lattice conditions rather than asserting universal representation.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch not yet displayed for this item at the snapshot
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Geometry & topology

### 4. [Extended weak order in all rank three Coxeter groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38849)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove that extended weak order is a lattice for every rank-three Coxeter group and deduce Dyer’s Conjecture A for these groups, using cleanliness of the associated Coxeter arrangements.
- **Authors:** Grant Barkley, Colin Defant, Patricia Hersh, Jon McCammond, Thomas McConville, David E Speyer
- **arXiv:** `2610.38849v1` (published 2026-10-28 20:32:59 UTC; categories: `math.CO`, `math.GR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract claims a result for all rank-three Coxeter groups and a linked conjecture consequence; the paper spans combinatorics and group theory.
- **Claimed result:** The authors claim extended weak order is a lattice for every rank-three Coxeter group and that cover relations are single-root toggles, as asserted by Dyer’s Conjecture A.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The result concerns Coxeter groups of rank three and their Coxeter arrangements; the arrangement property used is that every biclosed set is hyperplane-separated from its complement.
- **Caveat:** The theorem is rank-specific; it does not establish the corresponding lattice and cover-relation statements in higher rank.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch not yet displayed for this item at the snapshot
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Algebra & number theory

### 5. [Sharp Bounds for Toral Eigenfunctions and Threefold Additive Energy on Circles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37423)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove an optimal-order upper bound for threefold additive energy on lattice circles and a uniform weak `L^6` bound for normalized square-torus eigenfunctions, with consequences for arithmetic random-wave nodal lengths.
- **Authors:** Guiyu Xie, Cheng Zhang
- **arXiv:** `2610.37423v1` (published 2026-10-27 15:36:50 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math.CO`, `math.NT`, `math.SP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Four mathematics categories connect PDE, combinatorics, number theory, and spectral theory; the authors claim both an optimal additive-energy bound and Bourgain’s conjectured eigenfunction bounds for `1≤p<6`.
- **Claimed result:** For `P_n={z∈Z²: |z|²=n}`, the authors prove `J_3(P_n)≤C|P_n|³`; they also prove a uniform weak `L^6` estimate and uniform `L^p` bounds for every `1≤p<6`.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The arithmetic set is the integer lattice circle; the eigenfunctions are `L²`-normalized eigenfunctions on the square torus.
- **Caveat:** The stated endpoint is weak `L^6`; the abstract does not claim a uniform strong `L^6` bound.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 28 October 2026
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 6. [Minimal acceleration and the topology of premonotonic cocycles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37479)
- **Abstract:** The authors answer Avila’s acceleration question for real-analytic `SL(2,R)` cocycles over every irrational circle rotation, relating the lower bound by topological degree to premonotonicity and proving path-connectedness of the nonzero-degree premonotonic locus.
- **Authors:** Xu Xia, Jiahao Xu
- **arXiv:** `2610.37479v1` (published 2026-10-27 16:07:08 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.SP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper states an affirmative answer to a named question without arithmetic restrictions and links dynamical and spectral theory; its further path-connectedness result adds a concrete structural consequence.
- **Claimed result:** For every irrational frequency, the authors affirm the acceleration lower bound by the absolute topological degree and characterize equality at nonzero degree by premonotonicity; they also prove the stated path-connectedness result.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The objects are real-analytic `SL(2,R)` cocycles over irrational rotations; the cited characterization of uniform hyperbolicity is discussed in the positive Lyapunov exponent regime.
- **Caveat:** The conclusions concern this one-frequency analytic cocycle setting; the additional characterization in the positive Lyapunov exponent regime should not be read as a statement about all cocycles or all spectral models.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 28 October 2026
- **Themes:** Dynamical systems & spectral theory

### 7. [Local Law and Eigenvector Delocalization for Random Lifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37463)
- **Abstract:** For random lifts of a fixed finite graph, the authors prove high-probability Green-function estimates down to spectral scales away from singular energies, implying a logarithmic-scale sup-norm bound for eigenvectors in that region.
- **Authors:** Jiaoyang Huang, Ruizhe Xu
- **arXiv:** `2610.37463v1` (published 2026-10-27 16:00:41 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`, `math.CO`, `math.SP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Four categories tie probability, combinatorics, spectral theory, and mathematical physics; the abstract gives a quantitative local law and a concrete delocalization consequence.
- **Claimed result:** For random `N`-lifts, the authors claim entrywise Green-function estimates at scales `(log N)^C/N` and, as a consequence, `||u||∞≤(log N)^C/√N` with high probability for the specified eigenvectors.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The base is a fixed finite connected simple graph of minimum degree at least three; eigenvalues must remain a fixed positive distance from singular energies of the universal-cover spectrum.
- **Caveat:** Singular energies are excluded, and the theorem’s stated base-graph hypothesis does not cover every finite graph.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 28 October 2026
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Mathematical physics

### 8. [Sharp Stability of Brenier Potentials and its Link to Poincaré Inequalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37659)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove a functional inequality stronger than Poincaré for uniformly log-concave measures, yielding sharp stability of quadratic-transport Brenier potentials and a Gaussian matrix-distance comparison.
- **Authors:** Cyril Letrouit, Tudor Manole
- **arXiv:** `2610.37659v1` (published 2026-10-27 17:59:00 UTC; categories: `math.FA`, `math.AP`, `math.MG`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Four mathematics categories and the stated link between functional inequalities, optimal transport, and probability provide multiple corroborating scope signals.
- **Claimed result:** For uniformly log-concave measures, the authors establish a variance bound for differences of convex functions that gives a sharp stability estimate for Brenier potentials as the target measure varies; for Gaussians, they recover equivalence of the Bures–Wasserstein and square-root Frobenius distances.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The source measure is fixed and uniformly log-concave; the transport is quadratic and the result is about variation of the target.
- **Caveat:** The stated theorem relies on uniform log-concavity and concerns quadratic optimal transport; the abstract does not assert the same estimate for arbitrary source measures or costs.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 28 October 2026
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Probability & statistics; Optimization & control

### 9. [A logarithmic error term for subset sums of zero-sum-free sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38373)
- **Abstract:** The author proves a near-quadratic lower bound, with logarithmic error, on the number of distinct nonempty subset sums of a zero-sum-free set in any finite abelian group, obtaining the optimal leading coefficient and an Olson-constant consequence.
- **Authors:** Chao Liu
- **arXiv:** `2610.38373v1` (published 2026-10-28 07:12:26 UTC; categories: `math.CO`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper gives a uniform quantitative improvement across finite abelian groups and links additive combinatorics with number theory.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves `f(k)≥k²/2−13k log(k+1)−116k`, hence `f(k)=k²/2+O(k log k)`, and derives `Ol(G)≤√(2|G|)+O(log|G|)` for every finite abelian group.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The set has `k` elements and is zero-sum-free; the lower bound ranges over all finite abelian groups, with no order or stabilizer restriction.
- **Caveat:** The asymptotic error remains `O(k log k)`; this is not an exact formula for each group or each `k`.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch not yet displayed for this item at the snapshot
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Algebra & number theory

## Trending Research Themes

- **Conjecture and classification closures are prominent in this selected set, not evidence of a field-wide shift.** Ben-Bassat’s monoid realization theorem, Burness–Feng–Lu’s primitive-action case of Kantor’s conjecture, Wang–Yi’s Lorentzian-lattice classification, and Barkley et al.’s rank-three Coxeter result each close a distinct conjecture or classification question.
- **Sharp estimates connect combinatorial structure to spectral behavior.** Xie–Zhang pass from additive energy on lattice circles to toral eigenfunction bounds; Huang–Xu use local resolvent control on random graph lifts to derive eigenvector delocalization. These are separate techniques, but both deliver quantitative spectral consequences from discrete structure.
- **Geometry interfaces with analysis and probability.** Letrouit–Manole connect log-concave functional inequalities to optimal transport stability, while Nienhaus–Wulle identify a boundary of a curvature-based connectedness principle in Alexandrov geometry. These are complementary examples of proving a sharp positive estimate versus locating a counterexample boundary.
- **Finite algebraic and combinatorial objects support strong uniform statements.** Liu’s subset-sum bound is uniform over finite abelian groups, and Barkley et al. obtain a lattice structure across all rank-three Coxeter groups; the commonality is breadth over a class of discrete objects, not a shared proof method.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated scope gap — generalized quadrangles:** Burness–Feng–Lu settle Kantor’s conjecture when the flag-transitive group acts primitively on both points and lines. The abstract says the wider problem remains open outside resolved cases. A concrete follow-up is to classify the non-primitive actions not covered by their almost-simple Lie-type analysis. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38819))
- **Author-established gap — higher Lorentzian lattices:** Wang–Yi show universal two-root representation fails for `n≥3`; their result characterizes non-representation for sufficiently large negative norm, while the stated threshold depends on `n`. A follow-up is to sharpen the low-negative-norm range and identify which perpendicular-lattice features decide representability there. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38414))
- **Research direction (synthesis) — endpoint eigenfunction control:** Xie–Zhang prove weak `L^6` and strong uniform `L^p` bounds for `p<6`, but do not claim strong `L^6`. Testing whether the endpoint can be strengthened, and which nodal-statistics consequences would follow, is a natural boundary question rather than a result of the preprint. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37423))
- **Research direction (synthesis) — singular energies in random lifts:** Huang–Xu’s local law excludes energies near singularities of the universal-cover spectrum. A follow-up could quantify behavior as the excluded distance shrinks with `N`, or test whether a modified scaling persists at those energies. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37463))
- **Research direction (synthesis) — transport beyond uniform log-concavity:** Letrouit–Manole’s stability estimate assumes a uniformly log-concave source and quadratic cost. Extending it to weaker curvature/integrability assumptions or different transport costs would test which hypotheses are essential. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37659))
