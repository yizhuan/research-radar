## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Observation of light-induced Haldane gap in graphene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42115)
- **Abstract:** The authors report a transient Dirac gap in monolayer graphene that appears only under circularly polarized driving, identifying it with a light-induced Haldane mass and Floquet topology.
- **Authors:** Fei Wang, Xuanxi Cai, Wanying Chen, Xiao Tang, Jinze Li, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.42115v1` (published 2026-10-30T17:49:52Z; categories: `cond-mat.mes-hall`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A specific experimental claim was independently discussed by [The Machine Press](https://themachinepress.com/story/mp-2026-11-02-012/circular-light-opens-graphene-s-haldane-gap) and indexed by [ArXiv TLDR](https://arxivtldr.org/category/condensed-matter); the [authors' group publication list](https://info.phys.tsinghua.edu.cn/zhou/ZhouLab_Publication%20list.html) also records the preprint. These are coverage and research-release signals, not evidence of citation growth.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report a 203 ± 30 meV light-induced Dirac gap under circularly polarized mid-infrared pumping, absent under linear polarization; time-resolved spectra and Floquet calculations link it to dynamical time-reversal-symmetry breaking (arXiv abstract and paper).
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; time- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (TrARPES) on monolayer graphene, interpreted with Floquet calculations.
- **Caveat:** The effect is transient and measured in a particular pump–probe regime on monolayer graphene; the topological Berry-curvature/Chern interpretation is supported by calculations rather than a transport measurement of a quantized response.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-30 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

### 2. [Probing the charm-quark dead cone effect in heavy ion collisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01285)
- **Abstract:** The CMS authors measure late, collinear jet emissions in PbPb collisions and find a suppression of small-angle emissions in prompt-D⁰-tagged jets consistent with the charm-quark dead-cone effect.
- **Authors:** CMS Collaboration
- **arXiv:** `2611.01285v1` (published 2026-10-31T21:38:03Z; categories: `nucl-ex`, `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is described as the first experimental study of the charm dead cone in heavy-ion collisions, uses CMS LHC data, is cross-listed between nuclear and high-energy experiment, and is [submitted to Physical Review Letters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01285). The collaboration links its public result page in the arXiv comments: [CMS-HIN-25-013](https://cms-results.web.cern.ch/cms-results/public-results/publications/HIN-25-013/).
- **Claimed result:** CMS reports that D⁰-tagged jets have fewer small-angle splittings than inclusive jets in PbPb data, consistent with the charm dead cone; its PbPb-to-pp comparison disfavors scenarios with strong filling of that cone by medium-induced radiation (arXiv abstract and paper).
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; 2018 CMS PbPb collisions at √sNN = 5.02 TeV, with 1.7 nb⁻¹ integrated luminosity and late-kT jet grooming.
- **Caveat:** Within the measured kinematic range and precision, the PbPb-versus-pp comparison shows no significant difference and has limited power to distinguish the jet-medium models considered by the authors.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-31 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 3. [A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger Observatory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06284)
- **Abstract:** The Pierre Auger authors model Earth-skimming and upward-going air showers to set limits on ultrahigh-energy fluxes of MeV–100 MeV sterile neutrinos mixing mainly with tau neutrinos, and derive benchmark constraints for superheavy dark matter decays.
- **Authors:** The Pierre Auger Collaboration, A. Abdul Halim, P. Abreu, M. Aglietta, M. Ahmed, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.06284v1` (published 2026-11-04T10:23:29Z; categories: `astro-ph.HE`, `hep-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper was selected for [Antonio Marrone's neutrino/particle-physics arXiv digest](https://home.ba.infn.it/~marrone/highlights/2026-11-05.html) and cross-lists astrophysical and particle phenomenology. It presents limits using Auger event samples and a detailed propagation simulation rather than claiming a sterile-neutrino detection.
- **Claimed result:** For an E⁻² spectrum and sin²θN ≲ 10⁻¹, the authors derive a 90% confidence upper limit of approximately E²ΦN ≤ 2 × 10⁻⁹ sin⁻²θN GeV cm⁻² sr⁻¹ s⁻¹ over 0.3–200 EeV; the paper also gives benchmark dark-matter-decay constraints (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** Computational; Monte Carlo exposure calculation for the Pierre Auger Observatory using observed air-shower event samples.
- **Caveat:** The limits are sensitivity constraints, not evidence of a signal; the authors' benchmark sterile-neutrino fluxes lie several orders below current sensitivity, and tau-lepton energy-loss modeling contributes a dominant +34%/−28% exposure uncertainty.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-04 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Astrophysics

### 4. [DESI DR2 Results III: The Mass of the Milky Way](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05518)
- **Abstract:** The DESI authors combine four mock-calibrated dynamical methods applied to DR2 halo-star data to estimate the Milky Way's mass and quantify method-dependent biases and correlations.
- **Authors:** DESI Collaboration, C. Aganze, J. Aguilar, S. Ahlen, C. Allende Prieto, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.05518v1` (published 2026-11-03T19:15:56Z; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper appears in the [5 November DailyArXiV priority digest](https://rhysyt.github.io/DailyArXiV/Priority.html), is identified as a DESI Collaboration Key Project publication, and combines the largest samples of the two stated halo-star tracers with four independently calibrated methods. This is one collaboration data release, not independent confirmation by four surveys.
- **Claimed result:** From 10,036 RR Lyrae and blue-horizontal-branch stars, the authors report a joint mass within 80 kpc of 6.36⁺⁰·⁵³₋₀·⁴⁸ × 10¹¹ M☉ and a virial M200c of 1.19⁺⁰·¹⁴₋₀·¹² × 10¹² M☉ after mock-based corrections (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** Observational; DESI DR2 six-dimensional halo-star data, calibrated against cosmological mock surveys and Gaia DR3-like uncertainties.
- **Caveat:** The estimates depend on mock calibration, selection-function assumptions, and residual correlations among methods; the authors note that method-specific results can differ despite the joint estimate.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-03 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Astrophysics

### 5. [A multiprobe ΛCDM consistency analysis with gravitational-wave standard sirens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00415)
- **Abstract:** The authors combine LVK GWTC-5.0 standard sirens with DES, DESI, supernova, and CMB measurements to assess ΛCDM consistency and the H₀ and S₈ tensions.
- **Authors:** T. M. C. Abbott, M. Aguena, F. Andrade-Oliveira, S. Avila, D. Bacon, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.00415v1` (published 2026-10-30T22:04:20Z; categories: `astro-ph.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It brings a new observable class—GW standard sirens—into a joint analysis with several major cosmological datasets, carries DES and Fermilab report numbers, and is [submitted to A&A Letters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00415). [INSPIRE](https://inspirehep.net/literature/3221853) records the paper and its abstract; citation evidence remains sparse for this very recent preprint.
- **Claimed result:** Adding standard sirens improves the Ωm–S₈ constraints by about 30% and yields a 6.2% H₀ constraint in the authors' analysis, while neither the S₈ discrepancy nor the H₀ tension is materially reduced at current GW sensitivity (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** Observational; LVK GWTC-5.0, DES Year 6 lensing and clustering, DESI BAO, Type Ia supernovae, and Planck/ACT/SPT CMB data combined with normalizing flows.
- **Caveat:** The present standard-siren precision is not enough to resolve either tension; the inferred constraints remain conditional on the adopted joint-probe analysis and its common priors.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-30 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Astrophysics

### 6. [Latest νs from the Earliest Galaxies: Probing neutrino self-interactions with JWST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07013)
- **Abstract:** The authors use high-redshift JWST ultraviolet luminosity-function data together with Planck CMB data to constrain beyond-Standard-Model neutrino self-interactions.
- **Authors:** Ranjini Mondol, Priyank Parashari, Anirban Das, Vera Gluscevic, Ranjan Laha
- **arXiv:** `2611.07013v1` (published 2026-11-04T19:00:02Z; categories: `astro-ph.CO`, `hep-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper is cross-listed between cosmology and particle phenomenology and uses JWST's early-galaxy luminosity function as a new small-scale structure probe alongside Planck. The arXiv abstract says this is the authors' first use of that observable for this constraint; no independent expert discussion or citation momentum was verified for this entry.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report 95% upper limits on log₁₀(Geff/MeV⁻²) of about −2.75 for one interacting neutrino flavor and ≲−3.41 when all three flavors interact, stronger than their Planck-only constraints (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** Observational; cosmological inference combining Planck CMB and JWST high-redshift UV luminosity-function measurements.
- **Caveat:** These are model-dependent parameter constraints, not a detection of neutrino self-interactions; they depend on the assumed neutrino-interaction scenario and interpretation of the high-redshift galaxy luminosity function.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-04 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 7. [Constraining tau-sterile neutrino mixing with high-energy astrophysical neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05170)
- **Abstract:** The authors use IceCube's measured neutrino flavor composition to constrain tau–sterile mixing and project the reach of future neutrino telescopes.
- **Authors:** Mauricio Bustamante, Qinrui Liu
- **arXiv:** `2611.05170v1` (published 2026-11-03T16:38:30Z; categories: `hep-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper was included in the [Global Nu daily neutrino-paper digest](https://global-nu.org/digest.html) and uses astrophysical neutrinos to probe a mixing channel that the authors describe as comparatively weakly constrained by terrestrial measurements. The principal additional signal is its quantitative connection to planned IceCube-Gen2 and HUNT sensitivities.
- **Claimed result:** Fitting IceCube flavor-composition data while leaving the source composition free, the authors obtain |Uτ4|² < 0.88 at 90% confidence; their projected combined sensitivity reaches 0.24–0.29 by 2040 and 0.12–0.14 by 2050 under the stated future-instrument assumptions (arXiv abstract).
- **Evidence type:** Observational; inference from IceCube flavor-composition measurements, followed by future-sensitivity forecasts.
- **Caveat:** The present bound is weak, and projected improvement depends on future detector performance and flavor-composition assumptions; these forecasts are not future measurements.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-03 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Astrophysics

## Trending Research Themes

- **Neutrinos as astrophysical probes of particle physics:** Auger constrains tau-mixing sterile states through Earth-crossing air-shower channels (paper 3); JWST galaxy counts paired with Planck test neutrino self-interactions (paper 6); IceCube flavor data constrain tau–sterile mixing (paper 7). These are complementary analyses at different energies and scales, not independent confirmations of a common signal.
- **Joint cosmological inference and systematic calibration:** DESI DR2's Milky Way mass analysis (paper 4) calibrates multiple dynamical methods on mock surveys, while the standard-siren study (paper 5) combines GW catalogs with galaxy surveys, BAO, supernovae, and CMB datasets. Both illustrate the effort to integrate heterogeneous measurements while modeling cross-method or cross-probe uncertainty.
- **Driven quantum matter and heavy-ion substructure are distinct experimental signals:** TrARPES observes a light-induced graphene gap (paper 1), whereas CMS uses groomed jet emissions to study charm-quark radiation in a quark–gluon plasma (paper 2). Each is a single-paper signal in this week's selected set; they should not be read as a shared experimental trend or as replicated results.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Graphene topology beyond transient spectroscopy (paper 1):** The authors establish a transient gap under the tested pump conditions and infer Floquet topology from spectroscopy plus theory; a direct transport or other topology-sensitive measurement is not reported. A useful follow-up would map the gap and its lifetime across pump frequency, fluence, and helicity, then test for a transport signature while controlling heating and substrate effects.
- **Separating charm-mass and medium effects (paper 2):** The authors report limited model-discriminating power in the measured phase space. Higher-statistics measurements over jet energy, collision centrality, and heavy-flavor species, paired with consistent pp reference data, could test whether medium-induced radiation measurably modifies the dead-cone region.
- **Improving sterile-neutrino reach (paper 3):** The present benchmark fluxes remain below Auger's sensitivity, while tau energy-loss modeling dominates the quoted exposure uncertainty. Follow-up work could refine tau propagation and hadronic-interaction systematics and combine larger exposure across Earth-skimming and upward-going channels; improvements should be reported as sensitivity changes, not detections.
- **Robustness of cosmological mass and tension estimates (papers 4–5):** DESI's joint Milky Way estimate depends on mock-calibrated method performance, and current GW standard sirens do not remove the H₀ or S₈ tensions. Repeating the DESI calibration with expanded mock populations and future survey data, and extending the standard-siren combination as LSST, Euclid, Roman, and GW catalogs grow, would test the stability of both inferences.
- **Astrophysical modeling in neutrino constraints (papers 6–7):** The JWST limit depends on modeling the high-redshift UV luminosity function, while the IceCube mixing bound and future reach depend on flavor-composition and instrument assumptions. Joint analyses with improved galaxy-selection/systematic models, broader neutrino source-flavor priors, and future IceCube-Gen2/HUNT data could test how much of the apparent reach survives those uncertainties.
