### 1. [Singularity of harmonic measures for hyperbolic lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40357)
- **Abstract:** For cocompact hyperbolic lattices containing a convex-cocompact codimension-one subgroup, the author proves singularity of hitting measures for every finitely supported admissible random walk, extending the singularity conjecture to several classes of lattices.[1]
- **Authors:** Nikolay Bogachev
- **arXiv:** `2609.40357v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:59:35 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.GR`, `math.GT`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete conjecture-level result with a four-category mathematical footprint; the paper's introduction states the precise lattice criterion and theorem.[1]
- **Claimed result:** The author proves that each finitely supported admissible walk on the specified lattice has hitting measure singular to visual/Lebesgue measure; the paper also derives the Kaimanovich–Le Prince singularity conjecture for listed classes, including cocompact Kleinian groups.[1]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The lattice is cocompact in `Isom(H^n)`, `n ≥ 2`, and contains a convex-cocompact codimension-one subgroup in Sageev's sense; the walk distribution is finitely supported and admissible.[1]
- **Caveat:** The theorem does not cover every cocompact hyperbolic lattice; the codimension-one subgroup hypothesis is essential to the stated result.[1]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 30 September 2026
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Probability & statistics

### 2. [On Greenberg's conjecture for rational elliptic curves at Eisenstein primes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40282)
- **Abstract:** For rational elliptic curves with ordinary reduction at an odd prime and a rational prime-degree isogeny, the authors prove Greenberg's conjecture on the vanishing of algebraic Iwasawa μ-invariants and confirm a related conjecture for analytic μ-invariants.[2]
- **Authors:** Zichao Lin, Mulun Yin
- **arXiv:** `2609.40282v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:47:52 UTC; categories: `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A clearly stated conjecture resolution in arithmetic number theory, with a second stated consequence through the Iwasawa Main Conjecture; the abstract makes the hypotheses explicit.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove the stated Greenberg conjecture case and, via the Iwasawa Main Conjecture for elliptic curves at Eisenstein primes, confirm Stevens's conjecture on analytic μ-invariants.[2]
- **Assumptions and setting:** `E/Q` has ordinary reduction at an odd prime `p` and admits a rational `p`-isogeny (equivalently, the authors' Eisenstein-prime condition).[2]
- **Caveat:** This is a conditional-on-the-curve-class result, not a theorem for all elliptic curves or all ordinary primes.[2]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 30 September 2026
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory

### 3. [Minimal submanifolds and waists of locally symmetric spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40200)
- **Abstract:** The authors establish higher-dimensional topological expansion for compact locally symmetric manifolds associated with `SL(n,R)` and more general split simple groups, using volume lower bounds for minimal submanifolds, and give a locally symmetric example of power-law systolic freedom.[3]
- **Authors:** Mikołaj Frączyk, Ben Lowe
- **arXiv:** `2609.40200v1` (published 2026-09-30 17:16:38 UTC; categories: `math.DG`, `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract combines higher expansion, minimal-submanifold volume estimates, and a first example claim for this setting, and the work connects differential geometry with combinatorics.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The authors show the relevant compact locally symmetric manifolds form topological higher `d`-expander families for `d ≤ n/8` in the `SL(n,R)` case, extend the result to split simple non-compact real Lie groups for `d` linear in rank, and report the first locally symmetric example of power-law systolic freedom.[3]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Compact locally symmetric manifolds with universal cover the symmetric space of `SL(n,R)` or a split simple non-compact real Lie group; the expansion dimension is bounded as specified in the paper.[3]
- **Caveat:** The results concern the stated families and dimension/rank ranges, not arbitrary locally symmetric spaces; the authors note that the paper partially supersedes earlier work.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 30 September 2026
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 4. [Central Limit Theorems for Finitary Factors of iid Processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35332)
- **Abstract:** For multidimensional random fields that are finitary factors of iid processes, the authors derive central limit theorems with explicit rates and Brownian-sheet weak convergence under decay conditions on the coding-radius tail, with applications to several statistical-mechanics models.[4]
- **Authors:** Raimundo Briceño, Zemer Kosloff, Nicolò Paviato
- **arXiv:** `2609.35332v1` (published 2026-09-28 14:53:39 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math.DS`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper supplies quantitative limit theorems and links probability with dynamics; the abstract identifies concrete model classes as applications.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The authors establish explicit CLT convergence rates and weak convergence to a Brownian sheet for suitably regular observables, with conditions expressed through coding-radius tail decay.[4]
- **Assumptions and setting:** A multidimensional random field is a finitary factor of iid; the observable is suitably regular and the coding-radius tail satisfies the paper's decay conditions.[4]
- **Caveat:** The abstract does not claim a CLT for all finitary factors; rates and convergence depend on the tail and regularity hypotheses.[4]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 28 September 2026
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics

### 5. [Galois groups of random polynomials of large degree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40123)
- **Abstract:** For random monic integer polynomials with independent bounded coefficients, the authors bound the probability of a square discriminant under a Rényi-entropy condition and combine this with prior results to obtain full symmetric Galois groups with high probability under additional hypotheses.[5]
- **Authors:** Guy Blachar, Emmanuel Breuillard, Gady Kozma
- **arXiv:** `2609.40123v1` (published 2026-09-30 16:45:58 UTC; categories: `math.NT`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A quantitative probability estimate feeds into a classical arithmetic question, giving a direct number-theory/probability bridge; the abstract gives a numerical entropy threshold.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove unconditionally that `P(discriminant is a square) = O_μ(1/log n)` when the order-2 Rényi entropy `H₂(μ) > 12`; together with earlier results and additional hypotheses, this yields full `Sym(n)` Galois group with high probability when conditioned on `ω₀ ≠ 0`.[5]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Coefficients are independent, uniformly bounded integer-valued random variables; `ω₁,…,ωₙ₋₁` share a fixed law `μ` with `H₂(μ)>12`; the full-Galois-group conclusion also requires the paper's additional hypotheses.[5]
- **Caveat:** The entropy threshold is a sufficient condition, and the full symmetric-group conclusion is not unconditional on that threshold alone.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 30 September 2026
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Probability & statistics

### 6. [A centerpoint theorem for three planar convex bodies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32953)
- **Abstract:** For three planar convex bodies satisfying a midpoint containment condition, the authors prove a sharp `2/9` halfspace centerpoint guarantee, settling the three-slice case of Oertel's mixed-integer centerpoint conjecture.[6]
- **Authors:** Hongyu Cheng, Amitabh Basu
- **arXiv:** `2609.32953v1` (published 2026-09-26 21:25:00 UTC; categories: `math.MG`, `math.OC`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract states a sharp constant and a resolved finite case of a named conjecture, bridging metric geometry and optimization.[6]
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove that for planar convex bodies `A₀,A₁,A₂` with `½(A₀+A₂) ⊆ A₁`, their three-slice union contains a point for which every closed halfspace containing it captures at least `2/9` of the total area; they call this the best possible constant.[6]
- **Assumptions and setting:** Exactly three planar convex-body slices, with the middle body containing the Minkowski midpoint of the outer two; the conclusion is in `Z × R²`.[6]
- **Caveat:** The theorem handles this three-slice, two-dimensional case; it does not by itself resolve the conjecture in general dimensions or for arbitrary numbers of slices.[6]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 26 September 2026
- **Themes:** Optimization & control; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

## Trending Research Themes

- **Probability across random systems:** The finitary-factor CLT paper and the random-polynomial Galois paper both use probabilistic models to establish asymptotic structure, though their techniques and objects differ.[4][5]
- **Geometry with global structural consequences:** The harmonic-measure paper and the locally symmetric expansion paper both connect geometric hypotheses to global measure or expansion behavior; the first uses boundary/random-walk methods, while the second combines minimal-submanifold estimates and representation-theoretic decay.[1][3]
- **Sharp finite-case geometry for optimization:** The centerpoint result proves the conjectured constant for a specific three-slice setting; it is a focused signal, not evidence of a broader shift by itself.[6]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem suggested by the stated scope:** The harmonic-measure theorem leaves its conjecture beyond cocompact lattices with the specified codimension-one subgroup hypothesis untreated. A next step is to test whether alternative geometric or subgroup hypotheses can cover further cocompact lattices.[1]
- **Research direction:** For Greenberg's result, investigate whether the argument can be extended beyond the rational-isogeny/Eisenstein-prime setting, while separately tracking the ordinary-reduction and Iwasawa-theoretic hypotheses.[2]
- **Research direction:** For the finitary-factor limit theorems, examine whether the coding-radius tail assumptions can be weakened or whether the stated rates are sharp for the Ising, hard-core, and coloring applications.[4]
- **Research direction:** For random polynomials, test whether the Rényi-entropy threshold can be lowered and which additional hypotheses in the full-Galois-group deduction can be removed.[5]
- **Research direction:** Extend the centerpoint method beyond three slices or to higher-dimensional mixed-integer settings, preserving the sharp constant where possible.[6]
