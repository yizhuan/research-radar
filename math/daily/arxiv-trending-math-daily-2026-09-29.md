### 1. [Convex counterexamples to the Schiffer and Pompeiu conjectures in dimensions three, four, six, eight, ten and fourteen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35419)
- **Abstract:** Constructs non-ball convex domains in six specified dimensions with analytic spherical boundaries supporting an overdetermined Helmholtz solution, yielding counterexamples to both conjectures in those dimensions.[2]
- **Authors:** Jizhou Guo
- **arXiv:** `2609.35419v1` (published 2026-09-28 15:30:29 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math.CA`, `math.NA`, `math.SP`)[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** No usable citation or independent-attention signal (Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429). The claimed counterexamples address two longstanding problems, span four math categories, and the paper describes interval-certified finite-dimensional bounds plus analytic tail bounds; this is significance-based, not popularity-based.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The author claims bounded, convex, non-ball domains in dimensions 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 14 for which a nonconstant solution of `Δu+u=0` satisfies `u=1` and `∇u=0` on the boundary; Green’s identity then gives the stated Schiffer/Pompeiu counterexamples.[2]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The domains have real-analytic spherical boundaries; the dimensional list is specific, not a result for all dimensions. The proof uses rank-two compact Lie algebra reductions in dimensions 4, 6, 8, 10, and 14, plus a separate axisymmetric construction in dimension 3.[2]
- **Caveat:** This is a preprint and a computer-assisted proof; the interval bounds, code, and reconstruction of convex domains deserve independent reproduction and expert review. The author’s “first known” priority statements are also author-attributed.[2]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-29 announcement batch (v1; arXiv timestamp 2026-09-28)
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Numerical analysis & computation

### 2. [A proof of the generalized packing-covering conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34910)
- **Abstract:** Claims a computer-assisted proof, for all linear codes over finite fields and every admissible order, of `d_t(C) ≤ 2R_t(C)+2`.[3]
- **Authors:** Gianira N. Alfarano, Giuseppe Marino, Alessandro Neri, Rocco Trombetti
- **arXiv:** `2609.34910v1` (published 2026-09-28 11:12:15 UTC; categories: `cs.IT`, `math.CO`)[3]
- **Evidence for ranking:** No usable citation or independent-attention signal (Semantic Scholar HTTP 429). The claim covers every finite field and admissible order and combines general reductions with exact checking of the remaining finite cases; it also bridges coding theory and combinatorics.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The authors claim the generalized packing-covering inequality for all linear codes, using a parity-check reformulation, length bounds, successive puncturing, and exact computer verification of the residual finite cases.[3]
- **Assumptions and setting:** `C` is a linear code over any finite field and `t` is any admissible order; the claimed inequality compares its `t`th generalized Hamming weight and generalized covering radius.[3]
- **Caveat:** The abstract says all orders `t ≥ 32` are handled by reductions and the smaller orders by a finite exact computation. Reproducibility and independent checking of the computation are central; this remains an unreviewed preprint claim.[3]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-29 announcement batch (v1; arXiv timestamp 2026-09-28)
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 3. [Complete classification of 7-adic Galois images for non-CM elliptic curves over Q](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35460)
- **Abstract:** Claims to settle a conjecture of Furio and Lombardo and thereby complete the classification of 7-adic Galois images for non-CM elliptic curves over `Q`.[4]
- **Authors:** Nguyen Xuan Tho
- **arXiv:** `2609.35460v1` (published 2026-09-28 15:48:56 UTC; categories: `math.NT`)[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** No usable citation or independent-attention signal (Semantic Scholar HTTP 429). The stated consequence is a field-specific classification result resolving an explicit prior conjecture; the paper supplies extensive computational data and checks, but popularity is unmeasured.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The author claims the relevant rational points on a genus-3 modular curve are exactly four specified points, which—combined with cited work—excludes two candidate image groups and completes the classification.[4]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The paper’s main theorem assumes the Jacobian of the curve has Mordell–Weil rank 3; it identifies a cited fake 2-Selmer computation as establishing that hypothesis. The argument uses a Mordell–Weil sieve and `p`-adic heights.[4]
- **Caveat:** The rank premise and computational arithmetic need independent verification. The abstract discloses that the author used a long AI-training process; that is not itself evidence for or against correctness, but the claimed theorem should be checked on its mathematics and computations.[4]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-29 announcement batch (v1; arXiv timestamp 2026-09-28)
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory

### 4. [Very sharp distance and range transitions for random walk bridges on Ramanujan graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35213)
- **Abstract:** Finds a bounded critical window in which the maximum distance of a logarithmic-length random-walk bridge changes from order `√log N` to order `log N`, alongside a two-point limiting law for normalized range.[5]
- **Authors:** Itai Benjamini
- **arXiv:** `2609.35213v1` (published 2026-09-28 14:03:32 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math.CO`)[5]
- **Evidence for ranking:** No usable citation or independent-attention signal (Semantic Scholar HTTP 429). The precise transition and limiting-law claims connect probability with graph combinatorics; ranking is based on the abstract’s concrete theorem claims and cross-listing only.[5]
- **Claimed result:** For vertex-transitive Ramanujan graphs with logarithmic girth, the paper claims a sharp distance transition and a two-point limiting law for the normalized range in the same `O(1)` window.[5]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The graph sequence is vertex-transitive and Ramanujan with logarithmic girth; bridge length is of order `log N`, where `N` is the graph size.[5]
- **Caveat:** Do not generalize this to arbitrary expanders or random walks: the graph hypotheses are restrictive, and the abstract does not state the full constants or all technical conditions.[5]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-29 announcement batch (v1; arXiv timestamp 2026-09-28)
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 5. [Qin’s quasimodularity conjecture for Hilbert schemes of points](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33884)
- **Abstract:** Claims the predicted quasimodularity and mixed-weight bound for reduced generating series of tautological-bundle intersection numbers on Hilbert schemes of points, using a trace formula for Nakajima operators.[6]
- **Authors:** Victor Alekseev, Avik Chakravarty, Daebeom Choi, Shengjing Xu
- **arXiv:** `2609.33884v1` (published 2026-09-27 19:59:20 UTC; categories: `math.AG`)[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** No usable citation or independent-attention signal (Semantic Scholar HTTP 429). The authors state a resolution of a named conjecture and provide a new operator-trace method; the abstract explicitly reports GPT-5.6 Sol assistance. This is a claim-strength ranking, not an attention ranking.[6]
- **Claimed result:** The authors claim Qin’s conjecture for smooth projective complex surfaces with numerically trivial canonical class, with the predicted mixed-weight bound, and give an algorithm for general reduced generating series.[6]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The surface `X` is smooth and projective over `C` and has numerically trivial canonical class; the objects are tautological bundles and intersection series on `X^[n]`.[6]
- **Caveat:** The theorem is limited to that surface class. The work is explicitly AI-assisted and has not been independently assessed here; the new trace argument and weight bookkeeping warrant specialist review.[6]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-29 announcement batch (v1; arXiv timestamp 2026-09-27)
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology

- **Abstract:** Gives a counterexample to a strengthened Hadwiger-type statement requiring every vertex in each later branch set to connect to each earlier branch set, using a complement of a pseudorandom triangle-free graph.[7]
- **Authors:** Freddie Illingworth, Raphael Steiner
- **arXiv:** `2609.35361v1` (published 2026-09-28 15:07:34 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)[7]
- **Evidence for ranking:** No usable citation or independent-attention signal (Semantic Scholar HTTP 429). It targets a recent, strictly stronger conjecture and states a specific pseudorandom construction; the proof is described by the authors as an exposition of an AI-discovered argument, not as a result about classical Hadwiger’s conjecture.[7]
- **Claimed result:** The authors claim the dominating Hadwiger conjecture is false; the abstract identifies the construction as the complement of a suitably sampled block-geometric triangle-free graph.[7]
- **Assumptions and setting:** The statement concerns graphs with chromatic number at least `t` and the stronger “dominating `K_t`-model” condition; it does not disprove the original Hadwiger conjecture.[7]
- **Caveat:** The abstract says ChatGPT 6 Astra Ultra found the disproof and the authors provide their exposition. The argument is a preprint claim and should be independently checked; careful distinction from the original Hadwiger conjecture is essential.[7]
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-09-29 announcement batch (v1; arXiv timestamp 2026-09-28)
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics
