## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [The closure of the nonreal roots of bounded-height polynomials is connected exactly at pronic heights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14517)
- **Abstract:** For the closure of nonreal roots of integer polynomials with coefficients bounded in modulus by an integer height, the authors prove connectedness holds exactly at pronic heights and establish extensive component results at other heights.
- **Authors:** Bernat Espigule
- **arXiv:** `2611.14517v1` (published 2026-11-10T18:58:59Z; categories: `math.CV`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper states a complete arithmetic classification of connectedness and resolves named prior observations/conjectures at particular heights; it is cross-listed between complex analysis and number theory and includes a substantial computational supplement archived at Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24117729). A Wolfram MathWorld entry also summarizes the pronic-height criterion.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves the nonreal-root closure is connected iff the height is q(q−1), q≥2; otherwise it has at least five components. The abstract also reports infinitely many components in broad ranges and resolves specific double-zero conjectures.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Integer polynomials of arbitrary degree with each coefficient in absolute value at most an integer h≥1; the set is the closure in the complex plane of their nonreal roots.
- **Caveat:** This is a long new preprint (45 pages plus a 35-page supplement), not yet an independently reviewed theorem; some exceptional nonpronic heights remain unresolved in the authors’ component-count statements.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Wed, 11 Nov 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Algebra & number theory

### 2. [Phase transitions in the hard-wall large deviations for the Gaussian free field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14448)
- **Abstract:** For the discrete Gaussian free field in dimension d≥3 constrained above a moving hard wall on a dilated bounded domain, the authors identify a critical window where collective displacement and suppression of local violations contribute together to a sharp variational rate.
- **Authors:** Xinyi Li, Jialu Shi
- **arXiv:** `2611.14448v1` (published 2026-11-10T18:18:11Z; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper gives a sharp asymptotic variational formula in a precisely specified transition window, derives matching off-window regimes, and connects the result to random-interlacement cover levels; the probability and mathematical-physics cross-list reflects this bridge.
- **Claimed result:** For h_N(β)^2=g_0(4 log N+log log N+2β), with β_N→β, the log probability of the hard-wall event, normalized by N^(d−2)/log N, converges to the negative infimum of a Dirichlet-energy plus exponential-potential functional. The paper also gives moving-speed asymptotics on either side of the window.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Centered GFF on Z^d, d≥3, with covariance the simple-random-walk Green function; D is a bounded Lipschitz domain and D_N=(ND)∩Z^d.
- **Caveat:** The theorem concerns d≥3 and the stated domain/scaling regime; the paper identifies sharp rates for the Gaussian field but leaves the analogous critical-window rate for random interlacements open.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Wed, 11 Nov 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics

### 3. [The distribution of small gaps between zeros of the Riemann zeta function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14486)
- **Abstract:** Using an unconditional variant of Montgomery’s pair-correlation method, the author improves the explicit constant in Selberg’s theorem on the proportion of consecutive zeta zeros with unusually small gaps, showing that λ₀=0.7892 is admissible.
- **Authors:** Youness Lamzouri
- **arXiv:** `2611.14486v1` (published 2026-11-10T18:46:37Z; categories: `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract gives a concrete improvement over the best explicit constant cited there (1−2.36×10⁻⁶) and does so unconditionally; the result is directly checkable against the earlier bounds it names.
- **Claimed result:** For a positive proportion of consecutive nontrivial zeta zeros, their ordinate gap is at most 2πλ₀/log γ_n with λ₀=0.7892<4/5, unconditionally.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Nontrivial zeros in the upper half-plane, counted with multiplicity; the result is unconditional and concerns a positive proportion, not every gap.
- **Caveat:** This is a new preprint establishing an admissible bound for a positive proportion; it does not determine the full gap distribution or assert the bound for all consecutive zeros.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Wed, 11 Nov 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory

### 4. [No 13-vertex triangulation of the three-torus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14367)
- **Abstract:** The author rules out a simplicial triangulation of the three-torus with 13 vertices, narrowing the minimum possible number of vertices to 14 or 15.
- **Authors:** Geunho Lim
- **arXiv:** `2611.14367v1` (published 2026-11-10T17:23:48Z; categories: `math.GT`, `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It closes the first unresolved vertex count after the existing lower bound of 13, uses a combination of face-number inequalities, fundamental groups and Alexander duality, and is cross-listed in topology and combinatorics. Wolfram MathWorld’s three-torus entry also records the resulting 14–15 range.
- **Claimed result:** The paper proves that no simplicial triangulation of T³ has 13 vertices; together with cited existing bounds and a 15-vertex construction, this yields 14≤v(T³)≤15.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite simplicial triangulations of the closed three-torus; the contradiction uses the Novik–Swartz g₂ inequality and properties of small simplicial complexes.
- **Caveat:** The argument excludes 13 vertices but does not decide whether a 14-vertex triangulation exists or whether the known 15-vertex construction is minimal.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Wed, 11 Nov 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 5. [Regularity of Q-valued C¹ harmonic functions with applications to stable minimal hypersurfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14409)
- **Abstract:** The author proves Hölder regularity and branch-set structure for Q-valued C¹ functions harmonic on their regular set, then applies these results to stable minimal hypersurfaces under small-slope and singular-set hypotheses.
- **Authors:** Sidney Stanbury
- **arXiv:** `2611.14409v1` (published 2026-11-10T17:52:13Z; categories: `math.DG`, `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract makes several specific regularity and rectifiability claims and applies them across geometric measure theory and elliptic analysis; the differential-geometry/PDE cross-list signals the mathematical bridge.
- **Claimed result:** The derivative of a Q-valued C¹ function locally harmonic off its branch set is locally Dirichlet-minimizing, with the same branch set; consequently the function is C¹,α and its branch set is countably (n−2)-rectifiable for n≥3 and discrete for n=2. A small-Lipschitz multivalued graph satisfying the stated stable-hypersurface hypotheses is also C¹,α.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Locally harmonicity is required on the regular set. The hypersurface application assumes a two-sided, properly immersed stable minimal graph, locally finite H^(n−2) singular measure, and sufficiently small Lipschitz constant.
- **Caveat:** The conclusions depend on the stated multivalued and stability hypotheses; they do not assert regularity for arbitrary singular minimal hypersurfaces.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Wed, 11 Nov 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Analysis & PDE

### 6. [Local Rigidity of Bergman Logarithmic Flatness on Compact Hermitian Symmetric Spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14507)
- **Abstract:** Near the standard invariant positive polarizations on compact Hermitian symmetric spaces, the author proves that sufficiently small smooth deformations preserving Bergman logarithmic flatness arise only from holomorphic automorphisms and constant rescaling.
- **Authors:** Zhiqin Lu, Yuang Shi
- **arXiv:** `2611.14507v1` (published 2026-11-10T18:55:40Z; categories: `math.DG`, `math.CV`, `math.RT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper proves a local rigidity theorem across differential geometry, complex analysis and representation theory, and states a finite-coefficient criterion for rigidity near the standard metric rather than requiring the entire infinite flatness condition.
- **Claimed result:** For a fixed standard polarization on a compact Hermitian symmetric space, sufficiently small deformations satisfying vanishing of a finite consecutive block of canonically twisted Bergman coefficients are the pullback of the reference metric by a polarized holomorphic automorphism, up to a positive constant.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Compact Hermitian symmetric space with fixed invariant positive polarization; the deformation remains positive and small in a suitable C^(s,α) norm, and a sufficiently long finite block of Bergman coefficients vanishes.
- **Caveat:** The theorem is local and relies on the symmetric-space setting and fixed polarization; the authors conjecture an extension to general compact polarized Kähler manifolds.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Wed, 11 Nov 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Analysis & PDE

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem (author-stated):** Is the minimum number of vertices in a simplicial triangulation of T³ 14 or 15? Lim excludes 13 but does not settle 14-vertex existence. [Lim, arXiv:2611.14367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14367)
- **Open problem (author-stated):** Does a two-dimensional analogue of the GFF hard-wall transition window exist, and what is its sharp rate? Li and Shi also leave the predicted critical-window rate for random interlacements and its cover-time counterpart open. [Li and Shi, arXiv:2611.14448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14448)
- **Research direction (synthesis):** Test the GFF variational mechanism under other covariance kernels or random conductances, preserving the competition between macroscopic energy and local violations and checking which assumptions control the critical centering. [Li and Shi, arXiv:2611.14448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14448)
- **Open problem (author-stated):** Does local Bergman logarithmic-flatness rigidity extend from compact Hermitian symmetric spaces to general compact polarized Kähler manifolds? The paper formulates this as a conjecture. [Lu and Shi, arXiv:2611.14507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14507)
- **Research direction (synthesis):** Seek a constructive or computational classification of the remaining square-adjacent heights in Espigule’s exceptional ranges, to distinguish finite from infinite component behavior beyond the cases settled in the preprint. [Espigule, arXiv:2611.14517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14517)
