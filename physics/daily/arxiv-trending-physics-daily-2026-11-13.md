## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Observation of Genuine Multi-Time Quantum Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17268)
- **Abstract:** The authors derive a three-time witness for quantum memory that pairwise tests miss, and demonstrate the hierarchy in a time-continuous superconducting-qubit experiment.
- **Authors:** Konstantin Beyer, Valentin Link, Surihan Sean Borjigin, Qian Cao, Orion Lee, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.17268v1` (published 2026-11-12 18:46:04 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A new analytical witness is tested experimentally on a superconducting-qubit platform, giving a concrete result that links quantum-information criteria to open-system dynamics; the paper is unusually specific about why pairwise tests fail.
- **Claimed result:** The authors show a minimal qubit example whose every pair of times has a classical-memory explanation, while a joint three-time test certifies quantum memory.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; time-continuous superconducting-qubit realization with time-independent couplings, supported by an analytical witness and channel tomography.
- **Caveat:** The authors leave open how much further information can be extracted from the full continuous dynamics; this is a proof-of-principle platform demonstration, not a general scaling result.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Fri, 13 Nov 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Quantum information

### 2. [JWST Rocky Worlds DDT Observations of LTT 1445 A b and c in Secondary Eclipse Using MIRI/F1500W](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17300)
- **Abstract:** Three JWST/MIRI eclipse visits, combined with prior spectra and radial velocities, indicate that nearby rocky planet LTT 1445 A b has no substantial atmosphere, while the sibling planet c remains weakly constrained.
- **Authors:** Jegug Ih, Shubham Kanodia, Nicholas Wogan, Mark Fortune, Natalie H. Allen, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.17300v1` (published 2026-11-12 18:55:27 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.EP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** Three independent reductions and a joint analysis strengthen the 15-μm eclipse constraint; the result also received independent specialist discussion and science-media coverage shortly after posting.
- **Claimed result:** The authors infer a CO₂ partial pressure below 0.6 mbar and surface pressure below about 0.1 bar for planet b; the eclipse of c is too uncertain to establish its atmosphere.
- **Evidence type:** observational; JWST MIRI/F1500W secondary eclipses, prior JWST spectra, and a joint fit including 220 radial velocities.
- **Caveat:** Planet c’s potentially grazing eclipse and overlapping events leave its eclipse depth poorly determined; planet b’s atmospheric limits depend on the retrieval and forward-model assumptions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Fri, 13 Nov 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics

### 3. [Observation of the $\Lambda(1520)$ suppression in Pb-Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}} = 5.02$ TeV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17010)
- **Abstract:** ALICE reports a central-to-peripheral suppression of the $\Lambda(1520)/\Lambda$ yield ratio in Pb-Pb collisions and compares the trend with statistical-hadronization and hydrodynamic-plus-transport models.
- **Authors:** ALICE Collaboration
- **arXiv:** `2611.17010v1` (published 2026-11-12 16:07:03 UTC; categories: `nucl-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors report a 6.8σ result and describe it as the first observation of resonance suppression within one collision system; the paper is submitted to PRL and tests late-stage transport predictions against the measured centrality trend.
- **Claimed result:** The measured suppression fraction is $0.589 \pm 0.015^{stat} \pm 0.086^{syst}$; the authors find MUSIC coupled to the SMASH hadronic afterburner reproduces the trend within uncertainties.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; ALICE Pb-Pb collision data at $\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.02$ TeV, measured across six centrality classes and compared with models.
- **Caveat:** The role of late-stage rescattering is inferred through model comparison rather than isolated experimentally; the suppression measurement carries an 8.6% systematic uncertainty.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Fri, 13 Nov 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 4. [The full Gaia colour-magnitude diagram with white dwarfs reveals a declining Milky Way star formation history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17337)
- **Abstract:** A self-consistent population-synthesis fit to Gaia DR3 main-sequence and white-dwarf stars favors an early-peaking, subsequently declining local Milky Way star-formation history with two recent bursts.
- **Authors:** K. Sysoliatina, Akash Vani
- **arXiv:** `2611.17337v1` (published 2026-11-12 18:59:56 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.SR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The analysis jointly fits 7.98 million Gaia DR3 stars, including 110,000 white dwarfs, and reports substantially improved agreement across the full colour-magnitude diagram; it cross-links Galactic and stellar astrophysics and has specialist discussion.
- **Claimed result:** The authors’ 18-parameter CALOMERA fit reproduces counts within 3% in each colour-magnitude region and recovers a declining star-formation history with bursts near 2.4 and 0.5 Gyr ago.
- **Evidence type:** observational; Gaia DR3 photometry and astrometry, interpreted with a semi-analytic chemo-dynamical population model and three white-dwarf cooling libraries.
- **Caveat:** The inferred early history remains conditional on stellar-evolution and cooling prescriptions; the paper reports faint-end discrepancies and notes that binary evolution is not included.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Fri, 13 Nov 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

- **Caveat:** This is a calibration and data-readiness paper, not a new dark-energy constraint; downstream scientific accuracy remains dependent on calibration performance.

### 6. [The Gator low-background spectrometer: readout upgrade and photomultiplier tube radioassay for XLZD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17187)
- **Abstract:** An upgraded Gator high-purity germanium spectrometer adds waveform-level noise rejection and reports radioassay results for photomultiplier tubes intended as XLZD candidates.

- **Claimed result:** The authors report 2.14 ± 0.22 keV FWHM at 2614.5 keV and a 238U/232Th activity reduction by a factor of three in the tested improved-stem tubes.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; low-background high-purity germanium measurements at Gran Sasso, including radioassay of 16 Hamamatsu R17317 photomultiplier tubes.
- **Caveat:** The stated sensitivity is for a specified sample geometry, and the tubes are candidate photosensors; the report does not establish the background performance of a complete XLZD detector.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Fri, 13 Nov 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Instrumentation & detectors; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

- **Caveat:** The efficiency range applies to the plateau region and the reported 2026 improvements were evaluated in selected test chains, not presented as a full future-trigger deployment.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, Fri, 13 Nov 2026 announcement batch

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Quantum memory — author-stated open question:** The paper asks how much additional memory information is accessible from the full continuous dynamics beyond the three-time witness (#1). **Research direction (synthesis):** test the witness hierarchy under controlled decoherence and in larger systems, then compare finite-time witnesses with full process-tensor reconstructions.
- **Rocky-planet atmospheres — author-stated limitation:** LTT 1445 A c remains too weakly constrained because its eclipse may be grazing and overlaps other events; for b, thin or transparent atmospheres remain possible (#2). **Research direction (synthesis):** combine forthcoming transmission observations with repeat eclipse measurements, and extend the comparison to rocky planets on the predicted atmosphere-retaining side of the Cosmic Shoreline.
- **Late-stage heavy-ion dynamics — paper-based question:** ALICE’s result favors a transport description with rescattering and regeneration, but a single resonance and collision system cannot isolate all late-hadronic contributions (#3). **Research direction (synthesis):** measure additional short-lived resonances across centrality and collision energy and compare them with transport models under varied rescattering/regeneration assumptions.
- **Galactic star-formation history — author-stated systematics:** The Gaia fit has unresolved faint-end residuals and omits binary evolution; the early-history amplitude depends on stellar-evolution and cooling assumptions (#4). **Research direction (synthesis):** add binary populations and improve faint-end selection modeling, then cross-check with volume-limited white-dwarf samples and independent empirical initial-final mass constraints.
- **Survey and detector calibration — author-stated next work:** Euclid identifies calibration optimizations for future releases (#5), while ATLAS reports improvements only in selected test chains (#7); Gator’s assay concerns a specified sample geometry (#6). **Research direction (synthesis):** track calibration residuals across observing conditions and detector position, validate trigger changes on larger collision samples, and extend radioassay/sensitivity studies to representative full-detector configurations.
