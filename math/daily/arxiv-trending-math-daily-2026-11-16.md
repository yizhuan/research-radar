## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [On the pure part of the cohomology of character varieties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18560)
- **Abstract:** For twisted genus-g GL(r) character varieties, the authors prove the Hausel–Rodriguez-Villegas purity conjecture, expressing the graded dimension of pure cohomology through the Kac polynomial of a one-vertex, g-loop quiver.
- **Authors:** Olivier Schiffmann, Eric Vasserot
- **arXiv:** `2611.18560v1` (published 2026-11-13 18:58:59 UTC; categories: `math.AG`, `math.RT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A named purity conjecture is stated as proved, with an explicit quiver/Kac-polynomial formula; the paper is cross-listed in algebraic geometry and representation theory, and its introduction and main theorem were inspected.
- **Claimed result:** The authors' Theorem 1.3 proves the purity conjecture and gives the pure part's graded dimension in terms of the Kac polynomial for the one-vertex quiver with g loops.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The main setup is a twisted GL(r) character variety for a smooth compact surface of genus g at least 2, with r positive and d coprime to r.
- **Caveat:** The result concerns the pure part, not the full mixed Hodge polynomial, and its proof composes several recent P=W, P=C, and BPS-theoretic results.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Geometry & topology

### 2. [A proof of the Jamneshan-Tao conjecture by embedding nilspaces in nilmanifolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17567)
- **Abstract:** Every compact finite-rank nilspace embeds into a toral nilspace of the same step, and the authors use this to prove the Jamneshan-Tao conjecture and obtain a Gowers-norm inverse theorem for second-countable compact abelian groups.
- **Authors:** Pablo Candela, Diego González-Sánchez, Balázs Szegedy
- **arXiv:** `2611.17567v1` (published 2026-11-12 22:46:05 UTC; categories: `math.GR`, `math.CO`, `math.DS`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper claims a proof of a named higher-order Fourier-analysis conjecture; its toral-embedding theorem is the stated structural step, with relevance across group theory, combinatorics, and dynamics. The introduction and theorem statements were inspected.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove that each k-step compact finite-rank nilspace embeds continuously into a k-step toral nilspace, then derive the conjectured inverse theorem with connected filtered nilmanifold targets.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The inverse theorem is for 1-bounded Borel functions on second-countable compact Hausdorff abelian groups with a Gowers U^(k+1) norm bounded below by a positive parameter.
- **Caveat:** The conclusion is qualitative: the finite target family and constants depend on k and the norm threshold, with no effective bounds stated in the abstract; the proof uses the general nilspace inverse theorem.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Geometry & topology

### 3. [Sharp Finite-Time Blow-Up for Unforced Axisymmetric Euler Without Swirl via Multiscale Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18151)
- **Abstract:** The authors construct compactly supported vorticity for an unforced, axisymmetric three-dimensional Euler flow without swirl that blows up in finite time, with initial velocity simultaneously in every C^(1,alpha) class for alpha below 1/3.
- **Authors:** Jeaheang Bang, Alexey Cheskidov
- **arXiv:** `2611.18151v1` (published 2026-11-13 13:28:08 UTC; categories: `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors state a sharp near-critical regularity result and give a specific multiscale blow-up mechanism; the main theorem and its hypotheses were checked in the paper's introduction.
- **Claimed result:** The authors' Theorem 1.1 constructs finite-energy initial data whose classical solution has a non-integrable-in-time vorticity supremum before a finite time T*.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The flow is unforced, axisymmetric, without swirl, on R^3; the initial velocity is smooth away from the origin, belongs to the intersection of C^(1,alpha) for 0<alpha<1/3, and has compactly supported vorticity.
- **Caveat:** The initial data is not smooth at the origin and the construction has no swirl; it does not establish blow-up from smooth data, which the authors identify as an open direction for axisymmetric Euler with swirl.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 4. [Matrix ultraproducts have trivial fundamental group](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18328)
- **Abstract:** The authors claim that every tracial ultraproduct of complex matrix algebras has trivial fundamental group, implying uncountably many nonisomorphic such ultraproducts and answering a question of Sorin Popa.
- **Authors:** Hiroshi Ando, Isaac Goldbring
- **arXiv:** `2611.18328v1` (published 2026-11-13 15:40:56 UTC; categories: `math.OA`, `math.LO`, `math.RT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It states a resolution of a named operator-algebra question and a stronger first-order variant, with cross-listing in operator algebras, logic, and representation theory.
- **Claimed result:** The authors' abstract asserts triviality of the fundamental group for every tracial matrix ultraproduct and of its first-order fundamental group.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The objects are tracial ultraproducts of sequences of full matrix algebras M_n(C), with the fundamental group notions used in operator-algebra/model-theoretic settings.
- **Caveat:** The result is stated for matrix ultraproducts, not all II_1 factors; the arXiv comment calls this a first draft and invites comments.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Logic & foundations

### 5. [A counterexample to Arveson's finiteness question for subdiagonal algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17636)
- **Abstract:** The authors construct a maximal subdiagonal algebra in a type-II_1 von Neumann factor with scalar diagonal and uncountably many faithful normal conditional expectations, most of which are not preserved by any nonzero finite trace.
- **Authors:** Guoxing Ji
- **arXiv:** `2611.17636v1` (published 2026-11-13 01:10:37 UTC; categories: `math.OA`, `math.FA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract gives a concrete negative answer to Arveson's named finiteness question and an explicit operator-algebra construction, spanning operator algebras and functional analysis.
- **Claimed result:** The authors' construction shows that only the trace-associated expectation preserves the normalized trace, while the other expectations are not preserved by any nonzero finite trace, answering the 1967 question negatively.
- **Assumptions and setting:** A unital sigma-weakly closed subalgebra of a type-II_1 factor is considered, with scalar diagonal equal to the complex multiples of the identity.
- **Caveat:** The abstract supplies a counterexample to the finiteness question, not a classification of all subdiagonal algebras or their conditional expectations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory; Analysis & PDE

### 6. [Smale's mean value constant (1981) equals 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17373)
- **Abstract:** For the normalized complex polynomials in Smale's mean-value problem, the author proves that the supremum of the degree-wise constants is 4 and that the constants tend to 4 as degree grows, with a Lean 4 formalization of the main result.
- **Authors:** Dave Goel
- **arXiv:** `2611.17373v1` (published 2026-11-10 13:27:58 UTC; categories: `math.CV`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract settles the sharp universal constant and gives an explicit construction; a Lean 4 proof artifact is linked in the arXiv comments.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves sup_d K(d)=4 and K(d) tends to 4, showing that Smale's upper bound cannot be replaced by any smaller uniform constant.
- **Assumptions and setting:** For complex polynomials p of degree d at least 2 with p(0)=0 and p'(0) nonzero, K(d) is defined from the minimum over the critical points of p.
- **Caveat:** Each individual K(d) remains strictly below 4, so the supremum is approached only as degree tends to infinity and is not attained at a finite degree.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

- **Claimed result:** The author's abstract claims that the four-dimensional Borsuk number is either five or six: six always suffice, and there are sets for which fewer than five parts cannot suffice.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The problem concerns partitioning sets of diameter one in R^4 into subsets each of strictly smaller diameter.

### 8. [No free lattice contains an infinite ascending chain of copies of FL(3)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18541)

- **arXiv:** `2611.18541v1` (published 2026-11-13 18:49:26 UTC; categories: `math.RA`, `cs.LO`, `math.GR`, `math.LO`)

- **Claimed result:** The authors rule out the infinite chain in free lattices, while showing that without freeness FL(3) does contain an infinite ascending chain of 3-generated sublattices.
- **Assumptions and setting:** The formal statement is for free lattices over generators with decidable equality; the authors report using Agda's safe flags and no classical principle.
- **Caveat:** The chain prohibition depends on freeness and is specific to copies of FL(3); the contrasting nonfree example shows the freeness hypothesis cannot simply be dropped.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-16

- **Claimed result:** The author claims an upper bound e^(O(sqrt(n))) for the nth Ulam number, replacing the previous exponential upper bound.

These are author-stated preprint claims, not independent validations.
- **Formal proof artifacts appear across distinct areas, rather than as one mathematical method.** The Borsuk partition result and Smale constant result include Lean formalizations, while the free-lattice theorem is checked in Agda (Chalmers; Goel; DeMeo–Freese). The shared signal is machine-checkable proof development, not a common analytic technique.
- **Geometric structures connect algebra, analysis, and combinatorics.** Nilspace embeddings into filtered nilmanifolds turn into a Gowers-norm inverse theorem (Candela et al.); quiver data controls pure character-variety cohomology (Schiffmann–Vasserot); and Borsuk partitions use cones, balls, and rational polytopes (Chalmers). This is a set of concrete bridges, not evidence of an archive-wide shift.
- **Several papers isolate thresholds or equality regimes.** The Euler construction approaches the C^(1,1/3) boundary from below (Bang–Cheskidov), the Smale constants tend to 4 without reaching it at finite degree (Goel), and the interchange-process paper classifies equality configurations (Chen). These results have different tools and settings; the commonality is the precision of the boundary statement.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated open problem:** Bang and Cheskidov identify unforced finite-time blow-up from smooth data in axisymmetric Euler with swirl as open. A follow-up could test whether the paper's angular-redistribution and forward-cascade estimates can be adapted to smooth axisymmetric data or to the swirl term; this is a proposed extension, not a result of the paper. [Bang–Cheskidov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18151)
- **Author-stated unresolved alternative:** Chalmers narrows the four-dimensional Borsuk number to five or six but does not determine which value is correct. Tightening the intermediate-circumradius partition argument or producing a counterexample to five parts would resolve the remaining gap. [Chalmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17375)
- **Scope gap and proposed direction:** Schiffmann and Vasserot determine the pure part of the mixed Hodge structure; deriving further mixed-weight information beyond that pure contribution would extend the reported formula. [Schiffmann–Vasserot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18560)
- **Author-reported validation need:** Kravitz says the proofs of the Ulam bound are due to ChatGPT. Independent verification of the covering lemma and its use in the recurrence is a prerequisite before relying on the claimed asymptotic bound. [Kravitz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17584)
- **Synthesis from the stated bounds:** For directed-landscape paths the exceptional-set dimension is bounded above by 57/61 and shown attainable at least to 1/3. Sharpening either side, or constructing paths approaching the upper bound, would test the gap. [Agarwal–Sarkar–Virág](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18360)
- **Synthesis from the result's scope:** The Jamneshan–Tao inverse theorem supplies existence of finite target families and constants depending on k and the Gowers-norm threshold; effective bounds for these quantities would strengthen its qualitative conclusion. [Candela–González-Sánchez–Szegedy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17567)
