## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Nonuniqueness of Solutions to 2D Incompressible Euler Equations with Unbounded Vorticity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37653)
- **Abstract:** The author constructs two distinct finite-energy solutions of the planar incompressible Euler equation from the same compactly supported, mean-zero initial vorticity lying in every finite L^p space, using a cascade of quadrupole transitions across scales.
- **Authors:** Hyungjun Choi
- **arXiv:** `2610.37653v1` (published 2026-10-27 17:58:26 UTC; categories: `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract and inspected main theorem state a concrete nonuniqueness result at a delicate regularity boundary; the multiscale construction makes the claimed mechanism unusually specific. Its potential significance, rather than demonstrated uptake, drives this placement.
- **Claimed result:** The author states that two distinct global solutions share the same initial vorticity, with vorticity in every finite L^p at all times and velocity in C([0,∞);L^2); their L^1 difference is positive at time 1.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Unforced 2D Euler on R^2; compactly supported, mean-zero initial vorticity in the intersection of all finite L^p spaces. The stated solutions conserve each finite-p vorticity norm.
- **Caveat:** “Every finite L^p” does not mean L^∞ or bounded vorticity. The author says the construction does not establish sharpness of the quantitative Yudovich uniqueness criterion; the proof has not been independently checked here.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 2. [Local Law and Eigenvector Delocalization for Random Lifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37463)
- **Abstract:** For random N-lifts of a fixed finite graph of minimum degree at least three, the authors prove high-probability entrywise Green-function estimates down to logarithmic-over-N scales away from singular energies, and deduce complete eigenvector delocalization there.
- **Authors:** Jiaoyang Huang, Ruizhe Xu
- **arXiv:** `2610.37463v1` (published 2026-10-27 16:00:41 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`, `math.CO`, `math.SP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A quantitative local law and a uniform eigenvector conclusion are paired in one result, with cross-listing across probability, mathematical physics, combinatorics, and spectral theory. The inspected introduction identifies the result as covering the bulk, regular edges, and regular cusps for general fixed base graphs.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove Green-function control at scales (log N)^C/N and an eigenvector sup-norm bound (log N)^C/√N with high probability.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Uniformly sampled N-lifts of a fixed finite connected simple base graph with minimum degree at least three; eigenvalues must remain a fixed positive distance from singular energies of the universal-cover spectrum.
- **Caveat:** Singular energies are excluded, and claims are for fixed base graphs; the abstract does not assert a bound at the excluded energies.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Mathematical physics

### 3. [All Strict Calabi--Yau Threefolds of Height One Lift to Characteristic 0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37075)
- **Abstract:** The author proves that every strict Calabi–Yau threefold of Artin–Mazur height one over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic has a projective lift to characteristic zero, even with crystalline torsion and arbitrary characteristic, and gives a counterexample to an unobstructedness conjecture.
- **Authors:** Matthew Ward
- **arXiv:** `2610.37075v1` (published 2026-10-27 11:37:33 UTC; categories: `math.AG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The inspected introduction states both a general height-one lifting theorem and an explicit counterexample to a conjecture, while distinguishing liftability from unobstructedness. The result has a crisp scope and a concrete follow-up frontier.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves projective liftability to W(k) for strict height-one Calabi–Yau threefolds and shows that an Addington–Bragg example has W₂-lifts that fail to extend to W₃.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Strict Calabi–Yau threefold over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic p>0, with Artin–Mazur height one; no torsion-freeness assumption on crystalline cohomology.
- **Caveat:** The theorem gives existence of a lift, not unobstructedness of the whole deformation space or a canonical lift. The author identifies height two and dimensions at least four as open extensions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology

### 4. [The sharp exponent for the Banach--Mazur distance to the cube](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36917)
- **Abstract:** The author proves a lower bound of order n^(2/3)(log(2n))^(-10) for the maximal Banach–Mazur distance from an n-dimensional real normed space to ℓ∞^n, which matches a previously known upper bound at the exponent level and yields n^(2/3+o(1)).
- **Authors:** Omer Friedland
- **arXiv:** `2610.36917v1` (published 2026-10-27 09:44:35 UTC; categories: `math.FA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract gives a matching asymptotic exponent for a central finite-dimensional normed-space problem and describes a probabilistic construction with exponentially high probability. Placement reflects that specific resolution, not citation momentum.
- **Claimed result:** The author establishes R∞(n) ≥ c n^(2/3)(log(2n))^(-10); together with the cited prior upper bound this yields R∞(n)=n^(2/3+o(1)).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Maximal Banach–Mazur distance from n-dimensional real normed spaces to ℓ∞^n; lower-bound examples are polars of symmetric Gaussian polytopes with 2n generators.
- **Caveat:** The matching exponent uses an upper bound from earlier work, and the new lower bound retains a logarithmic loss and unspecified constant.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 5. [Escape of mass for finite-area hyperbolic surfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37573)
- **Abstract:** For a noncompact finite-area hyperbolic surface, the authors give a lower bound for the L² mass of eigenfunctions and Eisenstein series on a compact part, extending a result of Soundararajan to the nonarithmetic setting.
- **Authors:** Alex Cohen, Zhenhao Li, Zhongkai Tao
- **arXiv:** `2610.37573v1` (published 2026-10-27 17:18:11 UTC; categories: `math.SP`, `math.AP`, `math.DS`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The stated extension reaches nonarithmetic surfaces and spans spectral theory, PDE, and dynamics. The abstract also identifies a long-time Egorov theorem, uncertainty input, and exponential-sum estimates, giving a checkable account of the mathematical bridge.
- **Claimed result:** The authors establish the stated lower bound on compact-part L² mass for eigenfunctions and Eisenstein series; the abstract does not specify its numerical form.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Noncompact hyperbolic surface of finite area; the quantities are eigenfunctions and Eisenstein series restricted to a compact part.
- **Caveat:** The abstract does not state the bound’s explicit constants or parameter range; its proof depends on long-time Egorov estimates and other technical inputs.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology; Analysis & PDE

### 6. [Sharp Stability of Brenier Potentials and its Link to Poincaré Inequalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37659)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove a functional inequality stronger than Poincaré for uniformly log-concave measures, bounding variances of differences of convex functions and yielding sharp stability of quadratic-optimal-transport Brenier potentials under target-measure changes.
- **Authors:** Cyril Letrouit, Tudor Manole
- **arXiv:** `2610.37659v1` (published 2026-10-27 17:59:00 UTC; categories: `math.FA`, `math.AP`, `math.MG`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The result connects optimal-transport stability with functional inequalities and includes a Gaussian-matrix consequence; four mathematical categories provide a concrete cross-field signal.
- **Claimed result:** The authors establish the inequality and, for Gaussian measures, recover equivalence between Bures–Wasserstein distance of positive semidefinite matrices and Frobenius distance between their square roots.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Uniformly log-concave source measure and quadratic optimal transport; the stability statement concerns varying the target measure.
- **Caveat:** The stated framework is uniformly log-concave and the quadratic-cost setting; the abstract does not claim the same stability beyond those hypotheses.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Probability & statistics; Geometry & topology

### 7. [High-dimensional critical percolation and critical Branching random walks are intersection equivalent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37133)
- **Abstract:** In dimensions d>6, under a standard comparability assumption on the two-point and Green functions, the authors show that critical percolation and critical branching random walk have comparable probabilities of hitting a distant finite set relative to its diameter.
- **Authors:** Bruno Schapira
- **arXiv:** `2610.37133v1` (published 2026-10-27 12:28:31 UTC; categories: `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract states a precise comparison between two foundational critical models and gives a named proof mechanism, a truncated equilibrium measure with an approximate last-passage formula.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves the two hitting probabilities are within fixed positive multiplicative constants of one another under the stated distance condition.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Critical models on Z^d with d>6; the two-point function and Green function must be of the same order, and the finite target set must be sufficiently far away compared with its diameter.
- **Caveat:** The theorem is restricted to high dimensions and the stated comparability hypothesis; it does not cover low dimensions or arbitrary critical models.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics

The paper also discloses overlap with a recent related preprint, making the boundary of the result checkable.
- **Claimed result:** The authors establish their uncertainty principle for subsets of R satisfying weak branching and a nonlinearity condition on the phase.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Subsets of the real line with weak branching; the phase must meet the paper’s nonlinear condition.
- **Caveat:** The nonlinear condition is essential in the stated result; the authors say this is part of work in progress, released early, and overlaps arXiv:2609.30261.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-28
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

- **Assumptions and setting:** Smooth nondegenerate curve γ in R^n and the associated maximal averaging operator; p must be strictly greater than n.

## Trending Research Themes

- Quantitative thresholds and sharp bounds are prominent in several selected papers, but the techniques are distinct: random-lift local laws reach logarithmic-over-N scales (Huang–Xu), a normed-space distance exponent is matched up to n^o(1) (Friedland), and curve maximal estimates cover p>n (Gan–Oh–Shin). This is a pattern among selected papers, not evidence of a field-wide shift.
- Spectral analysis connects discrete and continuous settings in two different ways: Huang–Xu relate random finite graph lifts to their universal-cover spectrum, while Cohen–Li–Tao study eigenfunction and Eisenstein-series mass on finite-area hyperbolic surfaces. These are adjacent spectral questions, not one shared technique.
- An analysis/geometry/probability bridge appears in Letrouit–Manole’s transport stability inequality, while Schapira compares critical percolation with branching random walk. These are isolated cross-area signals rather than a common new method.
- A nonlinear-uncertainty interface is visible in Cohen–Dyatlov’s new principle; the hyperbolic-surface paper explicitly uses a related earlier result, arXiv:2609.30261. The connection is to that earlier paper, not a direct dependency on the selected 2610.37549 preprint.
- Algebraic geometry contributes a distinct lifting/deformation result: Ward’s height-one theorem and counterexample. It is a notable isolated signal in this batch, not a recurring cluster.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- Author-stated: Ward asks whether every strict Calabi–Yau threefold of height two lifts to characteristic zero, and whether height-one Calabi–Yau varieties in dimension at least four lift. He also asks whether finite-height examples can have nilpotent Frobenius on the relevant torsion. These questions follow from the scope and obstruction analysis in [Ward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37075).
- Author-stated limitation and follow-up synthesis: Choi notes that the construction does not settle sharpness of the quantitative Yudovich uniqueness condition. A follow-up would test which stronger growth controls on the initial vorticity restore uniqueness or still permit branching; this is a proposed direction, not a result of the preprint. [Choi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37653)
- Boundary-case direction: Huang–Xu exclude singular energies of the universal-cover spectrum. Extending local-law or delocalization control to those energies, or characterizing the obstruction there, would test the boundary of the theorem. [Huang–Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37463)
- Boundary-case direction: Gan–Oh–Shin establish the maximal estimate for p>n. Investigating whether an endpoint estimate at p=n holds under additional curvature or geometric assumptions is a concrete follow-up; the abstract does not state that this endpoint is open. [Gan–Oh–Shin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37250)
