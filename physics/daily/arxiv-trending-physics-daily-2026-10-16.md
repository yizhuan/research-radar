## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [First Observation of the Radiative Decay $D^0 \to \gamma\omega$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21982)
- **Abstract:** BESIII reports the first observation of this radiative charm decay, with a measured branching fraction of $(2.12\pm0.40_{\rm stat}\pm0.25_{\rm syst})\times10^{-5}$.[1]
- **Authors:** M. Ablikim, M. N. Achasov, P. Adlarson, X. C. Ai, C. S. Akondi, et al. (BESIII Collaboration)
- **arXiv:** `2610.21982v1` (published 2026-10-15 14:34:05 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete first-observation claim at 6.0σ, with an explicit 20.3 fb$^{-1}$ dataset and branching-fraction measurement; stronger evidence of scientific substance than early attention metrics can provide.[1]
- **Claimed result:** Using $e^+e^-$ data at 3.773 GeV, the authors measure the branching fraction above and argue it supports long-distance hadronic dynamics in radiative charm decays; the interpretation is not itself a direct measurement of the underlying mechanism.[1]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; BESIII $e^+e^-$ collision data, analyzed with a Particle Transformer classifier.[1]
- **Caveat:** The quoted rate has statistical and systematic uncertainties; the long-distance-dynamics conclusion is an interpretation of the measurement, not a separately isolated mechanism.[1]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 16 October 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 2. [The hadronic-vacuum-polarization contribution to the muon anomalous magnetic moment in isospin-symmetric lattice QCD with twisted-mass fermions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22224)
- **Abstract:** The Extended Twisted Mass Collaboration computes the leading-order hadronic-vacuum-polarization contribution to the muon $g-2$, obtaining a full-HVP value of $(714.9\pm8.1)\times10^{-10}$ after applying the WP25 leading isospin-breaking correction.[2]
- **Authors:** C. Alexandrou, S. Bacchio, A. De Santis, A. Evangelista, J. Finkenrath, et al. (Extended Twisted Mass Collaboration)
- **arXiv:** `2610.22224v1` (published 2026-10-15 17:03:50 UTC; categories: `hep-lat`, `hep-ph`, `hep-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The result is cross-listed across three high-energy archives and reports roughly 1% relative uncertainty, with a four-lattice-spacing continuum extrapolation and a direct comparison to the 2025 Muon $g-2$ Theory Initiative baseline.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The authors find their Standard Model prediction consistent with the latest Fermilab measurement and strengthen the tension with pre-CMD3 data-driven dispersive determinations; this is a lattice calculation, not a new experimental measurement.[2]
- **Evidence type:** Computational; $N_f=2+1+1$ Wilson-clover twisted-mass lattice QCD, four lattice spacings, and spatial sizes up to about 7.7 fm.[2]
- **Caveat:** The headline full-HVP number incorporates an external WP25 isospin-breaking correction; continuum and finite-volume extrapolations and the isospin-symmetric setup remain important to the uncertainty and comparison.[2]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 16 October 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 3. [Band-Resolved Superconducting Gaps via the Thermal Hall Effect in CeRu$_2$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22361)
- **Abstract:** Thermal Hall transport, constrained by specific heat and normal-state magnetotransport, is used to assign CeRu$_2$’s larger superconducting gap to heavy electron sheets and its smaller gap to hole sheets.[3]
- **Authors:** Darian Hall, Luke Pritchard Cairns, Nico Rowland, Yuanqi Lyu, Kohtaro Yamakawa, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.22361v1` (published 2026-10-15 17:59:02 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.supr-con`, `cond-mat.str-el`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper combines a low-field thermal-Hall sign reversal with independent thermodynamic and normal-state transport constraints, offering a directly testable band assignment rather than only a bulk gap estimate.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The authors infer gap scales near 13 K and 8 K and assign the larger gap to the heavy Ce $4f$ electron sheets; the assignment comes from the measured transport response interpreted in a two-band framework.[3]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; thermal-Hall and longitudinal heat transport, specific heat, and normal-state magnetotransport in CeRu$_2$ single crystals.[3]
- **Caveat:** The band assignment relies on a compensated two-band transport picture and a two-gap Bardeen–Rickayzen–Tewordt analysis; it is a result for one material, not yet a general demonstration across multiband superconductors.[3]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 16 October 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

### 4. [Discovering state-of-the-art quantum circuits with large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22367)
- **Abstract:** An evolutionary search in which LLMs propose circuits and an independent evaluator scores them reduces Toffoli and qubit counts against reported baselines in seven of eight tested tasks.[4]
- **Authors:** Andi Gu, Alek H. Kemeny, Susanne F. Yelin, Mikhail D. Lukin
- **arXiv:** `2610.22367v1` (published 2026-10-15 17:59:20 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`, `cs.AI`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper reports benchmarked resource reductions across chemistry, optimization, simulation, numerical computation, and number-theory tasks, and is cross-listed between quantum physics and AI.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The authors report a new reversible architecture for prime-field modular inversion and improved resource trade-offs; these are algorithmic benchmark results, not evidence of quantum advantage on hardware.[4]
- **Evidence type:** Computational; evolutionary LLM circuit proposals evaluated by an independent scoring procedure on eight tasks.[4]
- **Caveat:** The evidence is limited to the chosen tasks and cost metrics; the abstract reports no experimental quantum-hardware validation or general practical speedup.[4]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 16 October 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Computational & data methods

### 5. [Gallium Anomaly and Solar Neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22219)
- **Abstract:** Comparing gallium capture results with solar-neutrino source strengths calibrated through neutrino–electron scattering, the authors find agreement rather than an anomaly.[5]
- **Authors:** Steven R. Elliott, R. G. Hamish Robertson
- **arXiv:** `2610.22219v1` (published 2026-10-15 17:02:46 UTC; categories: `nucl-ex`, `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It addresses a live neutrino-rate discrepancy with an independent solar-neutrino comparison and reports quantitative agreement across two experimental archives.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The authors compare $66.1\pm3.1$ SNU from SAGE and Gallex/GNO with solar-source strengths of $67.5^{+1.4}_{-1.1}$ SNU, concluding that the gallium anomaly is not present in this comparison.[5]
- **Evidence type:** Observational; analysis of published gallium capture rates and solar-neutrino source strengths calibrated using standard-model neutrino–electron scattering.[5]
- **Caveat:** The conclusion depends on the solar-neutrino source-strength calibration and is an analysis of existing rates, not a new direct measurement of the gallium cross section or counting efficiency.[5]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 16 October 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 6. [Projected sensitivity of a spinor Bose-Einstein condensate comagnetometer to proton monopole-dipole interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22346)
- **Abstract:** The authors project that an upgraded $^{87}$Rb spinor-BEC comagnetometer could improve direct laboratory sensitivity to proton monopole–dipole interactions by several orders of magnitude.[6]
- **Authors:** Wenjing Zhou, Diego Blas, Morgan W. Mitchell
- **arXiv:** `2610.22346v1` (published 2026-10-15 17:56:58 UTC; categories: `physics.atom-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The proposal connects demonstrated comagnetometry performance to a specific source-mass geometry and a quantified one-week sensitivity forecast, while addressing a parameter range where direct proton-spin tests are scarce.[6]
- **Claimed result:** For a proposed bismuth-germanate source mass, the authors project $|g_s^N g_p^p|\approx2\times10^{-22}$ after planned upgrades and $3\times10^{-24}$ at the projection-noise limit for $\lambda=100\,\mu$m; these are forecasts, not new force limits.[6]
- **Evidence type:** Forecast/proposal; sensitivity calculations based on demonstrated $^{87}$Rb spinor-BEC magnetometry/comagnetometry performance and proposed apparatus upgrades.[6]
- **Caveat:** The reach depends on planned improvements, source-mass positioning, magnetic environment, and assumed noise and integration time; the projection-noise-limit value is not demonstrated.[6]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 16 October 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Atomic, molecular & optical physics; Instrumentation & detectors

## Trending Research Themes

- **Precision results with different evidence chains:** BESIII reports a new decay observation, while the lattice-QCD paper updates a key Standard Model input for muon $g-2$; the gallium paper reinterprets existing rates using solar-neutrino calibration. These are complementary but independent analyses, not confirmations of one another.[1][2][5]
- **AI enters distinct physics workflows:** A Particle Transformer supports event analysis in the BESIII study, while an evolutionary LLM framework searches for lower-cost quantum circuits. This is a cross-field connection in the selected set, not evidence of a single unified AI trend.[1][4]
- **New observables and proposed reach:** CeRu$_2$’s thermal-Hall sign reversal is an isolated experimental example of assigning multiband gaps; the BEC comagnetometer paper is an isolated forecast for improved short-range force searches.[3][6]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Lattice versus dispersive $g-2$ determinations — author-stated open issue:** The lattice result is consistent with the latest Fermilab measurement but deepens tension with pre-CMD3 dispersive determinations. Independent lattice calculations and tighter control of isospin-breaking and finite-volume effects would help test the comparison.[2]
- **Band-specific superconductivity — follow-up synthesis:** The CeRu$_2$ assignment depends on a two-band interpretation. Applying thermal-Hall constraints alongside band-resolved spectroscopy in other multiband materials could test whether the approach generalizes.[3]
- **Projected exotic-force reach — author-stated experimental gap:** The BEC sensitivity is forecast, not a detection. Implementing the stated upgrades and measuring technical noise and source-mass systematics would establish whether the projected range is attainable.[6]
- **Radiative charm dynamics — follow-up synthesis:** The BESIII branching fraction is proposed as a benchmark for non-perturbative $D\to\gamma V$ calculations; improved theoretical predictions and additional independent decay measurements can test that interpretation.[1]
- **Gallium-rate interpretation — follow-up synthesis:** The solar-neutrino comparison does not itself repeat the artificial-source measurements. Further independent checks of source strengths, neutrino interaction inputs, and detector response would clarify which elements of the original discrepancy remain.[5]
- **Quantum-circuit search — follow-up synthesis:** The reported gains are benchmark-level. Extending the evaluator to broader workloads and testing the resulting circuits under realistic hardware noise would determine how robust the resource savings are.[4]

## Takeaway

The batch’s clearest result is experimental: BESIII reports a 6σ first observation in radiative charm decay. The other standout papers either sharpen precision theory and condensed-matter interpretation or propose computational and experimental routes that still need broader validation; none should be read as a confirmed new-physics discovery.
