## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [Experimental evidence of forward energy transfer induced by core-edge coupling in multiscale tokamak turbulence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30638)
- **Abstract:** The authors combine collective Thomson scattering and Doppler backscattering measurements to report forward transfer of turbulent energy from ion to electron scales associated with an edge-originating quasi-coherent mode in a tokamak core.
- **Authors:** F. Chen, Z. M. Shen, N. Sun, P. Li, Z. Y. Qiu et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.30638v1` (published 2026-10-22T02:58:21Z; categories: `physics.plasm-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A concrete experimental result pairs two diagnostics that resolve different aspects of turbulence; the full paper reports recurrence of the signatures in more than 1,000 EAST discharges across 2021–2026. This makes it a strong, directly testable plasma-physics result rather than a theory-only proposal.
- **Claimed result:** The authors interpret the measured cross-scale correlations as forward energy transfer driven by coupling between an edge quasi-coherent mode and broadband core turbulence; they report signatures across EAST campaigns.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; multichannel collective Thomson scattering and radially localized Doppler backscattering on EAST, with nonlinear simulations for interpretation.
- **Caveat:** The authors say simulations do not reproduce all observed spectral detail; missing impurity-density and temperature profiles limit quantitative comparison. They also report that the improved-confinement regime requires careful density and impurity control.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Plasma & fluid physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [Measurements of the branching fractions of Λc⁺→ΛK⁺π⁰ and Λc⁺→ΛK⁺η decays at Belle and Belle II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31377)
- **Abstract:** Using 1.55 ab⁻¹ of Belle and Belle II data, the collaborations improve the relative branching-fraction measurement for Λc⁺→ΛK⁺π⁰ and report the first observation of Λc⁺→ΛK⁺η.
- **Authors:** Belle and Belle II Collaborations; M. Abumusabh, I. Adachi, A. Aggarwal, Y. Ahn, H. Aihara et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.31377v1` (published 2026-10-22T14:48:37Z; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The combined collider dataset yields a first observation above 10σ and a more precise measurement of a related decay; both are specific, checkable experimental milestones.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report a Λc⁺→ΛK⁺η branching fraction of (6.20 ± 0.25 ± 0.31 ± 0.30)×10⁻⁴ and find it larger than current SU(3)-flavor-symmetry predictions; the third uncertainty comes from the reference branching fraction.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; Belle and Belle II e⁺e⁻ collision samples at KEKB and SuperKEKB, totaling 1.55 ab⁻¹.
- **Caveat:** Absolute branching fractions inherit uncertainty from the world-average reference mode; the authors also note sub-combination mass-spectrum differences from phase-space simulation that may reflect unresolved intermediate resonances.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 3. [Detection of Solar-like Oscillations and Granulation at Infrared Wavelengths using JWST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30594)
- **Abstract:** JWST/NIRISS time-series observations of the exoplanet host WASP-121 reveal solar-like oscillations and granulation in infrared light, with amplitudes that decrease with wavelength but depart from simple blackbody scaling.
- **Authors:** Angharad Weeks, Daniel Huber, Timothy R. Bedding, Jared Splinter, Hans Kjeldsen et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.30594v1` (published 2026-10-22T01:54:33Z; categories: `astro-ph.SR`, `astro-ph.EP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is a first infrared detection of two stellar-variability signals, bridging stellar convection and exoplanet time-series analysis; the arXiv abstract page notes resubmission to ApJ Letters following a favourable referee report.
- **Claimed result:** The authors use JWST observations of the F6V star WASP-121 to detect infrared oscillations and granulation and report wavelength-dependent amplitudes inconsistent with a simple blackbody scaling.
- **Evidence type:** Observational; JWST/NIRISS SOSS time-series photometry of WASP-121, analysed with a Gaussian-process framework.
- **Caveat:** The result is based on one star; the paper says the time series is too short to robustly measure the lower-frequency supergranulation component.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 4. [A cosmic filament catalog for COSMOS-Web Data Release 1 across 0.5 < z < 3.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31168)
- **Abstract:** The authors combine COSMOS-Web Data Release 1 photometric-redshift probability distributions with a DisPerSE-based workflow to release a catalog of 12,523 cosmic filaments and 136,210 segments over 0.5 < z < 3.5.
- **Authors:** Qiaoyang Hao, Tao Wang, Weishan Zhu, Hong Guo, Peng Wang et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.31168v1` (published 2026-10-22T12:24:46Z; categories: `astro-ph.CO`, `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.IM`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A substantial catalog, segment catalog, and probability maps are released, and the work is cross-listed across cosmology, galaxy astrophysics, and instrumentation/methods; the arXiv record also provides a journal DOI.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report extracting 12,523 filaments from COSMOS-Web DR1, with parameters tuned against TNG-based mock catalogs to reduce discrepancies between projected and 3D structures.
- **Evidence type:** Observational and computational; COSMOS-Web DR1 photometric-redshift PDFs, DisPerSE extraction, and TNG simulation mock catalogs.
- **Caveat:** Filament identification relies on photometric-redshift probability modeling and calibrated projection from 3D mock structures; the extracted catalog is not a direct spectroscopic 3D map.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 5. [Odd radio circle J1555+2726: Observational evidence for shock re-acceleration of fossil plasma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31664)
- **Abstract:** Multi-frequency LOFAR, uGMRT, and MeerKAT maps of the odd radio circle J1555+2726 show spectral curvature and radial flattening that the authors interpret as likely shock re-acceleration of pre-existing relativistic plasma.
- **Authors:** L. Bruno, B. Koribalski, R. P. Norris, A. Ivleva, T. Venturi et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.31664v1` (published 2026-10-22T17:59:32Z; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The analysis combines three radio facilities over 50–1284 MHz and reports resolved spectral properties, making it a multi-instrument observational test of competing odd-radio-circle formation pictures; it also spans galaxy and cosmology categories.
- **Claimed result:** The authors find a convex spectral curvature and flattening toward the ring, and argue that a mixture of electron populations with different spectra is consistent with re-acceleration of fossil plasma.
- **Evidence type:** Observational; LOFAR, upgraded Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope, and MeerKAT radio imaging and spectral-index maps.
- **Caveat:** The re-acceleration scenario is an interpretation of the spectral morphology; the authors state that standard shock acceleration of thermal particles plus ordinary spectral ageing does not explain the observed shape.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Astrophysics

### 6. [From phase analysis to phase tracking: a robust approach to atomic-scale strain mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30665)
- **Abstract:** Geometric phase tracking analysis follows displaced Bragg reflections pixel by pixel and, on 13,795 simulated and experimental atomic-resolution images, lowers the reported failure rate relative to conventional geometric phase analysis.
- **Authors:** Ying Han, Yuan Tian
- **arXiv:** `2610.30665v1` (published 2026-10-22T03:29:10Z; categories: `cond-mat.mtrl-sci`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper gives a large, quantified comparison set and reports lower failure rates (27% to 11.5%) and up to 335-fold better accuracy in tested cases; it also adds confidence maps and grain-boundary strain mapping.
- **Claimed result:** The authors propose GPTA, which tracks local reciprocal-space peaks and report improved strain/dislocation maps under large strain, rotation, and dynamical-scattering extinction.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; algorithm benchmarked on simulated and experimental atomic-resolution microscopy images.
- **Caveat:** The numerical advantage is reported for the tested image set and imaging conditions; broader performance across microscopy instruments and acquisition regimes remains to be established.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials; Computational & data methods

### 7. [Photometric classification and out-of-taxonomy detection of ZTF transients with FLARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30656)
- **Abstract:** FLARE classifies ZTF transients and flags events outside known classes; a simulated review loop recovers 83% of held-out out-of-taxonomy examples after inspecting 15% of the alert stream.
- **Authors:** Argyro Sasli, Felipe Fontinele Nunes, Xuanrui Zhang, Dimitrios Kourtesis, Saina Nikmehr et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.30656v1` (published 2026-10-22T03:19:15Z; categories: `astro-ph.IM`, `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It is evaluated on 10,497 spectroscopically confirmed ZTF transients plus 53 objects outside the training taxonomy, reports explicit recovery/false-alarm behavior, and the abstract says the module runs in production on the ZTF Fritz/SkyPortal system.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report that blind anomaly detection recovers few out-of-taxonomy events at a 1% false-alarm rate, while a simulated iterative review process improves recovery and benefits from confirmed examples being added as a new class.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; machine-learning classification and anomaly detection evaluated on ZTF light curves and a simulated review loop.
- **Caveat:** The out-of-taxonomy evaluation set contains 53 objects, and the 83% recovery figure comes from a simulated review process rather than a prospective controlled deployment trial.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 8. [A 13 Year Black Hole Microlensing Survey in Omega Centauri](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30673)
- **Abstract:** A search of nearly 200 Hubble single-band images spanning 13 years finds no convincing stellar-mass black-hole microlensing candidates in Omega Centauri, and releases the photometric dataset.
- **Authors:** Zack Freeman, Maximilian Häberle, Anil Seth, John Zaris, Matthew Whitaker et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.30673v1` (published 2026-10-22T03:38:03Z; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.SR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The long HST baseline, explicit synthetic-event completeness analysis, and released photometric dataset make this a useful, testable null search rather than an unqualified absence claim.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report no convincing candidates and, using their simulated-event completeness, find that zero events were expected in this dataset, contrary to earlier expectations.
- **Evidence type:** Observational and computational; Hubble Space Telescope images, synthetic microlensing events based on Omega Centauri Monte Carlo simulations, and a neural-network selection stage.
- **Caveat:** The null result depends on the assumed simulation-based event population and the survey's single-band sensitivity/completeness; it does not establish that the cluster contains no stellar-mass black holes.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement batch 2026-10-23
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

## Trending Research Themes

These are distinct results, not independent confirmations of one phenomenon.
- **Astronomy is increasingly multi-instrument and catalog-driven:** JWST/NIRISS time series (#3), the COSMOS-Web filament products (#4), three-facility radio mapping of an odd radio circle (#5), and the HST/Chandra-era Omega Centauri work (#8) show how multiwavelength coverage and reusable catalogs support questions from stellar convection to galaxy environments and compact remnants. These datasets do not constitute independent confirmations of a shared result.
- **Analysis tools are becoming part of the scientific result:** COSMOS-Web's redshift-PDF filament maps (#4), GPTA's pixel-wise microscopy analysis (#6), FLARE's novelty-aware ZTF triage (#7), and synthetic-event completeness testing in Omega Centauri (#8) all make inference pipelines or reusable data products central, while their conclusions remain tied to calibration and validation choices.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated open problem — plasma modeling:** Simulations of the EAST turbulence regime do not reproduce all measured spectral structure, and missing impurity-density/temperature profiles constrain quantitative comparison (#1). **Research direction (synthesis):** repeat nonlinear modeling with measured impurity profiles and compare long- and short-pulse discharges while tracking density-control feedback.
- **Author-stated open problem — stellar variability:** The JWST light curve is too short to robustly measure lower-frequency supergranulation (#3). **Research direction (synthesis):** obtain longer infrared time series and simultaneous multi-band observations across additional host stars to test whether the wavelength scaling generalizes.
- **Author-stated open problem — charmed-baryon dynamics:** The Belle/Belle II analysis notes sub-combination mass-spectrum deviations from phase-space simulation and possible intermediate resonances (#2). **Research direction (synthesis):** perform higher-statistics amplitude analyses and compare the observed structures with updated three-body decay calculations.
- **Methodological open problem — cosmic-web projection:** The COSMOS-Web filament catalog is inferred from photometric-redshift PDFs and parameters tuned on projected-versus-3D TNG mocks (#4). **Research direction (synthesis):** test completeness and purity against independent mock realizations and spectroscopic subsets, then quantify how structure choices affect galaxy-environment trends.
- **Interpretive open problem — odd radio circles:** The spectral curvature in J1555+2726 is inconsistent with standard thermal-shock acceleration plus ordinary ageing, so the fossil-plasma re-acceleration explanation remains an interpretation (#5). **Research direction (synthesis):** add deeper broadband, polarization, and spatially resolved follow-up to constrain electron populations and shock geometry.
- **Evaluation open problem — rare transient classes:** FLARE's out-of-taxonomy benchmark uses 53 examples and its strongest review-loop recovery is simulated (#7). **Research direction (synthesis):** measure prospective performance on a time-separated alert stream and broaden rare-class validation without conflating simulated recovery with field detections.

## Takeaway

The strongest signals in this batch are concrete new measurements and analysis products: cross-scale tokamak turbulence, a first Λc decay observation, infrared asteroseismology, and several reusable astronomy/microscopy tools. Their scientific reach is promising but unevenly established: some results rest on one object or a narrow benchmark, while the plasma and particle findings still need deeper quantitative modeling or follow-up.
