## Top papers (ranked)

### 1. [A Solution to the Kohayakawa-Prömel-Rödl Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27478)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove that for all finite graphs G and H with |V(G)|=k≥2 and |V(H)|=t≥2, their induced Ramsey number satisfies r_ind(G,H)≤t^(60k²+3), resolving the 1998 Kohayakawa–Prömel–Rödl conjecture.
- **Authors:** Chuang Zhong, Masaki Kashima, Yaping Mao
- **arXiv:** `2610.27478v1` (published 2026-10-20 01:44:36 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract states a resolution of a named 1998 conjecture with a uniform explicit polynomial bound for every eligible graph pair; this is a concrete, broad-scope result rather than a recency-only pick.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove r_ind(G,H)≤t^(60k²+3) for all k,t≥2. The paper's introduction states the sharper intermediate bound 7t^(60k²)≤t^(60k²+3).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite undirected simple graphs; k=|V(G)|≥2 and t=|V(H)|≥2. The bound is an upper bound on the minimum order of a two-edge-coloured host forcing a red induced G or blue induced H.
- **Caveat:** The stated exponent is large; the abstract gives no matching lower bound or claim of optimality.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-21 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 2. [One-Ring Bourgain Alternative And A 5-Decimal Bound On The Dimension Of Harmonic Measure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28037)
- **Abstract:** The authors improve upper bounds for the Hausdorff dimension of harmonic measure—to at most 3−10⁻⁵ in R³ and 4−10⁻⁷ in R⁴—and for every n≥3 give the additional bound n−(n−1)^−(2n+6.12√n), using a new thin-ring estimate and cubes in general position.
- **Authors:** Michael Albert, Matthew Badger, Alyssa Genschaw
- **arXiv:** `2610.28037v1` (published 2026-10-20 11:24:15 UTC; categories: `math.CA`, `math.AP`, `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract quantifies improvements over prior bounds in dimensions three and four, cross-lists analysis, PDE, and probability, and identifies a new geometric estimate; these are checkable mathematical-significance signals.
- **Claimed result:** The authors establish the stated 3D and 4D bounds and, for n≥3, the additional estimate n−(n−1)^−(2n+6.12√n); their one-ring alternative shows that a sufficiently thin surrounding ring with no large holes makes Brownian entry into the enclosed cube unlikely.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Harmonic measure on domains in Euclidean Rⁿ, for n≥3. In R³ and R⁴ the claimed bounds are respectively 3−10⁻⁵ and 4−10⁻⁷.
- **Caveat:** The bounds remain very close to the ambient dimension and do not reach Bishop's conjectured sharp bound; the authors list ten challenges toward it.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-21 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Probability & statistics

### 3. [Balanced subgraph counts and quasirandomness: a proof of the Janson–Sós conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28124)
- **Abstract:** For every fixed finite simple graph F with at least two edges, the authors prove that the random-graph asymptotics of symmetrized F-copy counts across all balanced vertex partitions characterize p-quasirandom graph sequences, including when F has isolated vertices.
- **Authors:** Guoqing Wang, Linlin Wang, Jun Yue
- **arXiv:** `2610.28124v1` (published 2026-10-20 12:33:03 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is an explicit proof of the Janson–Sós conjecture, extending the result to every eligible F, including graphs with isolated vertices; the arXiv introduction identifies the conjecture and its prior scope.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove the conjectured quasirandomness characterization for all fixed finite simple F with at least two edges. The introduction states that the equivalent unsymmetrized formulation also follows.
- **Assumptions and setting:** A graph sequence with vertex count tending to infinity, fixed F, and p∈(0,1]; the asymptotic symmetrized subgraph-count condition is uniform over balanced partitions into |V(F)| classes.
- **Caveat:** The characterization is conditional on the specified balanced-partition count data; the result does not say that one observed count or an unbalanced partition suffices.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-21 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 4. [Counting Bessel zeros and applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28477)
- **Abstract:** The authors combine o-minimal point-counting with an Ax–Schanuel theorem for Bessel functions and exponentials to prove sparsity for algebraic varieties containing tuples of Bessel zeros, with an application to spectral sets of the unit ball and Fuglede's conjecture.
- **Authors:** Gal Binyamini, Avner Kiro, Gady Kozma, Jonathan Pila
- **arXiv:** `2610.28477v1` (published 2026-10-20 16:27:59 UTC; categories: `math.CA`, `math.LO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper connects classical analysis and mathematical logic, supplies a functional-transcendence result and a point-counting application, and the introduction states a quantitative sparsity theorem rather than only a proposed framework.
- **Claimed result:** For fixed κ≥0 other than 1/2, the authors bound the number of maximal Bessel-special subvarieties of height at most H in a given algebraic variety by C(V,κ,ε)H^ε; for positive half-integer κ other than 1/2 they give a polylogarithmic bound. They also state an Ax–Schanuel theorem for Bessel functions and exponentials.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Bessel functions of fixed parameter; the sparsity theorem concerns algebraic V⊂(C×)ⁿ and maximal special subvarieties up to height H≥1. The improved polylogarithmic estimate has the stated positive half-integer restriction.
- **Caveat:** The main sparsity statement excludes κ=1/2, and the authors say their constants are not effective; they explain that the excluded parameter has zeros πZ and their approach breaks down there.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-21 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Logic & foundations; Algebra & number theory

### 5. [Space-time localized nonuniqueness for the inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28130)
- **Abstract:** For the three-dimensional torus, the author constructs weak solutions of the inhomogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes equations that match a smooth solution outside a prescribed space-time cylinder yet differ inside it, under specified density and velocity regularity conditions.
- **Authors:** Xingyu Li
- **arXiv:** `2610.28130v1` (published 2026-10-20 12:36:58 UTC; categories: `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract claims space-time localized nonuniqueness, including infinitely many solutions equal to rest outside a cylinder, and gives a concrete convex-integration construction; this is a distinct, sharply specified result in a central PDE.
- **Claimed result:** The author constructs such weak solutions for every 0<s<3/2 and p>1 sufficiently close to 1, with strictly positive density in C_t L^p and velocity in C_t H^s, using concentrated transported density profiles and localized inverse-divergence operators.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Inhomogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes on the 3D torus; prescribed smooth fields must satisfy necessary mass and spatial first-moment conditions. The solutions agree with a given smooth solution outside a compact subset of a chosen cylinder.
- **Caveat:** The theorem is limited to the stated low-density-integrability and velocity-regularity regime, and the construction includes non-renormalized density; it is not a claim of nonuniqueness for every conventional solution class.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-21 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 6. [Local well-posedness of wave kinetic equations associated with water waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28549)
- **Abstract:** The authors establish local well-posedness for pure-gravity waves with two-dimensional interfaces in weighted Sobolev spaces and for one-dimensional gravity-capillary interfaces in weighted L∞ spaces, while identifying immediate regularity loss and failure of continuity in the latter setting.
- **Authors:** Yu Deng, Alexandru D. Ionescu, Fabio Pusateri
- **arXiv:** `2610.28549v1` (published 2026-10-20 17:15:50 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper gives two contrasting well-posedness theories and identifies degenerate three-wave resonances as the mechanism behind the capillary instability; its math.AP/math-ph cross-list reflects the analysis–physics interface.
- **Claimed result:** For pure-gravity waves with 2D interfaces, the authors prove propagation of weighted-Sobolev regularity and continuous dependence in the same topology. For gravity-capillary waves with 1D interfaces, they prove existence, uniqueness, and continuity in a weaker topology in weighted L∞, and show that added regularity is generally lost instantly.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Wave kinetic equations associated with the two specified water-wave regimes: pure gravity/2D interfaces and gravity-capillary/1D interfaces.
- **Caveat:** In the gravity-capillary case, continuity in time and continuous dependence in the weighted L∞ norm fail even for smooth compactly supported data; this is part of the authors' instability result, not ordinary well-posedness in that norm.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-21 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Mathematical physics

## Trending Research Themes

- **Two distinct graph-theoretic conjecture resolutions.** Zhong, Kashima, and Mao settle a polynomial induced-Ramsey bound, while Wang, Wang, and Yue settle a balanced-count criterion for quasirandomness. Both are concrete advances in extremal/discrete mathematics, but the problems and proof mechanisms are different; they do not establish a single new graph-theory method.
- **Quantitative geometry of harmonic measure.** Albert, Badger, and Genschaw combine probability (Brownian motion), geometric measure ideas, and harmonic analysis to improve dimension bounds; the paper's new one-ring estimate and its ten listed challenges make the geometry-to-probability link explicit.
- **Analysis linked through resonance, regularity, and nonuniqueness.** Li's localized convex-integration construction and Deng, Ionescu, and Pusateri's resonance-driven wave-kinetic results both show how solution behavior depends strongly on function space and structure. This is a connection across two different PDE models, not evidence of a field-wide shift.
- **Functional transcendence meets spectral analysis.** Binyamini, Kiro, Kozma, and Pila bring o-minimality and an Ax–Schanuel theorem for Bessel functions to a sparsity question with an application to unit-ball spectral sets, bridging logic and classical analysis.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated:** Albert, Badger, and Genschaw list ten challenges toward Bishop's conjectured sharp harmonic-measure dimension bound. A follow-up direction, as synthesis, is to test which of their proposed challenges can be advanced by the paper's thin-ring estimate in dimensions beyond three and four. ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28037))
- **Author-stated limitation; proposed extension:** The Bessel sparsity theorem excludes κ=1/2 and its constants are ineffective. One can investigate whether the πZ structure at κ=1/2 admits a separate point-counting argument, and whether the existing bounds can be made effective for the covered parameters. ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28477))
- **Synthesis from the stated bound:** The induced-Ramsey theorem proves an explicit exponent 60k²+3 but does not claim optimality. Lower bounds for selected graph families, or improved dependence on k, would test how far the uniform estimate can be sharpened. ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27478))
- **Synthesis from the resonance limitation:** For the one-dimensional gravity-capillary wave kinetic equation, the authors identify degenerate three-wave resonances and failure of continuity in weighted L∞. Mapping which resonance geometries or alternative function spaces restore stronger continuity would probe the boundary of their theorem. ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28549))
