### 1. [Demonstration of Logical Error Suppression in a Surface Code Memory on a Cloud-Accessible IBM Superconducting Processor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23840)[1]
- **Abstract:** The authors report that optimized syndrome extraction on an IBM Nighthawk r2 reduces surface-code memory error rates, with distance-3-to-6 suppression factors of 1.68 in Z memory and 1.85 in X memory.[1]
- **Authors:** Shuwen Kan, Aaron Hoyt, Sean Garner, Ethan Vo, Adrian Harkness, et al.[1]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23840v1` (published 2026-10-16 17:56:53 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`)[1]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A hardware demonstration with quantitative logical-error suppression and a measured dependence on processor placement; selected for its direct relevance to fault-tolerant quantum computing, not on readership or citation counts.[1]
- **Claimed result:** The authors say their readout/reset/echo optimizations lower median per-round logical error rates by factors of 3.8 (X) and 1.4 (Z) versus their standard circuit, and increasing protected distance from 3 to 6 gives suppression factors of 1.68 (Z) and 1.85 (X).[1]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; rotated surface-code memory on an IBM Nighthawk r2 processor, with up to 150 syndrome rounds and maximum-likelihood decoding.[1]
- **Caveat:** The distance advantage weakens in long memory runs; the authors report long-lived correlations that may indicate a persistent error component, and performance varies with processor placement.[1]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[1]
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Instrumentation & detectors[1]

### 2. [Reactor Antineutrino Detection with JUNO-TAO: Commissioning Performance and First Results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23331)[2]
- **Abstract:** The JUNO-TAO collaboration reports commissioning performance and first reactor-on/off data from a 2.8-ton, cryogenic antineutrino detector 43.2 m from a reactor core.[2]
- **Authors:** JUNO Collaboration[2]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23331v1` (published 2026-10-16 11:32:51 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)[2]
- **Evidence for ranking:** First physics-run results from a major detector, with reported calibration, energy-resolution, vertex, veto, and reactor-on/off measurements.[2]
- **Claimed result:** The collaboration reports about 2.8% energy resolution for a central Ge-68 calibration source, about 90% of channels retained after quality selection, and a reactor-on IBD candidate excess over reactor-off data.[2]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; TAO commissioning and 31.3 days reactor-on plus 29.4 days reactor-off data.[2]
- **Caveat:** This is a detector-performance and initial-rate report, not yet the final high-precision reactor antineutrino spectrum measurement; reported resolution varies by calibration source and position.[2]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[2]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors[2]

### 3. [Observation of the Ξ_b⁻ → Ξ⁻μ⁺μ⁻ and study of the Ξ_b⁻ → Ξ⁻ψ(2S) decays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22452)[3]
- **Abstract:** LHCb reports the first observation of the rare Ξ_b⁻ → Ξ⁻μ⁺μ⁻ decay and measures its branching fraction in dimuon-mass intervals, alongside a ratio for the ψ(2S) and J/ψ channels.[3]
- **Authors:** LHCb collaboration: R. Aaij, M. Abdelfatah, A. S. W. Abdelmotteleb, C. Abellan Beteta, F. Abudinén, et al. (1092 additional authors are listed on the abstract page.)[3]
- **arXiv:** `2610.22452v1` (published 2026-10-15 16:02:05 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)[3]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A first-observation claim based on LHCb proton-proton collision data at three centre-of-mass energies and a reported integrated luminosity of 9 fb⁻¹.[3]
- **Claimed result:** The collaboration measures the integrated branching fraction as (118 ± 24 ± 7 ± 38) × 10⁻⁸ and the ψ(2S)-to-J/ψ branching-fraction ratio as 0.491 ± 0.041 ± 0.019.[3]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; LHCb data at 7, 8, and 13 TeV, corresponding to 1, 2, and 6 fb⁻¹.[3]
- **Caveat:** The largest quoted uncertainty on the integrated branching fraction comes from the normalization branching fraction, whose uncertainty is dominated by SU(3)-breaking effects.[3]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[3]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors[3]

### 4. [Complete g⁶ pressure of hot Yang-Mills theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23675)[4]
- **Abstract:** The authors calculate the full order-g⁶ pressure of hot SU(3) Yang-Mills theory, combining newly evaluated four-loop contributions with known electric- and magnetic-scale terms.[4]
- **Authors:** Keijo Kajantie, Pablo Navarrete, Risto Paatelainen, Leon Sandbote, York Schröder, et al.[4]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23675v1` (published 2026-10-16 16:06:23 UTC; categories: `hep-ph`, `hep-lat`, `hep-th`)[4]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper says it resolves a perturbative pressure term left open for more than two decades; it is cross-listed across phenomenology, lattice, and theory, and compares the result with four-dimensional lattice simulations.[4]
- **Claimed result:** The authors evaluate nine previously unknown nonfactorizing four-loop sum-integrals, complete the hard-scale contribution for general SU(Nc), and obtain the complete O(g⁶) pressure for SU(3) after combining known contributions.[4]
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical/computational; thermal loop-tree duality calculations with local UV/IR subtractions, combined with existing scale contributions and compared with lattice-simulation data.[4]
- **Caveat:** This is a weak-coupling theoretical calculation, not a direct measurement; the SU(3) result combines contributions from different momentum scales, and its usefulness depends on the temperature regime where the expansion is controlled.[4]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[4]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods[4]

### 5. [The pressure of the gluon plasma at O(g_s⁶), perturbatively](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23676)[5]
- **Abstract:** The authors compute a four-loop hard-scale matching correction and say it completes the high-temperature gluon-plasma pressure through O(g_s⁶), in agreement within uncertainties with an earlier lattice-data fit.[5]
- **Authors:** Mikael Chala, Luis Gil, Javier López Miras[5]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23676v1` (published 2026-10-16 16:06:23 UTC; categories: `hep-ph`, `hep-lat`, `hep-th`)[5]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A second new calculation addresses the same long-standing high-temperature QCD pressure problem with a distinct calculation; the abstract frames agreement with a lattice-based fit as a first direct test at this order.[5] It is complementary work, not an independent experimental confirmation.[5]
- **Claimed result:** The authors report a four-loop hard-scale matching correction whose value agrees, within uncertainties, with the value previously obtained by fitting the perturbative series to continuum-extrapolated lattice data.[5]
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical/computational; perturbative high-temperature calculation compared with a lattice-data fit.[5]
- **Caveat:** The comparison is with an earlier fit to lattice data rather than a new experiment; the result remains a perturbative, high-temperature statement.[5]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[5]
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods[5]

### 6. [Ionic switching between supermagnetic states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23829)[6]
- **Abstract:** The authors report reversible room-temperature Li-ion-driven switching between superferromagnetic and superspin-glass states in partially lithiated NiO, persisting over subsequent lithiation/delithiation cycles.[6]
- **Authors:** Sumaiyatul Ahsan, Yin-Ju Yen, Andrew H. Comstock, Tanya A. Balandin, Jason D. Azoulay, et al.[6]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23829v1` (published 2026-10-16 17:52:12 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.mtrl-sci`)[6]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A claimed first reversible switching between two collective magnetic states using room-temperature ionic transport, with repeatability across measured cycles.[6]
- **Claimed result:** The authors attribute the superferromagnetic state to a fragmented nanoscale-Ni network formed by lithiation, and the superspin-glass state to partial NiO regeneration during delithiation.[6]
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; room-temperature lithiation/delithiation of NiO with magnetic characterization.[6]
- **Caveat:** The result and proposed mechanism are specific to this NiO-based system; the abstract does not establish generality across materials.[6]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[6]
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials[6]

### 7. [Gravitational Waves as a Probe of Multi-Fluid Cosmologies: General Formalism and the Case of Cosmological Stasis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23808)[7]
- **Abstract:** The authors extend scalar-induced gravitational-wave calculations to multi-fluid early-universe cosmologies and show how fluid composition can alter the predicted spectrum and present-day abundance.[7]
- **Authors:** Keith R. Dienes, Fazlollah Hajkarim, Lucien Heurtier, Fei Huang, Kuver Sinha, et al.[7]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23808v1` (published 2026-10-16 17:31:55 UTC; categories: `astro-ph.CO`, `hep-ph`)[7]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A generalization across cosmology and particle phenomenology that identifies distinct velocity and redshifting effects; the authors give cosmological stasis as a concrete example.[7]
- **Claimed result:** The authors find that a multi-fluid composition can change scalar-induced gravitational-wave signals beyond what is captured by the overall expansion rate, and that stasis can yield spectra and present-day abundances differing from radiation domination.[7]
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical; formal analysis and model predictions, not a reported gravitational-wave detection.[7]
- **Caveat:** Predicted signals depend on the assumed early-universe history and multi-fluid model; the paper does not report observational confirmation.[7]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[7]
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics[7]

### 8. [Near-Extremal Black-Hole Formation Observed from Null Infinity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23486)[8]
- **Abstract:** Numerical evolutions of a spherically symmetric Einstein-Maxwell-charged-scalar system form near-extremal black holes, with their charge-to-mass ratios inferred from observables at future null infinity.[8]
- **Authors:** Thomas Mädler, Radouane Gannouji, Steven L. Liebling, Emanuel Gallo[8]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23486v1` (published 2026-10-16 14:02:19 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`)[8]
- **Evidence for ranking:** A computational-gravity result that cross-checks the inferred near-extremal charge-to-mass ratio using quasinormal frequencies and redshift decay, while relating critical scaling at null infinity and the apparent horizon.[8]
- **Claimed result:** The authors report formation up to charge-to-mass ratio 0.993 from regular, horizon-free, superextremal initial data in their fully nonlinear evolutions.[8]
- **Evidence type:** Computational; fully nonlinear numerical evolution of the spherically symmetric Einstein-Maxwell-charged-scalar system.[8]
- **Caveat:** “Observed” refers to extracting signals at future null infinity in the simulations, not an astronomical observation; the setup is spherically symmetric and uses a charged scalar field.[8]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[8]
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Computational & data methods[8]

### 9. [Eulerian ion energisation in three-dimensional hybrid-Vlasov-Maxwell turbulence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23740)[9]
- **Abstract:** Three-dimensional hybrid-Vlasov-Maxwell simulations show that the contribution of agyrotropic ion currents to energisation varies strongly between the two studied ion-plasma beta values.[9]
- **Authors:** L. Richard, S. S. Cerri, O. Pezzi, C. Granier, J. M. TenBarge[9]
- **arXiv:** `2610.23740v1` (published 2026-10-16 16:49:12 UTC; categories: `physics.plasm-pl`)[9]
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors provide a quantitative decomposition of ion energisation without assuming gyrotropy or scale separation, and report distinct dominant contributions at the two simulated beta values.[9]
- **Claimed result:** The agyrotropic term accounts for 21% of energisation at βᵢ₀ = 0.25 and 91% at βᵢ₀ = 4; the dominant instantaneous term changes with βᵢ₀.[9]
- **Evidence type:** Computational; decaying turbulence in three-dimensional hybrid Vlasov-Maxwell simulations at βᵢ₀ = 0.25 and 4.[9]
- **Caveat:** Conclusions are based on the two simulated initial beta regimes and resolved scales; they do not by themselves establish the same partition in every astrophysical or laboratory plasma.[9]
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-10-19.[9]
- **Themes:** Plasma & fluid physics; Computational & data methods[9]

- **Model dependence made more explicit:** The cosmology paper shows how early-universe fluid composition can affect gravitational-wave forecasts,[7] while the black-hole and plasma papers use numerical systems to isolate which signals diagnose nonlinear collapse or ion energisation.[8][9]

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Surface-code memory:** The authors identify long-lived correlations and a weakening distance advantage in long runs.[1] Follow-up work could measure how those correlations vary with qubit placement, circuit choices, and longer storage times, to test whether the reported suppression survives realistic memory durations.[1]
- **TAO spectrum extraction:** The first results establish detector operation and an on/off excess, but not the final precision spectrum.[2] Extended data and calibration across position, energy, and channel stability would test how the commissioning performance translates into spectral uncertainty.[2]
- **Rare-decay normalization:** The Ξ_b branching-fraction uncertainty is dominated by the normalization channel and SU(3)-breaking effects.[3] Improved normalization measurements or tighter theory constraints would sharpen the branching-fraction result.[3]
- **Hot-QCD perturbation theory:** The two calculations should be compared across temperature ranges and against continuum-extrapolated lattice data to identify where the completed order-g⁶ expansion is quantitatively predictive; agreement with fitted lattice values alone does not settle convergence.[4][5]
- **Multi-fluid gravitational-wave forecasts:** The new formalism predicts sensitivity to fluid composition.[7] A useful next step is to map which combinations of component abundances and transitions produce distinguishable spectra, then compare those predictions with detector sensitivities; this remains a proposed test, not a detection claim.[7]
- **Numerical model scope:** The black-hole result uses spherical symmetry and charged scalar matter,[8] and the plasma study samples two initial βᵢ regimes.[9]
  Nonspherical collapse tests and broader plasma parameter scans would probe how robust the reported diagnostics and energy partitions are outside those setups.[8][9]
- **Transferability of magneto-ionic switching:** Replication in related materials and under varied cycle conditions would test whether reversible switching is a broader design principle or specific to the NiO/nanoscale-Ni mechanism reported here.[6]
