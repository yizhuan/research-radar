### 1. [The Brown Dwarf Desert is a Mass-Ratio Desert: Astrometric Demographics of 63,254 Stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12881)
- **Abstract:** Using Hipparcos and Gaia astrometry for 63,254 stars, the authors infer that the brown-dwarf companion deficit is centered near a 1.8% companion-to-star mass ratio across host masses, rather than at a fixed companion mass.
- **Authors:** William Thompson, Alexandre Bouchard-Côté, Dori Blakely, Trevor Campbell, Jessica Speedie, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.12881v1` (published 2026-11-09T18:59:28Z; categories: `astro-ph.EP`, `astro-ph.SR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A 63,254-star joint astrometric demographics analysis spans M-to-B hosts and complements narrower radial-velocity and imaging surveys; the authors report submission to ApJL. Placement reflects sample scale and cross-method relevance, not measured readership.
- **Claimed result:** The authors estimate the desert minimum at mass ratio q = 1.76 (+0.26/-0.19)%, and report opposite metallicity trends below and above it; they interpret this as consistent with distinct formation channels.
- **Evidence type:** observational; hierarchical population analysis of Hipparcos and Gaia DR2/DR3 astrometry, with Gaia DR3 metallicities for a subset.
- **Caveat:** The inference is limited to the modeled 3–94-year companion orbits and to the survey's nonuniform astrometric sensitivity; the authors note that Gaia DR4 should sharpen some constraints, while longer-period companions still benefit from Hipparcos data.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 2. [Search for a Dispersion Measure Excess Toward Nearby Galaxy Halos Using Fast Radio Bursts from the Second CHIME/FRB Baseband Catalog](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12860)
- **Abstract:** A stack of 1,748 localized CHIME/FRB sources yields a tentative dispersion-measure excess toward nearby galaxy halos, which the authors use to constrain circumgalactic electron density.
- **Authors:** Affan Khadir, Ayush Pandhi, Jason Hessels, Calvin Leung, B. M. Gaensler, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.12860v1` (published 2026-11-09T18:49:50Z; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.CO`, `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It combines 1,748 sub-arcminute-localized FRBs with foreground-galaxy halo intersections and reports quantitative significance and a cross-correlation analysis; it is cross-listed across three astrophysics subfields.
- **Claimed result:** For halos within 50 Mpc, the authors find a mean DM excess of 119 ± 44 pc cm⁻³ (p = 0.0053, about 2.6σ); the impact-parameter trend out to 150 Mpc is also about 2.6σ and is described as tentative.
- **Evidence type:** observational; stacking and cross-correlation analysis of the Second CHIME/FRB Baseband Catalog and nearby-galaxy samples.
- **Caveat:** The signal is modest rather than a detection claim; host-galaxy FRBs can bias the smallest-impact-parameter bins, and finite FRB localization blurs the inferred halo profile.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 3. [AGN Feedback Turbulence in Cool-core Clusters: Evidence from XRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12845)
- **Abstract:** XRISM velocity measurements and tailored simulations suggest that AGN-driven turbulence dominates the core of A1795, while merger-driven motions dominate in A2029, consistent with an evolutionary picture for cool-core clusters.
- **Authors:** Congyao Zhang, Eugene Churazov, Irina Zhuravleva, Hannah McCall, Annie Heinrich, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.12845v1` (published 2026-11-09T18:39:54Z; categories: `astro-ph.HE`, `astro-ph.CO`, `astro-ph.GA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The study compares XRISM observations of two clusters with tailored hydrodynamic simulations, linking intracluster gas kinematics to merger history; the authors report submission to A&A.
- **Claimed result:** The authors infer feedback-dominated velocity dispersion in A1795's core, potentially supplying turbulent heating comparable to radiative cooling, but only an upper limit on AGN feedback in A2029, where merger motions dominate.
- **Evidence type:** observational; XRISM cluster velocity profiles interpreted with turbulent and tailored merger simulations.
- **Caveat:** Separating feedback from merger motions depends on simplified turbulence prescriptions and uncertain merger geometry; the A2029 feedback estimate is explicitly only an upper limit.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 4. [First demonstration of long-distance electron drift in liquid xenon with the Xenoscope time projection chamber](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12808)
- **Abstract:** The Xenoscope chamber demonstrates electron drift over 2.6 m in liquid xenon and measures drift velocities at two electric-field strengths, providing a benchmark for future large xenon detectors.
- **Authors:** N. Angelides, M. Babicz, E. E. Barillier, L. Baudis, H. Birch, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.12808v1` (published 2026-11-09T18:14:39Z; categories: `physics.ins-det`, `astro-ph.IM`, `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A first meter-scale drift demonstration with reported velocity uncertainties is directly relevant to next-generation XLZD detector design and is cross-listed across instrumentation, astrophysics instrumentation, and high-energy experiment archives.
- **Claimed result:** Using cosmic-muon ionization and external muon timing, the authors measure drift velocities of 1.320 ± 0.006 mm/μs at 80.92 ± 0.36 V/cm and 1.436 ± 0.001 mm/μs at 96.00 ± 0.42 V/cm over 2.6 m.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; dual-phase liquid-xenon Xenoscope time-projection chamber with cosmic-muon signals and external muon tagging.
- **Caveat:** This demonstrates long drift and charge readout in the prototype, not the performance of a full-scale next-generation observatory under all operating conditions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Instrumentation & detectors; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 5. [Tetragonal-to-orthorhombic distortion in CeRh$_{2}$As$_{2}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12873)
- **Abstract:** Directional thermal-expansion measurements on CeRh₂As₂ show a very small orthorhombic distortion below its phase-I ordering temperature, constraining possible order parameters in this superconducting material.
- **Authors:** P. Khanenko, M. Pfeiffer, K. Semeniuk, E. Hassinger, P. Thalmeier, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2611.12873v1` (published 2026-11-09T18:57:10Z; categories: `cond-mat.str-el`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper reports a direct symmetry-breaking measurement (orthorhombicity around 10⁻⁷) in a material with two superconducting phases and an unresolved phase-I order parameter; this is a concrete experimental result, though no independent attention signal was verified.
- **Claimed result:** The authors find broken fourfold in-plane symmetry below T₀ ≈ 0.46 K and argue that this constrains phase-I order and may disfavor a multicomponent superconducting order parameter developing within phase I.
- **Evidence type:** experimental; longitudinal thermal-expansion measurements along four in-plane crystallographic directions on a single crystal.
- **Caveat:** The structural distortion is extremely small, and the order parameter of phase I remains undetermined; the implication for superconducting symmetry is conditional rather than a direct identification.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

### 6. [Forward modeling linear variable filter data: High resolution PAH and Paschen-$\alpha$ maps from SPHEREx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12846)
- **Abstract:** The authors forward-model SPHEREx linear-variable-filter observations to make higher-resolution PAH and Paschen-α emission maps with reduced striping artifacts in selected Galactic and nearby-galaxy fields.
- **Authors:** Andrew K. Saydjari, Connor Hainje, Benjamin Dodge, Philipp Frank, David W. Hogg, Susan E. Clark
- **arXiv:** `2611.12846v1` (published 2026-11-09T18:40:04Z; categories: `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.IM`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper presents a scalable processing approach for SPHEREx's spatially varying spectral sampling and demonstrates maps at R ~ 35 and 8.5-arcsec FWHM across several astrophysical environments; it links galaxy science and instrumentation methods.
- **Claimed result:** The authors produce 3.3-μm and 3.4-μm maps for fields including M31, M33, the Rosette Nebula, and Corona Australis, reporting a 3.3-μm sensitivity as low as 2.7 × 10⁻⁸ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² sr⁻¹ in the demonstrated fields.
- **Evidence type:** computational; forward modeling of SPHEREx linear-variable-filter data, with pixel-level data handling, stellar masking, and conditional Gaussian-process infill.
- **Caveat:** The paper demonstrates selected fields rather than a completed all-sky PAH map; its estimate that a two-year survey extension could reach WISE-like sensitivity is a forecast dependent on a modified scan strategy.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-11-10 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Instrumentation & detectors; Computational & data methods

## Trending Research Themes

- **Large-sample inference of faint astrophysical components:** Thompson et al. use Gaia/Hipparcos astrometry to infer companion demographics, while Khadir et al. stack localized FRB dispersion measures to probe diffuse halo gas; both use population-level inference to extract signals beyond individual detections.
- **Instrument capability is shaping the questions:** XRISM enables spatially resolved cluster-gas kinematics (Zhang et al.), Xenoscope tests meter-scale charge drift for future xenon observatories (Angelides et al.), and SPHEREx's variable-filter data motivate a new map-making pipeline (Saydjari et al.). These are distinct instruments and results, not independent confirmations of one another.
- **Model-assisted interpretation remains central:** Brown-dwarf occurrence rates, FRB halo profiles, and cluster turbulence all require population or forward models (Thompson et al.; Khadir et al.; Zhang et al.); SPHEREx adds data-driven infill and forward modeling to measurement pipelines (Saydjari et al.).
- **An isolated condensed-matter signal:** Khanenko et al. report a tiny orthorhombic distortion in CeRh₂As₂; this is a notable single-paper result in this batch, not evidence of a repeated subject across the selected papers.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem (authors' stated limitation):** The astrometric desert result is constrained by period coverage and sensitivity, especially for longer-period companions; the authors point to Gaia DR4 and continued use of Hipparcos as ways to improve it. **Research direction (synthesis):** Refit the population with DR4 epoch astrometry and combine it with imaging constraints to test how the desert changes with separation. [Thompson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12881)
- **Open problem (authors' stated limitation):** The FRB halo excess is only about 2.6σ, and host-galaxy cross-matches and localization uncertainty affect the innermost profile. **Research direction (synthesis):** Increase the localized FRB sample, prioritize intermediate-redshift sightlines, and repeat the stack with explicit host-association and localization-error models. [Khadir et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12860)
- **Open problem (authors' stated limitation):** In XRISM cluster data, merger-driven and feedback-driven velocities are difficult to disentangle and depend on cluster history and model choices. **Research direction (synthesis):** Obtain broader spatial/azimuthal coverage and compare relaxed and recently merged clusters using matched simulations and resolved velocity maps. [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12845)
- **Open problem (authors' stated limitation):** The phase-I order parameter in CeRh₂As₂ remains unknown despite the measured symmetry breaking. **Research direction (synthesis):** Pair symmetry-sensitive probes with measurements across the phase-I/superconducting boundary to test which candidate order parameters remain compatible with the distortion. [Khanenko et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12873)
- **Open problem (authors' stated scope):** SPHEREx map quality and sensitivity are demonstrated on selected fields, while all-sky performance under a longer or modified scan is projected. **Research direction (synthesis):** Validate the pipeline on additional fields and then evaluate whether the proposed scan changes deliver the forecast sensitivity without reintroducing spatial striping. [Saydjari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12846)
