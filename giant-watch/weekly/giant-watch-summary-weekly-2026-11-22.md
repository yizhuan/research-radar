# Giant Watch Summary - Weekly - 2026-11-22

## Headline

Anthropic's record-breaking IPO anchors a week in which frontier labs raced out cheaper, agent-oriented models. The episode's throughline is cost-per-task competition: Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic all positioned new releases around efficiency rather than raw capability alone.

## Top Developments

### 1. Anthropic begins trading at $1.5T valuation a day after shipping Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 (Anthropic)
Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2, priced 20 percent below Fable 5.1 per token, and Claude Mythos 5.2, a version with fewer biology and cybersecurity safeguards available only to organizations approved through its Cyber Verification or Life Sciences Verification programs. The Batch reports Anthropic introduced the models a day before marketing its IPO, with shares expected to start trading November 19 at a $1.5 trillion market capitalization; a Baidu Baike entry and CNET both describe a November 19 Nasdaq listing, with CNET citing an $83 opening price and roughly $1.5 trillion valuation.
Why it matters: The largest single event of the week: a frontier lab going public at a scale that resets the sector's capital benchmark, paired with a model release that widens access to its top tier while cutting price.
Refs: https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/claude-fable-gets-a-price-cut, https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/openai-chatgpt-astra-6-2-microsoft-ignite-news/, https://baike.baidu.com/item/Anthropic/62639515?bst=1795057021.182acc5cb2c7a909#4

### 2. Google launches Gemini 4 Neon, a mid-tier model pitched at flagship intelligence for roughly half the cost (Google)
Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, positioned between Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen, with a 1 million-token context window, four thinking levels, and API pricing of $3 per million input and $15 per million output tokens. Google's comparison table places Neon at 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 (59) and GPT-6.2 Sol (57); at default high thinking it is described as on par with Carbon at 32 percent lower cost per task. It is available to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, in Antigravity, and via the paid API.
Why it matters: Google's fourth Gemini 4 model in seven weeks, and the clearest articulation of the week's cost-per-task framing; it also lands squarely in the same benchmark conversation as Anthropic's and OpenAI's releases.
Refs: https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-19/doc-inizfunk7825519.shtml, https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4076058991219589, https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/google-says-gemini-4-neon-can-hang-with-claude-opus-5-5-and-gpt-6-2-sol-for-less/

### 3. OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, cutting Astra's API price 20 percent (OpenAI)
OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra for demanding professional work and GPT-6.2 Sol for everyday multi-step tasks, both available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users through ChatGPT Work and Codex and to developers via the API. OpenAI says Astra scored 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra on the Agents' Last Exam benchmark at less than one-third the cost, and 5.5 points above Claude Fable 5.2 at 5 percent of the cost; Astra's API input and output prices fell 20 percent versus GPT-6 Astra with cached input 60 percent lower. OpenAI also said it added tighter cybersecurity safeguards because the models are better at identifying and exploiting vulnerabilities.
Why it matters: A direct competitive claim against Anthropic's new flagship, and a second lab in one week framing its release around token efficiency and lower cost per task.
Refs: https://enterpriseai.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/openai-launches-gpt-6-2-astra-sol-with-focus-on-professional-work-and-cost-efficiency/135952052?utm_source=Mailer&utm_medium=ET_batch&utm_campaign=etenterpriseai_news_2026-11-19&dt=2026-11-19, https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9624314-model-release-notes, https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/openai-chatgpt-astra-6-2-microsoft-ignite-news/

### 4. DeepSeek restricts long conversations and document reading, citing insufficient compute (DeepSeek)
Since November 15, DeepSeek users have received notices that long conversations inactive for 72 hours can no longer be processed and that long documents are capped at roughly the first 20 pages or 10,000 tokens, both attributed to a shortage of computing resources. Caixin reporting via ThinkChina notes DeepSeek offers no paid consumer subscription, unlike Doubao and Kimi, and that users have asked for one; it also reports Chinese labs broadly slowing foundational model releases in favor of agents, harnesses, and enterprise deals, and that DeepSeek announced application APIs on November 13 covering search, file input, and model safety.
Why it matters: The clearest evidence this week that compute scarcity is now shaping product behavior at a major Chinese lab, and a concrete data point on the shift from model releases to ecosystem monetization.
Refs: https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/deepseeks-computing-crunch-pushes-chinas-ai-developers-beyond-models?ref=top-hero, https://baike.baidu.com/item/DeepSeek/65368136?bst=1794800004.a7a916c0b53d7257#1

### 5. UK MP's lawsuit against xAI over Grok-generated sexualised images advances (xAI)
Labour MP Jess Asato is suing SpaceXSI (formerly xAI) for damages after sexualised images and videos, including depictions of her being sexually assaulted, were generated and shared using Grok. The BBC reports SpaceXSI filed a defence on October 30 admitting the chatbot is designed to expand on short user prompts by pulling data from X threads and the wider internet to create deepfakes; Asato's claim alleges design choices make the company responsible and that it misused her private information and breached data protection rules. SpaceXSI previously said users would no longer be able to generate sexualised images of real people.
Why it matters: A named-claimant liability case that tests whether model design choices, rather than user conduct alone, can ground responsibility for generated imagery.
Refs: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cq0k6gezp50vo

### 6. xAI retires grok-build-0.1 from the API (xAI)
Per xAI's release notes, grok-build-0.1 reached end of life on November 19, 2026 and is no longer served on the API. The same notes list Grok 4.7 as the current frontier model with a 500k context window and $2/$0.50/$6 per million tokens below 200k prompt tokens.
Why it matters: A minor lifecycle event, but a concrete dated change to xAI's developer surface in the window.
Refs: https://docs.x.ai/developers/release-notes

### 7. Z.ai publishes GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash pricing and model documentation (GLM)
Z.ai's documentation lists GLM-5.3 at $1.40 input and $4.40 output per million tokens and GLM-5.3-Flash at $0.15/$0.50, with a 1M context window for both; GLM-5.3 is described as offering Claude Fable 5-level coding and agent capabilities, and GLM-5.3-Flash as matching Claude Opus 4.8 in intelligence score at 1/40 the price. A third-party explainer notes GLM-5.3 ships under a custom license rather than MIT, with a security-review condition for large model-as-a-service providers.
Why it matters: Documents a low-cost open-weight challenger in the same price-per-task conversation as the US frontier releases, though the underlying model release predates the window.
Refs: https://docs.z.ai/guides/overview/overview, https://docs.z.ai/guides/overview/pricing, https://glm5.app/blog/is-glm-free

### 8. Qwen4-Max preview runs toward its November 25 model-ID retirement (Qwen)
Alibaba's Qwen4-Max preview, released November 11, is documented with a 1M-token context window, text/image/video input, deep thinking, and OpenAI, Responses, and Anthropic Messages compatibility, priced at $1.413 input and $4.24 output per million tokens in the global deployment scope. Alibaba developer documentation states the preview model ID goes offline at 24:00 on November 25, with the stable version to ship under a new model name, requiring integrators to plan a config change.
Why it matters: A dated migration deadline for developers building on Alibaba's flagship, and a concrete marker of the preview-to-GA cadence among Chinese vendors.
Refs: https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/qwen4-max, https://developer.aliyun.com/article/1786039

### 9. Moonshot documents Kimi K3.1 Pro as its flagship with a 1M-token context (Kimi)
Kimi API Platform documentation describes kimi-k3.1-pro as the flagship model for long-horizon coding and end-to-end knowledge work with native visual understanding and a 1M-token context window, with reasoning always on and configurable via reasoning_effort (low/high/max, default max). The platform also documents a hosted-agents offering that provides the agent harness as a cloud service.
Why it matters: Confirms Kimi's current flagship positioning and its push into hosted agent infrastructure, though the release itself predates the window.
Refs: https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/introduction, https://platform.kimi.com/docs/features

## Cross-Org Themes

- Frontier labs competed on cost per task rather than headline capability alone, with Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI each framing new releases around price cuts or efficiency claims.
- Agentic and long-horizon work is the shared product target: Anthropic's office suite, Google's Antigravity orchestration, OpenAI's professional-work positioning, and Kimi's hosted agents all point the same direction.
- Compute scarcity is now a product constraint, visible in DeepSeek's usage limits and in the broader shift of Chinese labs toward harnesses and enterprise revenue.

## Takeaway

The competitive axis has moved from benchmark leadership to cost per completed task, which makes independent pricing and harness-level evaluations the numbers to watch next. The near-term test is whether the preview-to-stable transitions at Alibaba and the compute limits at DeepSeek translate into measurable developer churn.
