### 1. [Thinning and sprinkling: from robust sampling to almost Hamiltonicity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30165)
- **Abstract:** The authors develop a thinning–sprinkling method for robustness under random vertex sampling and use it to obtain almost-Hamilton cycles in several highly connected graph families.
- **Authors:** Micha Christoph, Zach Hunter, Benny Sudakov
- **arXiv:** `2609.30165v1` (published 2026-09-24 17:19:59 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract reports asymptotic solutions to longstanding conjectures of Chvátal and Lovász, a concrete multi-family theorem, and a general technique; independent web-search attention was sparse and Semantic Scholar citation counts were unavailable because its API returned rate limits.
- **Claimed result:** Random induced subgraphs of tough graphs, high-degree connected vertex-transitive graphs, and nearly regular sublinear expanders retain strong connectivity/expansion with high probability; the framework yields cycles of length `(1-o(1))n` and asymptotically resolves the cited Hamiltonicity conjectures under polylogarithmic hypotheses, according to the arXiv abstract.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Finite graphs with toughness, vertex-transitivity, or near-regular expansion; in one result, `k`-connected graphs with `k=ω(log n)`.
- **Caveat:** This is a new preprint; the summary reports the authors' claims, not an independent proof check. The “almost” cycle conclusion is asymptotic rather than an exact Hamilton cycle in every stated setting.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement-batch 2026-09-25
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 2. [Bounded-orbit wandering domains do not exist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30103)
- **Abstract:** The paper proves that transcendental entire functions have no wandering Fatou components with bounded forward orbit and gives a stronger meromorphic-function statement.
- **Authors:** Kostiantyn Drach, Leticia Pardo-Simón, Beno Učakar
- **arXiv:** `2609.30103v1` (published 2026-09-24 16:46:35 UTC; categories: `math.DS`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract explicitly says it settles a long-standing question and search results independently surfaced the “long-standing question” significance; Semantic Scholar data was unavailable because its API returned rate limits.
- **Claimed result:** No transcendental entire function has a wandering Fatou component whose forward orbit is bounded; more strongly, every point in a wandering domain of a transcendental meromorphic function has an unbounded orbit, according to the authors.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Transcendental entire or meromorphic dynamics, Fatou components, and wandering domains; the proof uses uniform hyperbolic-area estimates.
- **Caveat:** The result is a preprint claim and relies on the hyperbolic-area approach of an earlier paper; proof validity and the scope of any hidden technical hypotheses require expert reading.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement-batch 2026-09-25
- **Themes:** Dynamical systems

### 5. [A 1/64 spectral gap for surfaces with δ=1/2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30261)
- **Abstract:** For convex co-compact hyperbolic surfaces with exponent of convergence in a stated interval, the authors prove an essential spectral gap and obtain a `1/64−ε` gap at `δ=1/2`.
- **Authors:** Alex Cohen, Semyon Dyatlov
- **arXiv:** `2609.30261v1` (published 2026-09-24 17:59:34 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math.SP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It provides an explicit quantitative spectral-gap constant and a new Fractal Uncertainty Principle, with cross-listing between PDE and spectral theory; search results independently exposed the technical result, while Semantic Scholar counts were unavailable due to rate limiting.
- **Claimed result:** The essential gap has size `β = 7/8(1/2−δ) + δ/32 − ε` for `δ` in `(2/5,14/27)`, hence `β=1/64−ε` at `δ=1/2`, using a two-ends Furstenberg theorem.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Convex co-compact hyperbolic surfaces and the exponent of convergence of the Poincaré series; `ε>0` is arbitrary.
- **Caveat:** The quantitative interpretation depends on the paper's definition of essential spectral gap and on the cited Furstenberg input; no proof assessment was performed here.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement-batch 2026-09-25
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE; Mathematical physics

### 8. [Diophantine approximation by primes and Landau–Siegel zeros](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30207)
- **Abstract:** Conditional on infinitely many, or sufficiently strong, Siegel zeros, the authors obtain infinitely many primes giving unusually close Diophantine approximations to `αp+β`.
- **Authors:** Sun-Kai Leung, Stelios Sachpazis
- **arXiv:** `2609.30207v1` (published 2026-09-24 17:41:46 UTC; categories: `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper links a famous unresolved zero phenomenon to an explicit prime-approximation exponent and presents two quantified conditional outcomes; no independent attention metric was available on this first-day snapshot.
- **Claimed result:** For irrational `α` of finite type and real `β`, infinitely many primes satisfy `||αp+β||≤p^(−1/3+ε)` assuming infinitely many Siegel zeros, and a stronger `p^(−1/3−1/20)` bound under a sufficiently strong-zero assumption.
- **Assumptions and setting:** `α>0` irrational of finite type, arbitrary real `β`, sufficiently small `ε>0`, and the stated conditional hypotheses about Landau–Siegel zeros.
- **Caveat:** These are conditional results under hypotheses that are themselves unresolved; the paper does not establish the existence of Siegel zeros.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, announcement-batch 2026-09-25
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory
