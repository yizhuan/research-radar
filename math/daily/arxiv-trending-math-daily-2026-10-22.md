### 1. [Scalar--Mean Rigidity for Hyperbolic Balls in Dimensions $2\le n\le 5$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29488)
- **Abstract:** The paper proves a non-spin hyperbolic scalar--mean rigidity theorem in dimensions 2 through 5, adds a weighted Euclidean ball--torus rigidity result through total dimension 7, and gives a counterexample to the analogous hyperbolic product statement.
- **Authors:** Chenghang Lu
- **arXiv:** `2610.29488v1` (published 2026-10-21 09:44:17 UTC; categories: `math.DG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract identifies a low-dimensional case of a problem posed by Gromov and pairs it with a second rigidity theorem and a counterexample; the main theorem has a clearly checkable geometric scope.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves that, for the stated scalar-curvature and boundary-mean-curvature bounds, a nonzero-degree 1-Lipschitz boundary map to the corresponding hyperbolic sphere extends to an isometry with the hyperbolic ball (arXiv abstract and Theorem 1.1).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Compact, connected, oriented smooth Riemannian manifold with connected nonempty boundary; dimension $2\le n\le5$; scalar curvature at least $-n(n-1)$; boundary mean curvature at least $(n-1)\coth\rho$; the boundary map targets the sphere of radius $\sinh\rho$.
- **Caveat:** The theorem stops at dimension 5; the paper also shows that a direct hyperbolic ball--torus analogue fails.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Geometry & topology

### 2. [A proof of Ward's conjecture on Griesmer codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29275)
- **Abstract:** For a Griesmer code over a field of size $q=p^f$, the authors prove Ward's conjectured lower bound on the $p$-divisibility of all codeword weights in terms of the $p$-adic valuation of the minimum distance.
- **Authors:** Haihua Deng, Weiye Zhang
- **arXiv:** `2610.29275v1` (published 2026-10-21 06:26:54 UTC; categories: `math.CO`, `cs.IT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is a stated proof of a named conjecture, and its cross-listing links a concrete coding-theory result to information theory.
- **Claimed result:** If $C$ is an $[n,k,d]_q$ Griesmer code, $q=p^f$, and $e=\nu_p(d)$, then the authors prove $\nu_p(C)\ge\max\{0,e-f+1\}$ (arXiv abstract and Theorem 1.1).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Linear $q$-ary code attaining the Griesmer bound; $p$ is prime, $q=p^f$, and $\nu_p(C)$ is the minimum $p$-adic valuation of the nonzero codeword weights.
- **Caveat:** The conclusion is specific to Griesmer codes and does not classify divisibility for arbitrary linear codes.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 3. [Effective stability and sharp logarithmic time scales for Hamiltonian PDEs on the circle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29307)
- **Abstract:** The authors derive regularity-dependent stability times for small Hamiltonian PDE solutions on the circle and construct examples with matching norm-amplification times, establishing sharpness under their analytic and arithmetic hypotheses.
- **Authors:** Bingqi Yu, Yong Li
- **arXiv:** `2610.29307v1` (published 2026-10-21 07:01:24 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math.DS`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper combines lower stability bounds with matching upper-time examples, verifies hypotheses for several PDE families, and bridges analysis with dynamical systems.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove Gevrey and logarithmic ultra-differentiable stability scales, verify them for convolution NLS, fractional NLS with fixed noninteger exponent $\eta>1/2$, and a beam equation, and construct two fixed analytic nonlocal PDEs whose first prescribed norm amplification occurs on matching scales (arXiv abstract and Theorem 1.1).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Small solutions on the one-dimensional torus; an analytic coefficient-majorant condition and a frequency/small-divisor hypothesis are required. The matching escape-time examples are for two specific analytic nonlocal Hamiltonian equations.
- **Caveat:** The sharp upper-time examples are not established for every Hamiltonian PDE satisfying the stability hypotheses; the conclusions depend on the paper's arithmetic and regularity assumptions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 4. [A well-posedness theory for the 2D inhomogeneous incompressible Euler equations and a class of global-in-time solutions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30096)
- **Abstract:** The author proves critical-regularity local well-posedness for a symmetric class of 2D inhomogeneous Euler data, then obtains global solutions for a related one-dimensional reduction and a long-time density/relaxation dichotomy.
- **Authors:** Ibrahim Suleiman
- **arXiv:** `2610.30096v1` (published 2026-10-21 17:45:10 UTC; categories: `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper connects a critical local theory to a nonperturbative global solution class and explicitly relates the local result to a problem posed by P. L. Lions.
- **Claimed result:** The author establishes local well-posedness for $m$-fold symmetric functions at critical regularity, and global well-posedness for a class of initial data in the reduced system with 0-homogeneous vorticity and density (arXiv abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Two-dimensional inhomogeneous incompressible Euler Cauchy problem; the local theorem assumes $m$-fold symmetry and permits velocity growth linear in space. The global theorem concerns the associated one-dimensional system and data away from equilibrium.
- **Caveat:** The global result is for a special reduced class, not general global well-posedness of the full 2D inhomogeneous Euler equations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 5. [Threshold Attainment and Sharp Asymptotics for $L^p$-Perturbed Moser--Trudinger Functionals on $\mathbb{R}^2$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29402)
- **Abstract:** The authors settle endpoint attainment cases for a critical planar Moser--Trudinger variational problem and derive asymptotic expansions and profile limits for its extremals in several penalty regimes.
- **Authors:** Kaiwen Guo, Yanjun Liu
- **arXiv:** `2610.29402v1` (published 2026-10-21 08:31:11 UTC; categories: `math.AP`, `math.FA`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract reports both endpoint existence and quantitative two-term asymptotics, with an exact transfer identity extending the conclusions to every constant planar norm.
- **Claimed result:** Under the unit $H^1(\mathbb R^2)$ constraint, the authors prove attainment at the specified thresholds, a two-term expansion for $p>4$ as $\lambda\to\infty$, and strong subsequential convergence of rescaled centered radial extremals (arXiv abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** The functional has critical exponential $e^{4\pi u^2}$, penalty $\lambda|u|^p$, and $p\ge2$; asymptotic statements distinguish $p>4$ from the endpoint regimes and use centered radial extremals.
- **Caveat:** Several profile conclusions are subsequential and radial; the abstract does not assert uniqueness of extremizers.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 6. [The exceptional set of Goldbach numbers within the set of squares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29470)
- **Abstract:** The authors bound the number of integers $n=2m^2<X$ that fail to be a sum of two primes by $X^{19/24+o(1)}$.
- **Authors:** A. Perelli, J. Pintz
- **arXiv:** `2610.29470v1` (published 2026-10-21 09:25:44 UTC; categories: `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It gives a precise exceptional-set exponent for an additive-prime problem on a sparse structured sequence, a clean number-theoretic result with a readily testable quantitative statement.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove that the count of non-Goldbach numbers of the form $2m^2$ below $X$ is at most $X^{19/24+o(1)}$ (arXiv abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Integers of the special form $n=2m^2$ with $n<X$; the assertion is an upper bound on the exceptional set, not a statement that every such number is Goldbach.
- **Caveat:** The estimate leaves room for an infinite exceptional set and does not settle the Goldbach representation for each square-doubled integer.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Algebra & number theory

- **Caveat:** One non-empirical term still depends on the tail behavior of regeneration times, and the framework requires regenerative structure.

### 8. [Minimax Optimality of the NPMLE for Gaussian Mixtures in Hellinger Distance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30109)
- **Abstract:** For univariate Gaussian mixtures, the authors establish minimax squared-Hellinger rates for the unconstrained NPMLE under sub-Gaussian mixing and for a compactly constrained NPMLE under compactly supported mixing.
- **Authors:** Shayan Hundrieser, Tudor Manole
- **arXiv:** `2610.30109v1` (published 2026-10-21 17:53:10 UTC; categories: `math.ST`, `cs.IT`, `cs.LG`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper states matching minimax-optimal rates in two different mixing-measure regimes and derives a cross-application to Gaussian empirical Bayes, spanning statistics, information theory, and machine learning.
- **Claimed result:** With $n$ i.i.d. observations, the unconstrained NPMLE attains squared-Hellinger rate $\log n/n$ for sub-Gaussian mixing; with compactly supported mixing, the interval-constrained NPMLE attains $\log n/(n\log\log n)$ (arXiv abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Density estimation for univariate Gaussian mixtures from independent identically distributed samples; the two rates require, respectively, a sub-Gaussian true mixing measure or compact support and a matching compact constraint.
- **Caveat:** The guarantees are tied to the stated mixing-measure classes; they do not establish the same rates for unrestricted heavy-tailed mixing distributions.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 2026-10-22 batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Machine learning theory

- **Abstract:** The authors extend an Alexandrov--Bakelman--Pucci estimate to fully nonlinear elliptic equations with singular or degenerate coefficients and gradient dependence, then apply it to several nonlinear PDE classes.

- **Claimed result:** The authors establish a weighted ABP-type bound for classical subsolutions and viscosity subsolutions with locally Lipschitz gradients; it yields sup-norm estimates for the stated equation families (arXiv abstract).
- **Assumptions and setting:** Fully nonlinear elliptic equations with singular or degenerate coefficients and nonlinear gradient dependence; growth rates are general, but a smallness condition is imposed when the associated gradient weight is integrable.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Author-stated scope / proposed extension:** Lu's non-spin hyperbolic rigidity theorem covers dimensions 2–5. Extending the result to dimensions $n\ge6$, or identifying a sharp obstruction there, would test how far the Jang-deformation and capillary-$\mu$-bubble approach can go (2610.29488).
- **Author-stated scope / proposed extension:** Suleiman's global result concerns a reduced 0-homogeneous class, while the local theorem uses $m$-fold symmetry. A concrete next step is to determine whether global control persists for broader data or with weaker symmetry assumptions (2610.30096).
- **Author-stated hypothesis / proposed extension:** The stability scales in Yu and Li depend on their small-divisor and analytic coefficient-majorant assumptions, and matching escape examples are given for two fixed nonlocal equations. Testing the same sharpness question for more standard local Hamiltonian PDEs or weaker arithmetic hypotheses would clarify the role of those conditions (2610.29307).
- **Author-stated model classes / proposed extension:** The NPMLE rates are proved for sub-Gaussian or compactly supported mixing measures. It would be useful to determine which rates or robust alternatives survive under heavy-tailed mixing or unknown support (2610.30109).
- **Synthesis direction:** Perelli and Pintz give an exceptional-set upper bound of $X^{19/24+o(1)}$ for Goldbach failures along $2m^2$. Improving the exponent, or testing whether stronger hypotheses reduce the exceptional set further, would sharpen the result without claiming that the paper resolves the individual Goldbach cases (2610.29470).
