## Notable recent papers (editorial order)

### 1. [Observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz transition $J/\psi\to e^+ e^- \eta_c$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09626)
- **Abstract:** BESIII reports the first observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz decay $J/\psi\to e^+e^-\eta_c$, measuring its rate relative to $J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_c$ and extracting the $q^2$-dependent form factors.
- **Authors:** BESIII Collaboration (listed authors begin M. Ablikim, M. N. Achasov, P. Adlarson, X. C. Ai, C. S. Akondi, et al.)
- **arXiv:** `2610.09626v1` (published 2026-10-07T08:03:09Z; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A first-observation claim backed by a stated sample of 10.087 billion $J/\psi$ decays, a measured branching ratio, and separately reported statistical and systematic errors.
- **Claimed result:** The BESIII authors measure the relative branching fraction as $(0.65\pm0.02_{\rm stat}\pm0.07_{\rm sys})\%$; they find no significant deviation of the extracted form factor from theoretical prediction.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; BESIII detector at the BEPCII $e^+e^-$ collider.
- **Caveat:** The result establishes the decay but not a discrepancy with theory; the reported systematic uncertainty is larger than the statistical one.
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 8 October 2026 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Instrumentation & detectors

### 2. [Remote magnon sensing detects the universal phase stiffness jump in Bi$_2$Sr$_2$CaCu$_2$O$_{8+\delta}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08934)
- **Abstract:** The authors use propagating magnons in CrSBr as remote optical probes of an underdoped cuprate and report a field-induced Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless phase-stiffness jump.
- **Authors:** Yue Sun, Mai Nguyen, Naiyuan James Zhang, Dingbin Huang, Shuai Yuan, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.08934v1` (published 2026-10-06T18:03:36Z; categories: `cond-mat.supr-con`, `cond-mat.str-el`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract describes a contactless, local measurement and a direct observation of the universal stiffness jump, linking a magnon-sensing method to a central question in two-dimensional superconductivity.
- **Claimed result:** The authors say the magnon group velocity encodes the cuprate's phase stiffness through Meissner screening and reveals a parallel-field-induced BKT transition.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; optical excitation/detection of CrSBr magnon wavepackets in a CrSBr/underdoped-Bi$_2$Sr$_2$CaCu$_2$O$_{8+\delta}$ heterostructure.
- **Caveat:** The inference depends on the magnon-velocity/stiffness calibration and Meissner-screening model in this specific heterostructure; the abstract does not quantify the extraction uncertainty.
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 8 October 2026 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials

### 3. [Extreme Stellar Death and Galaxy Feedback at $z=2$: A Rest-frame Ultraviolet Characterization of the Strongly Lensed Superluminous Supernova 2025wny](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08903)
- **Abstract:** Keck-II ultraviolet spectroscopy and Pan-STARRS photometry characterize a strongly lensed, hydrogen-poor superluminous supernova at redshift 2.0155, measuring its magnification and host-galaxy properties.
- **Authors:** Willem B. Hoogendam, David O. Jones, Christopher J. Storfer, Luca Izzo, Kyle W. Davis, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.08903v1` (published 2026-10-06T18:00:00Z; categories: `astro-ph.HE`, `astro-ph.GA`, `astro-ph.SR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A rare high-redshift transient is examined with both rest-frame UV spectra and photometry; the abstract reports a lens magnification that is 2–4 times mass-model estimates and quantitative host outflow/escape-fraction results.
- **Claimed result:** The authors estimate a brightest-image magnification of $19.4\pm1.6$, an outflow speed near 575 km/s, and Ly$\alpha$ escape fraction $0.23\pm0.03$; a magnetar-plus-$^{56}$Ni fit matches near-peak light but not the late plateau.
- **Evidence type:** Observational; Keck-II/KCWI integral-field spectroscopy and Pan-STARRS photometry, interpreted with a transient light-curve model.
- **Caveat:** The late-time plateau points to an additional power source, and the magnetar parameters are model-fit inferences rather than direct measurements of the engine.
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 8 October 2026 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Astrophysics

### 4. [Toward a complete understanding of the properties of GW190521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09199)
- **Abstract:** A multi-family waveform reanalysis of the short, massive gravitational-wave event GW190521 finds a highly eccentric interpretation and shows how eccentricity can be confused with spin precession.
- **Authors:** Aasim Jan, Sophia Nicolella, Aaron Zimmerman, Deirdre Shoemaker, Katerina Chatziioannou, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.09199v1` (published 2026-10-06T22:50:37Z; categories: `gr-qc`, `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It revisits a difficult, high-mass event using multiple waveform families, including eccentric and precessing models, and reports explicit injection tests against numerical-relativity waveforms.
- **Claimed result:** The authors infer eccentricity $0.81^{+0.05}_{-0.01}$ at 5 Hz and a probability of at least 97% that the primary black hole is in the pair-instability mass gap; secondary mass-gap membership depends on the waveform model.
- **Evidence type:** Computational; gravitational-wave event reanalysis with waveform families, injections, and highly eccentric numerical-relativity waveforms.
- **Caveat:** The inferred eccentricity lies beyond the calibration regime of current models; mass and spin conclusions remain waveform/model dependent.
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 8 October 2026 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Astrophysics; Computational & data methods

### 5. [Wavelength-Resolved Timing of Near-Infrared Scintillation in Gaseous Xenon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10161)
- **Abstract:** The authors measure alpha-induced near-infrared xenon scintillation across 12 wavelength bands and separate a fast line-associated component from a slower continuum component.
- **Authors:** Robert Hammann, Steffen Form, Humaira Nazneen, Gabriel Schöpfer, Milan Ončák, Teresa Marrodán Undagoitia
- **arXiv:** `2610.10161v1` (published 2026-10-07T14:39:36Z; categories: `physics.ins-det`, `hep-ex`, `physics.chem-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It supplies wavelength-resolved timing and pressure-dependent measurements relevant to a prospective detector signal, rather than relying only on a proposed emission mechanism.
- **Claimed result:** The authors identify a nanosecond-scale fast component and a dominant microsecond-scale component; they infer an approximately 3 μs intrinsic lifetime under a radiative-decay interpretation and find an ionic-dimer transition more consistent with the continuum data.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; alpha-particle interactions in gaseous xenon, measured over approximately 950–1620 nm.
- **Caveat:** The emitting-state assignments remain candidate explanations, and the lifetime estimate depends on interpreting the slower kinetic process as radiative decay.
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 8 October 2026 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Instrumentation & detectors; Atomic, molecular & optical physics

### 6. [Event-by-event net-charge fluctuations in pp, OO, Ne-Ne, and Pb-Pb collisions with ALICE at the LHC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09586)
- **Abstract:** ALICE studies event-by-event net-electric-charge fluctuations across proton-proton and three nuclear collision systems, comparing their density dependence with several event generators.
- **Authors:** Nida Malik (for the ALICE Collaboration)
- **arXiv:** `2610.09586v1` (published 2026-10-07T07:30:43Z; categories: `nucl-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The analysis compares multiple collision systems and collision energies with a fluctuation observable chosen for robustness to detector-efficiency losses, alongside PYTHIA, EPOS4, AMPT, and HIJING calculations.
- **Claimed result:** The authors report a smooth, energy-independent decrease in the magnitude of $\nu_{dyn}[+,-]$ with increasing system size and a dominance of opposite-sign over like-sign charge correlations.
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; ALICE data at the CERN Large Hadron Collider, compared with event-generator calculations.
- **Caveat:** This is a six-page, one-figure conference proceeding; the fluctuation pattern and generator comparisons alone do not uniquely identify a QGP phase-transition signal.
- **Announcement type:** New submission, 8 October 2026 announcement batch.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Additional supernova power source (authors' stated issue):** The magnetar-plus-radioactive-decay fit does not explain SN 2025wny's late plateau. Follow-up late-time spectroscopy and photometry could test candidate power sources; LSST and Roman samples could check whether the inferred similarity to nearby SLSNe holds across a population. [SN 2025wny](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08903)
- **Eccentric-waveform calibration (authors' stated limitation):** GW190521's inferred eccentricity is beyond current waveform calibration. Expand numerical-relativity coverage and run cross-family injection/recovery tests in this regime before treating the eccentric interpretation as settled. [GW190521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09199)
- **What emits the xenon continuum? (authors' candidate mechanisms):** The ionic-dimer transition agrees better than the neutral-excimer alternative, but the assignment is not definitive. Pressure- and wavelength-resolved measurements with independent spectral/kinetic constraints could discriminate them. [Gaseous xenon NIR scintillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10161)
- **Independent validation of the remote stiffness readout (synthesis):** Test the CrSBr magnon-velocity calibration and inferred BKT jump across additional cuprate samples, temperatures, and field geometries, and compare with an independent stiffness probe. [Remote magnon sensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08934)
- **Precision versus theory for the Dalitz decay (synthesis):** The first observation agrees with the cited theoretical prediction within the analysis; improved control of systematics and form-factor precision would make the comparison more discriminating. [BESIII Dalitz transition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09626)
