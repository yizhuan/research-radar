## Notable Papers

### 1. Scaling public input on AI policy with pluralistic agents

- arXiv: `2611.16028`
- Organizations: Google
- Attribution: verified - Google DeepMind
- Problem: AI deployment spans many cultures and stakeholder groups, but gathering diverse public input for AI policy is hard to scale.
- Method: Pluralistic agents (AI representatives that deliberate for individuals) were tested in computational experiments and a preregistered study with 746 human participants, comparing pluralistic-agent deliberation against single-AI input and direct human discussion.
- Key result: Agents shifted policies toward represented personas, with influence depending on persona instructions (verbosity and lower cooperativeness increased influence); pluralistic-agent deliberation was rated more useful and fair than single-AI input, recovering 68% of the satisfaction gap and 82% of the fairness gap versus direct discussion, though direct human participation was preferred overall.
- Caveat: The authors recommend cautious use as an amplifier of human input, not a replacement; agent influence was sensitive to persona instructions.
- Why it matters: It offers an empirical, preregistered test of whether AI agents can broaden participation in AI policy while flagging limits relative to direct human deliberation.
