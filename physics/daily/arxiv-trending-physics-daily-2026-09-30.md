### 1. [Search for compressed electroweak SUSY production in final states with two τ-leptons in √s = 13 and 13.6 TeV pp collisions with the ATLAS detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37215)
- **Abstract:** ATLAS searches for compressed stau and electroweakino production in Run 2 and Run 3 proton collisions, finding results broadly consistent with the Standard Model alongside several 2.1–2.4σ local deviations[1].
- **Authors:** ATLAS Collaboration
- **arXiv:** `2609.37215v1` (published 2026-09-29 10:30:20 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A new collaboration search uses 140 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV plus 53 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV, tackles difficult compressed spectra, and is submitted to JHEP; its local excesses are interesting but not evidence of discovery[1][10].
- **Claimed result:** The paper reports 95% CL limits for selected mass-splitting scenarios, including electroweakino masses up to 200 GeV and pure chargino masses up to 175 GeV; no combined-channel exclusion is obtained for direct stau production[1].
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; ATLAS LHC collision data recorded in 2015–2018 and 2022–2023[1].
- **Caveat:** The deviations are local (2.1–2.4σ) and the overall results are described as generally consistent with the Standard Model; they are not a discovery claim[1].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 2. [Measurements of the inclusive Branching fractions of the D_s^+ → η(η′) X decays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36028)
- **Abstract:** BESIII measures inclusive D_s^+ branching fractions to ηX and η′X as 30.72% and 12.30%, respectively, with statistical and systematic uncertainties, improving on earlier precision[2].
- **Authors:** BESIII Collaboration
- **arXiv:** `2609.36028v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:02:20 UTC; categories: `hep-ex`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is a direct charm-physics measurement using 7.33 fb⁻¹ of e⁺e⁻ data and substantially reduces the uncertainty of previous inclusive measurements; it also addresses decay modes relevant to normalizing other charm and beauty studies[2][9].
- **Claimed result:** From BESIII data at √s = 4.128–4.226 GeV, the collaboration reports B(D_s^+→ηX)=(30.72±0.90±0.46)% and B(D_s^+→η′X)=(12.30±0.94±0.37)%, with statistical then systematic uncertainties[2].
- **Evidence type:** Experimental; BESIII e⁺e⁻ collision sample, analyzed with single- and double-tag reconstruction[9].
- **Caveat:** Results are consistent with earlier CLEO measurements; the reported errors remain nonzero, and the inclusive determination depends on tag reconstruction and associated systematic treatment[2][9].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 3. [Constraining dark matter using 20-year INTEGRAL/IBIS observations II: Sub-GeV dark matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35968)
- **Abstract:** The authors combine spatial profiles and hard-X-ray spectra from 20 years of INTEGRAL/IBIS observations to set sub-GeV dark-matter annihilation and decay limits, reporting improvements of up to two orders of magnitude over prior X-ray bounds across much of the range[3].
- **Authors:** Jordan Koechler, Pedro De la Torre Luque
- **arXiv:** `2609.35968v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:00:01 UTC; categories: `hep-ph`, `astro-ph.CO`, `astro-ph.HE`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The work combines a long-baseline astrophysical dataset with particle-physics interpretations across multiple physics archives; the Semantic Scholar spot-check found zero citations at retrieval, so the placement reflects scope and claimed constraint-setting rather than citation momentum[3].
- **Claimed result:** For e⁺e⁻, μ⁺μ⁻ and π⁺π⁻ channels from 1 MeV to 5 GeV, the authors derive 95% upper limits on annihilation and decay, and translate them to dark-photon and scalar-portal parameters[3].
- **Evidence type:** Observational analysis; INTEGRAL/IBIS diffuse hard-X-ray spectra and Galactic longitudinal profiles from observations spanning 2003–2024[11].
- **Caveat:** The inference depends on astrophysical background and propagation models, including cosmic-ray-electron inverse-Compton emission and unresolved accreting white dwarfs; the paper itself identifies these backgrounds as uncertain[11].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Astrophysics; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 4. [One-dimensional quantum Gibbs states in constant circuit depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35973)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove an exact block-product mixture decomposition for 1D spin-chain Gibbs states at constant temperature, implying constant-depth preparation and strict bounds on several forms of thermal entanglement[4].
- **Authors:** Saúl Pilatowsky-Cameo, Georgios Styliaris, Ainesh Bakshi, Daniel Malz
- **arXiv:** `2609.35973v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:00:02 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`, `cond-mat.stat-mech`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A rigorous result connects quantum statistical mechanics, circuit complexity, and thermalization; the paper is cross-listed in quantum physics, condensed matter, and mathematical physics. The Semantic Scholar spot-check returned zero citations at retrieval, as expected for a very recent preprint[4].
- **Claimed result:** For any spin chain at constant temperature, the Gibbs state admits an exact bounded-block product-state mixture; the authors also give an analogous constant-depth decomposition for local 1D fermionic Hamiltonians[4].
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical; mathematical proof, with implications for 1D many-body state preparation and dynamics[4].
- **Caveat:** The stated results concern one-dimensional local systems at constant temperature; they do not establish the same constant-depth structure for higher-dimensional systems or arbitrary low-temperature regimes[4].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Quantum information; Condensed matter & materials

### 5. [Deciding the Attainability of the Multiparameter Quantum Fisher Information is NP-Hard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35975)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove that deciding whether individual-copy measurements can attain the multiparameter quantum Fisher-information bound is NP-hard, even for a restricted class of real, constant-rank models[5].
- **Authors:** Lorcan O. Conlon, Laura Shou, V Vijendran, Kishor Bharti, Aritra Das, et al. (10 authors total)
- **arXiv:** `2609.35975v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:00:02 UTC; categories: `quant-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** It addresses a recognized quantum-metrology question with a formal complexity result, reframing why a general exact criterion may be difficult; no citation-based momentum was available for this new submission[5].
- **Claimed result:** The reduction from bipartite-state separability establishes NP-hardness for exact attainability decisions in the restricted model class; unless P=NP, no efficiently computable necessary-and-sufficient general criterion exists for the stated problem[5].
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical; computational-complexity proof[5].
- **Caveat:** The hardness proof applies to the specified real, constant-rank setting and individual-copy measurements; it does not rule out useful efficient criteria for narrower physically structured families[5].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Quantum information

### 6. [Advancing Fuzzy-Sphere CFTs: SO(3)-Rotation-Resolving Exact Diagonalization, 3D Ising Heavy Primaries, and Quasi-Hole-Space Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35960)
- **Abstract:** The authors use rotational-symmetry-resolved exact diagonalization to reduce fuzzy-sphere computation costs by factors of several hundred, identify roughly 100 primaries in the 3D Ising CFT, and propose a reduced quasi-hole basis for studying CFTs linked to fractional quantum Hall states[6].
- **Authors:** Zheng Zhou, Yin-Chen He
- **arXiv:** `2609.35960v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:00:00 UTC; categories: `cond-mat.str-el`, `cond-mat.mes-hall`, `cond-mat.stat-mech`, `hep-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The paper bridges condensed matter and high-energy theory, with a concrete algorithmic gain and a spectrum result extending to scaling dimension Δ≈16.5[6].
- **Claimed result:** The authors report resolving a prior tension over a parity-odd scalar primary and identifying heavy scalar primaries; their reduced quasi-hole-space projection is proposed as a strategy for CFTs intertwined with topological order[6].
- **Evidence type:** Computational/theoretical; symmetry-resolved exact diagonalization and finite-size quantum Hall model spaces[6].
- **Caveat:** The spectrum and operator identification come from numerical finite-size regularization, so their interpretation and extrapolation to continuum CFT data remain method-dependent[6].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Condensed matter & materials; Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics

### 7. [A parameter independent analysis of the QCD chiral phase transition and its universal critical behaviour](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36010)
- **Abstract:** The authors analyze scaling behavior in (2+1)-flavor lattice-QCD simulations to estimate chiral-transition parameters and quantify departures from expected universal scaling as the light-to-strange quark-mass ratio changes[7].
- **Authors:** Jishnu Goswami, Frithjof Karsch, Sabarnya Mitra, Christian Schmidt
- **arXiv:** `2609.36010v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:00:27 UTC; categories: `hep-lat`, `hep-ph`, `hep-th`, `nucl-ex`, `nucl-th`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** A multi-archive lattice-QCD analysis connects finite-volume behavior, chiral symmetry breaking, and universal critical behavior; the authors describe it as proceedings prepared for a 2027 symposium, marking it as preliminary[7].
- **Claimed result:** Using highly improved staggered fermions with temporal extent Nτ=8, the authors estimate transition temperature and critical parameters directly from simulations and quantify scaling deviations[7].
- **Evidence type:** Computational; (2+1)-flavor lattice-QCD simulations over multiple volumes and light-quark masses[7].
- **Caveat:** The abstract reports Nτ=8 simulations and a proceedings contribution; continuum-extrapolation and finite-lattice effects remain important before treating the parameter estimates as final[7].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Particle, nuclear & high-energy physics; Computational & data methods

### 8. [Low Frequency Gravitational Wave Detection with Gravito-Magnetism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35962)
- **Abstract:** The authors propose using the gravito-magnetic component of low-frequency gravitational waves, measured through test-body Lorentz acceleration in a three-satellite constellation, to distinguish the signal from asteroid-belt gravity-gradient noise[8].
- **Authors:** Reza Ebadi, Surjeet Rajendran
- **arXiv:** `2609.35962v1` (published 2026-09-28 18:00:01 UTC; categories: `gr-qc`, `astro-ph.CO`, `astro-ph.IM`, `hep-ph`, `hep-th`, `quant-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** This is a cross-archive detector concept that links relativistic gravity, satellite geodesy, and atom-interferometer inertial references; it is a proposal rather than a reported detection[8].
- **Claimed result:** The authors argue that gravito-magnetic acceleration can break the degeneracy between low-frequency gravitational-wave signals (10 nHz–1 μHz) and asteroid-induced Newtonian gravity-gradient noise in a three-satellite setup[8].
- **Evidence type:** Forecast/proposal; theoretical signal-separation analysis and a proposed atom-interferometer proof-mass reference[8].
- **Caveat:** The proposal still requires a proof mass isolated from non-gravitational noise; no instrument demonstration or gravitational-wave detection is reported[8].
- **Announcement type:** New submission, announcement batch 2026-09-30.
- **Themes:** Cosmology & gravitation; Instrumentation & detectors

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **ATLAS:** The paper reports several 2.1–2.4σ local deviations but overall Standard-Model consistency. Follow-up data and independent channels can test whether these fluctuations persist; they should not be treated as a signal claim now[1].
- **Sub-GeV dark matter:** The limits depend on Galactic cosmic-ray propagation and diffuse X-ray background modeling. Better constrained background components and cross-checks with other observations would test the robustness of the portal limits[11].
- **Charm decays:** BESIII improves inclusive ηX and η′X precision, while the measurements still have statistical and systematic errors. Larger or independent samples and continued reconciliation with exclusive-mode sums could tighten the decay accounting[2][9].
- **Quantum metrology:** The NP-hardness theorem motivates identifying structured model classes where useful efficient tests or approximations remain possible, without claiming an efficient exact general criterion[5].
- **Lattice and detector follow-up:** The QCD scaling estimates need control of finite-lattice effects and continuum behavior; the gravitational-wave concept needs quantitative proof-mass noise budgets and a feasible atom-interferometer implementation[7][8].
