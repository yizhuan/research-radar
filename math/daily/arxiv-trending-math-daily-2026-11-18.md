### 2. [Cutoff on abelian groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23180)
- **Abstract:** The authors prove equivalence between cutoff and the product condition for abelian random walks under a logarithmic sparsity restriction, with a sharp mixing-window estimate and examples showing the restriction is optimal.
- **Authors:** Guy Blachar, Jonathan Hermon, Justin Salez
- **arXiv:** `2611.23180v1` (published 2026-11-17T18:26:30Z; categories: `math.PR`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The preprint reports a long-predicted equivalence, a quantitative mixing-window bound, and examples establishing the sparsity threshold’s optimality. Semantic Scholar’s current totals are 1 citation and 0 influential citations; no journal reference is listed.
- **Claimed result:** Theorem 1 gives a quantitative bound on the mixing-window width; the authors derive the cutoff/product-condition equivalence in the stated regime and construct abelian Cayley graphs showing sharpness of the sparsity restriction.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Sequences of continuous-time random walks on finite abelian groups; inverse transition probabilities grow at most logarithmically with group size. Reversibility is not required.
- **Caveat:** The equivalence requires the stated sparsity condition; the paper gives counterexamples near the boundary, so it is not a criterion for every abelian walk.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 18 November 2026 announcement batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics

## Trending Research Themes

- **Extremal combinatorics and geometry:** Several papers sharpen extremal statements or classify equality/feasibility structure: oriented-graph Sidorenko inequalities, simplex-avoiding density bounds, the strong chromatic index, and lattice-polytope volumes (Bitonti et al.; Czifra et al.; Wang; Liu et al.). Their tools differ—weakly norming graphs, fractional chromatic methods with rational certificates, local flag algebras, and lattice-width geometry—so this is a shared problem style, not one shared technique.
- **Sharpness and boundary structure in inequalities:** Cen’s weighted Calderón–Zygmund counterexample identifies a logarithmic loss, while Chen et al. establish sharp dependence for fractional capacitary Hardy inequalities. These are separate analytic frameworks, but both make optimality—not only an upper estimate—part of the result.
- **Broader mathematical settings:** The BMV paper carries a matrix-algebra conjecture into finite von Neumann algebras, while the Sidorenko paper moves from tournament comparisons to arbitrary oriented host graphs. These are two isolated examples of enlarging the setting, not evidence of a field-wide shift.
- **Isolated signal:** Blachar et al.’s abelian-walk cutoff theorem is a substantial probability result in today’s selection, but only one selected paper directly concerns mixing and cutoff.

## Open Problems and Research Directions

- **Open problem (reported by Liu et al.):** The Zaks–Perles–Wills volume conjecture remains unresolved in dimensions d ≥ 4; this paper proves the three-dimensional case. **Research direction (synthesis):** investigate whether the width-based decomposition and equality analysis used here can constrain higher-dimensional cases. [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22337)
- **Open problem (reported by Bitonti et al.):** The authors motivate further structural and classification questions for relative Sidorenko phenomena, especially beyond the characterized connected relatively Sidorenko class. **Research direction (synthesis):** seek criteria for relatively anti-Sidorenko orientations and test how weakly norming structure behaves under graph operations. [Bitonti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23222)
- **Research direction (synthesis):** For the two simplex-avoidance settings, test whether constructions can approach the new upper bounds and whether the exact rational certificates can be extended to related configurations or dimensions. The authors provide certificates/code, but the abstract does not claim the bounds are sharp. [Czifra et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23097)
- **Research direction (synthesis):** Explore whether cutoff criteria can be formulated for sparse abelian walks or non-abelian groups without contradicting the paper’s sharpness examples for its logarithmic sparsity restriction. [Blachar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23180)
- **Research direction (synthesis):** Identify alternative weight hypotheses or formulations that recover useful no-log Calderón–Zygmund bounds after the one-supremum counterexample, while respecting the paper’s optimal logarithmic lower bound. [Cen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22097)
