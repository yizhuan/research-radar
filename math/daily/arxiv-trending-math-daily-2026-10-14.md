### 1. [A proof of the rational exponents conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18713)
- **Abstract:** The authors present a proof of the Erdős–Simonovits conjecture that every rational exponent from 1 to 2 occurs as the extremal-number exponent of some single graph.
- **Authors:** David Conlon, Oliver Janzer, Tao Jiang, Jaehoon Kim, Joonkyung Lee, et al.
- **arXiv:** `2610.18713v1` (published 2026-10-13 16:56:19 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The claimed result covers every rational exponent in the conjecture, and the introduction distinguishes this single-forbidden-graph statement from prior finite-family versions. That breadth makes it a standout result in the batch; no independent attention metric is used.
- **Claimed result:** The authors say they explain and situate a proof claimed by Epoch AI: for each rational r in [1,2], there is a graph H with ex(n,H)=Theta(n^r). The paper's introduction places the argument within earlier work on rooted bipartite graphs and extremal constructions.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Extremal graph theory; the claim is existential for each rational exponent in the closed interval [1,2], with a potentially different graph H for each exponent.
- **Caveat:** This is a new preprint that explains a proof claimed by Epoch AI; the proof and its dependencies have not been independently assessed here or peer reviewed.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 October 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics

### 2. [Multidimensional Szemerédi Theorem for Pairwise Independent Jointly Intersective Polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18657)
- **Abstract:** The paper proves the Joint Intersectivity Conjecture for pairwise independent polynomial families, giving a result for arbitrary sequence length with several commuting measure-preserving transformations.
- **Authors:** Nikos Frantzikinakis, Davide Sclosa
- **arXiv:** `2610.18657v1` (published 2026-10-13 16:20:20 UTC; categories: `math.DS`, `math.CO`, `math.NT`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors call this the first general result for arbitrary-length sequences beyond the one-transformation setting; it also bridges dynamics, combinatorics, and number theory. The ranking reflects that concrete scope, not readership data.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove the conjecture for jointly intersective families that are pairwise independent, using a Nil-Bohr returns theorem and a nil-plus-null decomposition for polynomial multicorrelations.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Integral polynomials must be jointly intersective (their values are simultaneously divisible by every positive integer for some input) and pairwise independent; the associated transformations commute and preserve a probability measure. Pairwise independence is weaker than full linear independence.
- **Caveat:** The full conjecture remains open for general jointly intersective families; the paper's result does not cover every family with linear relations.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 October 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Probability & statistics

### 3. [Global existence of smooth solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations for initial data with infinite energy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18797)
- **Abstract:** The authors construct global Navier–Stokes solutions from arbitrary divergence-free distributional data on the d-dimensional torus, smooth for every positive time, and characterize data admitting weak solutions.
- **Authors:** Alexey Cheskidov, Hedong Hou
- **arXiv:** `2610.18797v1` (published 2026-10-13 17:41:14 UTC; categories: `math.AP`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The theorem's data class is exceptionally broad—every divergence-free distribution on the torus in dimension d≥2—and the authors also state sharp Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin ranges. This is a major scope claim, not independently verified popularity.
- **Claimed result:** The authors prove global existence with smoothness on (0,∞)×T^d for any divergence-free distributional initial data; for data in W^{-1,p}, 1<p<∞, they construct classical weak solutions and characterize the admissible data classes.
- **Assumptions and setting:** Incompressible Navier–Stokes on T^d for d≥2. The initial velocity is divergence-free and distributional; the constructed solution is continuous to the initial data in a negative Sobolev topology and smooth at positive times. Classical weak-solution status is asserted for W^{-1,p} data.
- **Caveat:** The paper is a preprint. Its formulation permits very rough data and only requires positive-time smoothness; it should not be conflated with uniqueness or the conventional finite-energy global-regularity problem. Proof validity has not been assessed here.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 October 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Analysis & PDE

### 4. [Superdiffusive Invariance Principle for the two-dimensional Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18847)
- **Abstract:** For two-dimensional ASEP at nonzero drift and uniaxial asymmetry, the author derives a sharp logarithmic diffusivity and proves convergence of the density-fluctuation field on anisotropic superdiffusive scales to a conservative stochastic heat equation with additive noise.
- **Authors:** Quentin Moulard
- **arXiv:** `2610.18847v1` (published 2026-10-13 17:57:59 UTC; categories: `math.PR`, `math-ph`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The abstract describes a first space-time scaling limit for a nonequilibrium interacting particle system at the critical dimension, including an exact diffusivity prefactor and a second-class-particle CLT. This is a specific, cross-field result; no citation momentum is inferred.
- **Claimed result:** The author proves a functional invariance principle with anisotropic scaling and a superdiffusive central limit theorem for the second-class particle, for any finite-range jump kernel with nonzero drift and uniaxial asymmetry.
- **Assumptions and setting:** ASEP in dimension two, initialized from a Bernoulli product measure with density in (0,1); the stated result assumes finite-range jumps, nonzero drift, and uniaxial asymmetry.
- **Caveat:** The theorem is for the stated critical-dimension model and scaling regime; it does not establish the same limit for arbitrary dimensions or arbitrary asymmetry. The 85-page proof has not been independently checked here.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 October 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Probability & statistics; Mathematical physics

### 5. [Szemerédi's theorem with random differences and arithmetic Kakeya](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18859)
- **Abstract:** The paper proves that a sufficiently dense subset of [n] contains a k-term arithmetic progression with a difference in a random set once that set's inclusion probability is at least C n^{-c/log log k}, for an absolute c>0.
- **Authors:** Gabriel Dahia
- **arXiv:** `2610.18859v1` (published 2026-10-13 17:59:26 UTC; categories: `math.CO`)
- **Evidence for ranking:** The authors say the threshold improves the previous n^{-2/k+o(1)} bound for sufficiently large k and connects a small-cover argument with arithmetic Kakeya. The explicit threshold improvement is the ranking signal.
- **Claimed result:** For every k≥3 and density δ>0, if p≥C(δ,k)n^{-c/log log k} for sufficiently large n, then with high probability every S⊂[n] of size at least δn contains a k-term progression whose common difference lies in a p-random subset of [n].
- **Assumptions and setting:** Fixed positive density δ, integer k≥3, sufficiently large n, and a Bernoulli p-random set of candidate differences; constants may depend on δ and k except for the absolute exponent constant c.
- **Caveat:** The exponent's constant is not made explicit in the abstract, and the theorem gives an improved sufficient threshold rather than a sharp threshold characterization.
- **Announcement type:** new submission, 14 October 2026 batch
- **Themes:** Combinatorics & discrete mathematics; Probability & statistics

A direct next question is whether the proof can extend to such families or what further hypotheses are necessary (2610.18657).
- **Research direction (informed synthesis):** Determine how close the random-difference threshold is to optimal, including sharper dependence on k and the role of arithmetic structure in the candidate-difference set (2610.18859). The paper's introduction records an existing conjectural much sparser threshold for fixed k.
- **Research direction (informed synthesis):** Test the ASEP scaling statement beyond uniaxial asymmetry and across other critical-dimension interacting particle models, while identifying which parts of the logarithmic correction and Gaussian limit are universal (2610.18847).
